# Pieces exist everywhere; fusion exists nowhere

Across five adjacent literatures — clarification/uncertainty dialogue, belief-state tracking, disfluency/repair linguistics, code-switching SLU, and noise-robust ASR benchmarking — nearly every sub-problem a "conversational integrity layer" would need to solve has already been solved once, somewhere, by someone. Confidence-threshold accept/confirm/clarify decisions, targeted reprise-style clarification, N-best/belief-state uncertainty representation, the reparandum–repair structural grammar of self-correction, schema-free dialogue state tracking, and graded acoustic-noise benchmarks are all mature, citable, decades-deep (in some cases) results. What does **not** exist, as far as this research could determine, is a single system or paper that fuses ASR-hypothesis uncertainty, semantic/referential ambiguity, disfluency-derived epistemic signal, and schema-free persistent state into one coherent, domain-agnostic runtime representation of "what the speaker currently means" — nor a benchmark that varies linguistic, conversational, acoustic, and contextual difficulty as orthogonal, combinable axes rather than one at a time. The genuinely open work is therefore integration and evaluation design, not invention of any single mechanism: Boli's most defensible contribution is the **abstraction and the composite benchmark**, not a new clarification policy, a new disfluency parser, or a new ASR-error corrector in isolation. The sections below work through all twelve requested angles — named sources and their core ideas, universal versus language-specific findings, correction/uncertainty preservation, ASR/noise robustness, reusable annotation schemes and datasets, a four-axis difficulty ladder, candidate abstractions, pre-architecture experiments, what is already commodity, and what residual gap survives honest scrutiny.

## 1. A map of the named literatures and what each one actually contributes

No single paper covers Boli's problem; instead five distinct research traditions each solved one facet, decades apart from each other. The table below anchors the most load-bearing sources against the core idea each contributes, and the narrative that follows explains how they connect.

| Tradition | Flagship source(s) | Core idea contributed |
|---|---|---|
| Confidence-threshold dialogue management | [On the use of confidence for statistical decision in dialogue strategies](https://aclanthology.org/W04-2321.pdf); [Bohus & Rudnicky, non-understanding errors](https://aclanthology.org/2005.sigdial-1.14.pdf) | Two thresholds (T1/T2) on a confidence score drive an accept/confirm/clarify-or-reject decision; thresholds must be tuned per-domain and per-state, not fixed globally |
| Clarification-request form | [Schlangen, Causes and Strategies for Requesting Clarification](https://aclanthology.org/W04-2325.pdf); [Purver thesis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2897205_The_Theory_and_Use_of_Clarification_Requests_in_Dialogue_Matthew_Purver_Dialogue) | Clarification causes live at multiple communication levels (acoustic, reference, intention); **88% of human clarification requests are "reprise questions"** that echo the ambiguous span, not generic repair prompts |
| Belief-state / POMDP dialogue management | [Young, Gašić, Thomson, Williams — POMDP review](http://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/~sjy/papers/ygtw13.pdf); [Williams, Poupart & Young, continuous observations](https://aclanthology.org/2005.sigdial-1.4/) | Never collapse to one hypothesis before the policy step — carry a probability distribution (or compressed/partitioned approximation) over dialogue states, because exact tracking is intractable at scale |
| LLM abstention & clarification behavior | [Know Your Limits survey](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.26.pdf); [Knowing but Not Showing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25284); [Madge, Purver & Poesio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10445) | LLMs can *recognize* ambiguity when explicitly probed but default to answering anyway rather than asking — recognition and clarification behavior are decoupled and must be separately engineered |
| Ambiguity taxonomy | [CLAMBER](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.578/); [Disambiguation survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12543v1) | Lexical / referential / scopal / contextual / "epistemic misalignment" / "aleatoric output" ambiguity categories, built for text; no category for ASR-hypothesis-level acoustic ambiguity exists yet |
| Dialogue state tracking architecture | [SOM-DST](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03906); [D3ST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08904); [FnCTOD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10466); [Beyond Ontology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22767) | State = persistent memory, updated turn-by-turn via an explicit "what kind of change is this" classification before "what is the new value"; the field has already moved from fixed ontology → schema-guided → fully ontology-free |
| Spoken/ASR-aware DST | [DSTC11 Track 3 overview](https://aclanthology.org/2023.dstc-1.10/); [OLISIA](https://aclanthology.org/2023.dstc-1.12/) | Never hand raw ASR transcript to a state tracker — insert an explicit ASR-error-correction + entity-normalization stage first; WER is a poor proxy for task-level (JGA) damage |
| Correction/retraction handling in DST | [Negative-feedback-utterance DST, KDD 2022](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3534678.3539385); [RSP-DST](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/6/1494); [Confidence Estimation for LLM-based DST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09629) | Mainstream DST still treats state as a single committed, non-revisable belief; contradiction-handling, calibration, and revisability are each addressed in isolation, never unified |
| Disfluency/repair structure | [Shriberg 1994 framework](https://www.sri.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/phonetic_consequences_of_speech_disfluency.pdf); [Levelt 1983](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_64752_4/component/file_64753/1983_levelt_monitoring.pdf) | Every self-interruption decomposes into reparandum → interruption point → interregnum → repair; Levelt's four repair types (error / appropriateness / different-information / same-information) distinguish *what kind* of correction occurred |
| Human recovery behavior | [Oviatt et al., CHAM model](https://www.isca-archive.org/icslp_1998/oviatt98b_icslp.html) | After a misrecognition, users hyperarticulate in a staged, targeted way (Stage I → Stage II) — and hyperarticulated speech is recognized *worse* by ASR trained on normal speech, producing a predictable "error spiral" |
| Code-switching ASR/SLU | [GLUECoS](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.329/); [MUCS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.00235); [Unified CS ASR+LID](https://aclanthology.org/2023.calcs-1.7.pdf) | The field has moved from LID-then-route cascades to unified end-to-end models because cascades compound latency and cut words mid-switch; even so, intrasentential code-switching is explicitly still unsolved |
| Indian-language disfluency/code-mixing annotation | [Kochar et al., WILDRE 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wildre-1.1/); [DISCO](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.855/) | First systematic disfluency-annotation guidelines for six Indian languages, explicitly including "code-mixed repairs" as a disfluency subtype; reports Kappa agreement of 92 (Hindi) / 89 (Bengali), suggesting the reparandum/repair distinction transfers |
| Graded acoustic-noise benchmarking | [CHiME-5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.10609); [Speech Robust Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07937) | CHiME-5 is real (not synthetic) multi-party domestic speech with documented 0–4-speaker overlap; SRB is a corpus-agnostic, severity-graded perturbation framework (114 perturbations, 6 categories) — the clearest existing template for a difficulty ladder |

Two things stand out from this map. First, every one of these literatures is individually mature — some (POMDP belief tracking, Levelt's repair model, Switchboard annotation) are 20–40 years old and textbook-settled; others (schema-free DST, LLM abstention, CS-ASR unification) are 2023–2026 and still actively moving, but moving within a well-defined problem frame. Second, the boundaries between these literatures are almost perfectly aligned with Boli's stated requirements (regional variation → code-switching tradition; self-correction/retraction → disfluency and DST-contradiction traditions; uncertainty/ambiguity → clarification and abstention traditions; ASR errors/noise → the ASR-robustness and speech-aware DST traditions) — which is itself informative: the problem has been decomposed this way by the field for a reason, and a unifying layer will have to either absorb four separately-optimized subsystems or demonstrate that a shared representation genuinely does better than the sum of parts.

## 2. What is universal across languages, and what is an English-telephone-corpus artifact

The clarification, belief-state, and disfluency traditions all distinguish a **structural layer** (which looks genuinely universal) from a **lexical/statistical layer** (which looks tied to the specific corpus it was measured on, usually English telephone speech). The reparandum–interruption-point–interregnum–repair skeleton was not even originally English: Levelt's foundational 1983 model was built from spontaneous **Dutch** speech, not English ([Levelt 1983](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_64752_4/component/file_64753/1983_levelt_monitoring.pdf)), and the same structural distinction has since been successfully re-applied to French, and — most relevantly for Boli — to six Indian languages by Kochar et al., who report **Kappa agreement of 92 for Hindi and 89 for Bengali** when annotators apply an adapted reparandum/repair scheme, direct evidence that the underlying cognitive-structural grammar of self-interruption transfers well beyond its original language ([Kochar et al., WILDRE 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wildre-1.1/)). The confidence-threshold accept/confirm/clarify decision structure shows the same pattern of durability: it was formalized for classic spoken dialogue systems in the mid-2000s and reappears essentially unchanged, just under new vocabulary, in the 2025 LLM-abstention literature's framing of "over-abstention reduces usefulness; under-abstention compromises reliability" ([Know Your Limits](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.26.pdf)). Purver's finding that 88% of human clarification requests are reprise-form, targeted echoes of the ambiguous span rather than generic "please repeat" requests, is treated across the field as the gold-standard *form* for a clarification question regardless of language or era ([Purver thesis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2897205_The_Theory_and_Use_of_Clarification_Requests_in_Dialogue_Matthew_Purver_Dialogue)).

By contrast, every *specific statistic* found in this research is explicitly tied to the register it was measured on and should not be assumed to transfer. Switchboard's finding that only 5.9% of tokens are disfluent and that repetitions account for more than half of all disfluency instances describes **English, two-stranger, assigned-topic, telephone conversation** — there is no cross-linguistic or cross-dialect replication of this specific distribution in the retrieved literature. The CHAM hyperarticulation model is robustly established for English voice interfaces in the 1990s–2000s, with one multilingual replication (Kraljevski et al., Interspeech 2017) confirming the phenomenon recurs across languages, but its full-text findings were not retrievable in this session, and **no study was found testing hyperarticulation in code-switched or regional-dialect speech directed at a voice agent** — whether a Bhojpuri speaker hyperarticulates toward their own dialect norm or toward a perceived "standard" Hindi register when correcting a misrecognition is an open empirical question. Similarly, the MultiWOZ 2.1 correction data shows that value-change (contradiction/retraction) turns make up only about 0.84% of all corrected ground-truth state transitions combined — a number specific to that benchmark's task-oriented-dialogue register, not a universal rate of how often real speakers correct themselves. The clearest Indian-language-specific finding in this research is that **dialect and code-switching are treated as conceptually distinct problems** in the literature even though both are theoretically "codes" a speaker switches between: Bhojpuri, Maithili, and Magahi get dedicated low-resource-language resource papers (NER datasets, similarity studies) rather than being folded into code-switching detection, and the official/administrative classification of these varieties as "dialects of Hindi" versus independent languages is itself contested and policy-driven, not a settled linguistic fact — a conversational-integrity layer should not hard-code a single language-versus-dialect taxonomy for Indian varieties, because the field it would be borrowing from does not have one either.

## 3. Preserving corrections, revisions, and uncertainty: four unmerged lineages, not one mechanism

The single most important structural finding of this research is that **nothing currently unifies confidence calibration, multi-hypothesis persistence, and explicit retraction handling into one mechanism** — four separate lineages each address one facet, and none of them talks to the others. The oldest lineage, pre-neural probabilistic belief tracking (e.g., the Neural Belief Tracker), explicitly maintained a full probability distribution per slot per turn rather than committing to a single value, and the POMDP tradition built an entire reinforcement-learning policy layer on top of that distribution precisely because carrying uncertainty forward measurably improves robustness to ASR/NLU errors ([Young et al., POMDP review](http://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/~sjy/papers/ygtw13.pdf)). That lineage fell out of favor as the field moved to MultiWOZ-scale generative and classification DST, where models like SOM-DST commit to a single best state per turn via a one-shot, non-reversible overwrite operation (CARRYOVER/DELETE/DONTCARE/UPDATE), and where discriminative multidomain models are documented to be "just over 55%" accurate while remaining fully confident in their wrong predictions ([SOM-DST](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03906); calibration-gap finding via [search synthesis citing calibration literature](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02586)). Three independent 2020s papers each patch one piece of this overconfidence problem without reconnecting to the others: an EMNLP 2020 paper proposes ensembling specifically to calibrate belief-state confidence ([Knowing What You Know](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.277.pdf)); a KDD 2022 paper observes that "most current research completely ignores user negative feedback utterances" — i.e., in-conversation corrections after a system error — and proposes a dedicated feedback-utterance schema to detect them ([negative-feedback DST, KDD 2022](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3534678.3539385)); and RSP-DST is framed explicitly around making the SOM-DST-style commit *revisable*, which by its own framing implies the baseline is not ([RSP-DST](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/6/1494)). A fourth, LLM-era lineage revives the confidence question yet again — evaluating softmax probabilities, raw token scores, verbalized confidence, and self-probing as four separate signal types for LLM-based DST, explicitly because "well-calibrated confidence scores are essential to handle uncertainties" ([Confidence Estimation for LLM-based DST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09629)) — without reference to the calibration-ensemble or negative-feedback work that precedes it by only two to four years.

On the linguistic side, Levelt's four-way repair typology gives exactly the vocabulary this fragmented engineering literature is missing: **error repair** (the final value genuinely corrects a mistake and should supersede the reparandum), **appropriateness repair** (both reparandum and repair may carry usable content — the speaker refined rather than reversed), and **different-information repair** (the reparandum is an abandoned intent, not a wrong value, and should not be silently merged into the repair). None of the disfluency-removal pipelines surveyed in this research use this distinction operationally; they treat disfluency uniformly as noise to strip before downstream NLU ever sees it, even though the psycholinguistic literature shows disfluency rate tracks a speaker's own metacognitive uncertainty — in one cited study, people recalling trivia answers "produced more disfluencies, longer latencies, more rising intonation, and more expressions of doubt when they reported having a low feeling of knowing about an answer" ([hedging/disfluency-uncertainty synthesis](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8579j2fh/qt8579j2fh.pdf)). The practical implication is that a reparandum is not merely something to delete: it is a timestamped record of what the speaker almost said, tagged with (at minimum) a Levelt repair-type label, and currently no reviewed system carries that record forward into downstream reasoning as structured metadata rather than discarding it at the disfluency-removal stage.

## 4. ASR errors and acoustic degradation: three convergent engineering lessons

Three separate challenge series converge on the same three lessons about handling noisy or erroneous ASR output, which is a strong signal they are genuinely settled rather than artifacts of one benchmark. First, **never hand raw ASR output to the downstream component** — DSTC11's winning speech-aware DST system (OLISIA) inserted an explicit ASR-error-correction stage (time-expression normalization, proper-noun/named-entity correction) between ASR and the state tracker, and reported that each correction step measurably recovered joint-goal accuracy even though the correction only ever touches a small fraction of the transcript's words ([OLISIA](https://aclanthology.org/2023.dstc-1.12.pdf)). Second, **word error rate is a poor proxy for task damage** — OLISIA's own ablation explicitly motivates measuring the downstream task metric (JGA) at every correction step rather than trusting WER improvements alone, because only a few words in a given utterance are actually state-relevant; this same lesson appears independently in the DSTC11 track's own evaluation-set controversy, where JGA degradation from ASR noise was reported as either "58.1%→47.2%" or a much starker "53.8%→26.1%" depending on whether the evaluation set allowed models to memorize slot-value strings from the original clean MultiWOZ test set — a methodological trap that inflates apparent ASR-robustness unless the eval set is explicitly constructed to prevent it ([DSTC11 overview and critique](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08704)). Third, **carrying richer-than-1-best ASR information through the pipeline helps** — modeling ASR ambiguity via word confusion networks rather than a flattened 1-best transcript improved DST accuracy from roughly 28.2% to 33.5% in one reported comparison, and the Dialog State Tracking Challenge series consistently found that the best trackers incorporate N-best scores or confusion-network features rather than discarding them after transcription ([word confusion networks for DST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.00768)).

On the acoustic side, the field has produced a genuinely reusable **severity ladder**, though no single existing benchmark spans both the acoustic axis and the multilingual/code-switching axis simultaneously. CHiME-4 is clean read speech with four overlaid environmental noise types (street, pedestrian area, cafe, bus); CHiME-5 is real, unscripted, multi-party domestic conversation with documented 0–4-speaker overlap percentages (22% noise-only, 51% single speaker, 20% two overlapping, 5% three, 2% four) and was re-released under CC BY-SA 4.0 in 2024, removing its prior LDC-license friction ([CHiME-5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.10609)); CHiME-6/7/8 add joint diarization+ASR evaluation (cpWER) and extend across home-dinner, lab-dinner (DiPCo), interview/telephone (Mixer 6), and office-meeting (NOTSOFAR-1) domains, functioning as a ready-made multi-domain "hard tier" battery ([CHiME-8 DASR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16447)). AMI contributes a freely licensed (CC BY 4.0), fully transcribed, 100-hour far-field meeting corpus with roughly 24.7% natural speaker overlap, complementary to CHiME's domestic-noise focus. Fisher and CALLHOME contribute a qualitatively different degradation type — narrowband telephony-channel artifacts (codec, handset, network noise) rather than room acoustics or overlap — though both remain LDC-gated. Most directly reusable as a *generic framework* rather than a fixed scenario is **Speech Robust Bench**, a corpus-agnostic benchmark of 114 perturbations across six severity-graded categories (clean, social gatherings, speech variation, environmental effects, digital augmentation, adversarial attacks) that can in principle be applied on top of any base corpus, including a Hindi or code-switched one ([Speech Robust Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07937)). No benchmark located in this research combines SRB-style graded acoustic perturbation with genuinely multilingual or code-switched content — CS-FLEURS's "noise handling" is denoising/artifact suppression during TTS synthesis, not a degradation axis, and the India-specific code-switching corpora (IITG-HingCoS, HiACC, VITB-HEBiC) claim "natural, noise-rich" recording conditions without publishing formal SNR or severity tiers.

## 5. Annotation schemes and datasets Boli could realistically reuse

Three annotation schemes are mature enough to adopt rather than reinvent. The **Shriberg/Switchboard reparandum–interruption-point–interregnum–repair bracket notation**, with its fluent/reparandum/repair token labels, is the de facto reference scheme for disfluency structure and has already been ported, with high inter-annotator agreement, to French and — critically for Boli — to six Indian languages by Kochar et al., who additionally define "code-mixed repairs" as a named disfluency subtype, directly relevant to a Hindi-English or other Indic code-switching target ([Kochar et al., WILDRE 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wildre-1.1/)). The **Schlangen clarification-cause taxonomy**, organized by which level of communication failed (acoustic/lexical non-understanding versus reference failure versus intention/acceptability failure) rather than by surface linguistic category, maps directly onto *what kind* of clarification a system should generate at each level and is a strong candidate for Boli's internal ambiguity-classification scheme ([Schlangen](https://aclanthology.org/W04-2325.pdf)). The **CLAMBER ambiguity taxonomy**, built from an input-process-output framework with eight fine-grained categories including "epistemic misalignment" and "aleatoric output," is the most LLM-era-relevant existing scheme and the closest thing to a standard, though it is text-only and entirely silent on acoustic/ASR-hypothesis-level ambiguity ([CLAMBER](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.578/)).

The dataset-reuse picture is mixed and licensing matters a great deal. Freely and openly reusable resources include AMI (CC BY 4.0, 100 hours, full manual transcripts, documented overlap), the post-2024 CC BY-SA 4.0 CHiME-5/6 release, DISCO (CC BY 4.0, disfluency-correction annotation spanning English, Hindi, German, and French — directly bridges the disfluency and Indian-language-emphasis strands), L3Cube-HingCorpus/HingLID (openly released on GitHub, 52.93M-sentence Hindi-English code-mixed text plus a production-quality LID model), and IndicVoices/IndicVoices-R (openly hosted on Hugging Face, 22 Indian languages, including code-switched and spontaneous speech). Gated or unconfirmed resources include Switchboard itself (LDC-licensed, LDC97S62/LDC99T42/LDC2009T26), CHiME-4 (LDC-gated via the WSJ0-dependent CHiME-3 package), Fisher and CALLHOME (LDC-licensed), and most of the Indian-language speech code-switching corpora found (HiACC, HingCos, MUCS challenge data, IITG-HingCoS, VITB-HEBiC) — their exact license terms were not confirmable from retrievable sources and should be checked directly before any reuse decision. The practical reuse strategy this research suggests is layering: use AMI or CHiME-5/6 for the acoustic-degradation axis, L3Cube/IndicVoices for the code-switching/multilingual axis, DISCO or a Kochar-et-al.-style re-annotation for the disfluency axis, and apply Speech Robust Bench-style perturbations on top of whichever base corpus is chosen — because no single existing dataset combines all three axes, and the earlier notes flag this combination as a genuine construction task rather than something off-the-shelf.

## 6. Grading difficulty: a four-axis ladder, not a single scale

Every benchmark surveyed in this research grades difficulty along exactly one axis — CHiME grades acoustic severity, MultiWOZ grades slot/domain complexity, CLAMBER grades ambiguity category, GLUECoS grades code-switching task type — and none grades more than one simultaneously. This is the clearest structural gap the research surfaced and the most direct input into an Easy/Medium/Hard/Very-Hard evaluation design: Boli's test conditions should be built as **combinations of four independently-variable axes** rather than a single composite difficulty score, because the underlying literatures treat them as genuinely separate failure modes with separate mitigations (disfluency removal does not fix ASR noise; ASR-error correction does not fix referential ambiguity; confidence calibration does not fix code-switching).

| Axis | What it varies | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|
| Linguistic difficulty | Clean monolingual → regional lexical variation → named-dialect speech (Bhojpuri/Maithili) → intrasentential code-switching → borrowed/naturalized vocabulary ambiguous with code-switching | Code-switching notes: dialect-ID as prosodic/acoustic problem, LID mid-90s accuracy ceiling at token level, borrowing-blindness in LLMs |
| Conversational difficulty | Single clean turn → single self-correction (appropriateness repair) → error/retraction repair → multi-turn contradiction requiring retraction of prior committed state → ambiguity requiring clarification | Disfluency notes: Levelt's four repair types; DST notes: negative-feedback utterances, RSP-DST's "revisable" framing |
| Acoustic difficulty | Clean studio/close-mic → environmental noise (CHiME-4-style) → far-field/natural overlap (AMI/CHiME-5-style) → telephony-channel artifacts (Fisher/CALLHOME-style) → combined/adversarial (SRB-style) | ASR-robustness notes: CHiME progression, SRB's six-category severity grading |
| Context/discourse difficulty | Reference resolvable within-utterance → reference requiring short dialogue history → reference requiring cross-turn/cross-domain carryover (MultiWOZ-style) → reference requiring resolution of a prior contradiction or hyperarticulated repeat attempt | DST notes: MultiWOZ 2.1's "delayed markups" and "forgotten values"; disfluency notes: CHAM Stage I/II hyperarticulation as a signal the *previous* turn failed |

Under this scheme, **Easy** is clean acoustic input, single standard-language utterance, no correction, no cross-turn reference — essentially what most current benchmarks (MultiWOZ 2.0/2.1 written condition, clean CHiME-4) already measure well. **Medium** introduces exactly one non-trivial axis at a time: either moderate ASR noise on an otherwise clean utterance, or a single appropriateness-repair disfluency in clean audio, or a single code-switched span in an otherwise monolingual sentence, or a single-turn clarification need — each of which has an existing benchmark or near-benchmark (DSTC11's ASR condition, GLUECoS's code-switch tasks, CLAMBER's ambiguity categories). **Hard** combines two axes — e.g., a retraction/contradiction turn (conversational) spoken under far-field overlap (acoustic), or a code-switched utterance (linguistic) containing a self-correction (conversational) — which is exactly the combination this research found no existing benchmark covers jointly. **Very Hard** stacks three or more axes simultaneously: a dialectal, code-switched, hyperarticulated correction attempt (linguistic + conversational + acoustic, with context difficulty compounding if it also requires resolving a multi-turn contradiction) — this is the realistic worst case for a deployed voice-AI system and, per this research, is not represented in any single existing dataset. Designing Boli's eval set this way — four independently dialed axes rather than a single "hardness" label — would itself be a direct, defensible contribution, since it is the evaluation-methodology gap that the schema-free DST literature explicitly flagged as unresolved even for text-only state tracking ("schema-freedom in the model is more mature than schema-freedom in evaluation methodology").

## 7. Candidate abstractions for a domain-agnostic conversational-integrity layer

Five abstractions recur across the literatures reviewed and look like the strongest building blocks, specifically because each is already domain-agnostic in its own tradition, not because any single paper proposes them together. The first is **N-best or distributional state, never single-hypothesis collapse before the policy step** — the POMDP tradition's central, hard-won conclusion, echoed by the modern finding that confidence calibration must be bolted onto DST precisely because single-best-state systems are overconfident on the ~45% of turns they get wrong. The second is a **separable "detect-need-to-act" classifier from "generate-the-response" module** — the clarification literature's two-subtask decomposition (need-prediction versus question-generation/selection) generalizes directly to "detect this turn needs a state update/retraction" versus "compute the update," which is structurally the same split SOM-DST already makes (state-operation prediction before slot-value generation). The third is a **typed update operation, not a bare overwrite** — SOM-DST's CARRYOVER/DELETE/DONTCARE/UPDATE vocabulary, enriched with Levelt's error/appropriateness/different-information repair types, gives a ready-made, cross-domain operation set for "what kind of change just happened to the speaker's meaning," distinct from "what the new value is." The fourth is **schema-as-description rather than schema-as-fixed-ontology** — D3ST's and FnCTOD's demonstrated pattern of supplying slot/intent meaning as natural-language description (or a callable function spec) rather than a hard-coded enum is the clearest existing precedent for a task-agnostic state representation that doesn't require retraining to add a new domain. The fifth is a **cost-sensitive, context-conditioned threshold rather than a global confidence cutoff** for the accept/confirm/clarify/abstain decision — both the classic SDS literature (thresholds must be tuned per-domain and per-dialogue-state) and the 2026 "Act or Clarify" framing (the decision should weigh the cost of acting wrongly against the cost of asking) converge on this, and it generalizes cleanly across ASR confidence, semantic ambiguity, and code-switching uncertainty as interchangeable inputs to the same threshold logic. Notably, no single reviewed paper combines all five into one architecture — each exists cleanly in its own tradition — which is exactly the synthesis work a conversational-integrity layer would need to do.

## 8. Experiments worth running before committing to an architecture

Given how unevenly validated the individual mechanisms are once combined, four experiments would meaningfully de-risk an architecture choice before investing in a full build. First, a **direct ablation of uncertainty representation richness** — build the same downstream task (e.g., a slot-filling or intent-classification equivalent) three ways: 1-best transcript only, N-best list with scores, full word-confusion-network — and measure task accuracy specifically on the contradiction/retraction subset of turns, not aggregate accuracy, since MultiWOZ-style benchmarks under-represent that subset and aggregate numbers would mask exactly the failure mode Boli cares about. Second, a **behavioral test of the recognition-versus-action gap** replicated in-house: following "Knowing but Not Showing" and the Madge et al. human/LLM asymmetry, measure whether Boli's own candidate LLM backbone, when it internally flags an utterance as ambiguous, actually produces a clarifying question or silently guesses — this is cheap to run early and would directly determine whether an explicit, separately-engineered policy layer is load-bearing or whether prompting suffices (the literature says it is load-bearing, but that should be verified against the specific backbone chosen, not assumed). Third, a **disfluency-signal-preservation A/B test** — compare downstream task performance and (if feasible) a proxy for "did the system use the right final value" between a pipeline that strips disfluencies before NLU and one that passes structured reparandum/repair metadata downstream — because this research found no existing study that measures whether preserving the reparandum as a feature actually helps a reasoning layer, only that stripping it is current standard practice; this is the experiment most directly validating or falsifying Boli's core novelty claim. Fourth, a **combined-axis stress test** using the four-axis difficulty ladder from Section 6 — construct even a small synthetic or lightly-collected test set that stacks two or three axes at once (e.g., code-switched + self-corrected, or dialectal + far-field noise) and measure where current off-the-shelf components (a commodity ASR, a commodity LLM dialogue manager) actually break, since every benchmark reviewed here varies one axis at a time and none predicts joint-axis degradation.

## 9. What is already solved and should not be presented as Boli novelty

Several pieces of what might intuitively feel like Boli's contribution are, on the evidence gathered, commodity or close to it, and claiming them as novel would not survive a literature-aware review. The basic **accept/confirm/clarify/reject decision structure driven by a confidence threshold** is 20-year-old, textbook spoken-dialogue-systems material, reappearing essentially unchanged in 2025 LLM-abstention framing. The **reprise-question form for clarification** (echo the ambiguous span rather than asking generically) is equally settled, backed by Purver's 88% corpus finding and treated as gold-standard form across both classic and LLM-era literature. The **reparandum/interruption-point/interregnum/repair structural grammar** of self-correction is textbook psycholinguistics dating to Levelt 1983, already successfully ported to Indian languages by Kochar et al. with high inter-annotator agreement — proposing this structure itself as new would be reinventing a well-cited wheel. **Whole-utterance, monolingual language identification** is explicitly described in the literature as a solved task approaching 100% accuracy with simple n-gram methods; it is only token-level, intrasentential identification that remains hard. The **principle that raw ASR output should never be handed directly to a downstream reasoning module** — insert explicit error-correction/normalization first — is the convergent, repeatedly-validated lesson of DSTC11's entire speech-aware track, not a novel insight. And the **general direction of travel away from fixed-ontology DST toward schema-as-description and ontology-free LLM-based state tracking** is an active but already well-populated 2022–2025 research program (SGD, D3ST, FnCTOD, Beyond Ontology) — a schema-flexible state representation is a sound design choice for Boli to adopt, but adopting it is following an established trend, not establishing one.

## 10. The gap that survives scrutiny

After deliberately trying to find a paper that already does what Boli proposes, none was found — but the honest gap is narrower than "nobody has solved voice-AI conversational integrity." It is specifically the **absence of a unified representation that carries ASR-hypothesis uncertainty, semantic/referential ambiguity, and disfluency-typed correction history through the same persistent state, updated by the same typed-operation mechanism, evaluated against a difficulty ladder that varies linguistic, conversational, acoustic, and contextual axes independently and in combination.** Each component piece is solved in its own silo — belief-state tracking solved uncertainty persistence for slot-filling SDS and then stalled as an isolated lineage; schema-free DST solved ontology flexibility but left contradiction/retraction a minority, under-modeled case; the disfluency literature established that self-correction carries real epistemic signal but no system operationalizes that signal downstream rather than discarding it at the cleanup stage; and no benchmark — Indian-language or otherwise — stacks acoustic degradation and code-switching and self-correction as combinable, independently-gradable difficulty axes. Three narrower, more falsifiable candidate contributions sit inside this larger gap and are worth distinguishing from the broader one: (a) an explicit typed-operation vocabulary (Levelt repair types merged with SOM-DST-style CARRYOVER/DELETE/UPDATE operations) applied to a schema-free, description-driven state representation — nothing found unifies these two; (b) architecturally preserving disfluency/reparandum content as structured metadata for downstream reasoning rather than stripping it, with an actual measured effect on a reasoning task — the clearest under-tested empirical claim in this research, flagged explicitly as absent from the literature in the disfluency notes; and (c) a composite, four-axis difficulty benchmark for Indian-language voice AI that combines acoustic severity (CHiME/SRB-style), code-switching (GLUECoS/IndicVoices-style), and conversational self-correction (Kochar-et-al.-style) in the same test items — which this research found no evidence of anywhere in the literature. Any of these three would be a legitimate, narrowly-scoped novelty claim; the broader claim of "a domain-agnostic conversational-integrity layer" is better understood as the engineering synthesis of (c)'s evaluation framework plus (a) and (b)'s mechanisms than as a wholly new scientific idea — which is not a weaker position, but it is a different one, and the literature does not support overstating it as more.

## Conclusion

The honest reading of this literature is that Boli's problem has been solved piecemeal, repeatedly, by people who were not talking to each other: dialogue-systems researchers solved the confidence-threshold and belief-state problem for slot-filling SDS in the 2000s and mostly stopped; psycholinguists solved the structural grammar of self-correction in the 1980s and it has quietly generalized across languages ever since; DST researchers solved schema flexibility in the 2020s but left contradiction-handling an afterthought; and ASR-robustness researchers built excellent graded acoustic benchmarks that never touch multilingual or code-switched content. What changes understanding here is less "there is a hidden gap nobody noticed" and more "the gap is specifically the connective tissue between well-mapped islands" — which means the highest-leverage next step is not a new clarification algorithm or a new disfluency parser, but building the typed-operation, schema-free state representation that can hold all four signal types at once, and the four-axis composite benchmark that can actually tell whether doing so helped. Both are tractable, scoped engineering projects grounded in named prior art rather than open scientific questions, and both should be judged against the "already solved" list in Section 9 before any claim of novelty is made public.
