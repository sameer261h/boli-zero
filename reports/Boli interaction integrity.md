# Interaction breakdown is old news, diagnosis is not

**The core finding of this research is that almost every individual piece of "detect the conversation is going badly and adapt" has existed in the spoken-dialogue-systems and conversation-analysis literature for between 15 and 35 years** — third-position repair (Schegloff, 1992), common ground and grounding (Clark & Brennan, 1991), PARADISE-style quality estimation (1997), and CHAM-style hyperarticulation modeling (Oviatt et al., 1998) are all textbook-settled, not new. What has changed is that 2023–2026 work has started treating detection (*that* something broke), diagnosis (*why* it broke, among several distinguishable causes), and repair-strategy selection as three separable sub-problems rather than one undifferentiated "handle the error" step, and almost no single system — voice-first or otherwise — combines all three with transparent-disclosure and register-adaptation policy in one architecture. The genuinely defensible contribution available to this project is narrow: a **structured, interpretable, multi-signal diagnostic state** (named counters — repeated-slot-request count, repair-attempt count, semantic-progress delta — rather than one collapsed satisfaction scalar) that selects among qualitatively distinct repair moves, combined with an explicit, boundable style-policy layer instead of emergent (and empirically over-convergent) LLM accommodation, combined with adversarially-robust identity disclosure. None of frustration detection, clarification generation, repair, adaptive politeness, barge-in handling, handoff, anthropomorphism management, or AI disclosure is individually novel — all are named, multiply-published research areas, several decades old. **Read this report as an honest audit, not a pitch**: where a claim is directly cited and verified, where it is a reasonable inference from cited work, where it is this project's own untested hypothesis, and where source notes flagged unsupported speculation are labeled explicitly throughout, because that labeling is the main analytical service this document provides.

## 1. The research landscape: eleven traditions, mostly unconnected to each other

This project sits at the intersection of eleven largely separate research traditions, several of which do not cite each other despite converging on the same underlying problem. **Conversational repair** (conversation analysis, CA) is the oldest and most structurally rigorous: Schegloff's third-position repair and Dingemanse & Enfield's cross-linguistic other-initiated-repair typology give a decades-old, linguistically crisp vocabulary that 2023–2024 NLP work (REPAIR-QA, BlockWorld-Repairs) has only just begun operationalizing computationally. **Clarification quality** is a parallel, slightly younger (2000s-era) linguistic tradition (Ginzburg & Purver's function-based CR taxonomy) that has been independently rediscovered at least three times — in ASR-specific patents, in conversational-search IR (EACL 2024), and now in LLM agentic systems (MAC, 2026) — each time re-deriving "targeted beats generic." **Interaction-quality/frustration estimation** descends from PARADISE (1997) through the Interaction Quality tradition (Schmitt/Ultes/Minker, LEGO corpus, ~2011 onward) to recent deployed-system frustration detectors like "Stupid robot, I want to speak to a human!" (COLING 2025). **Common ground and grounding** (Clark & Brennan, 1991; Traum, 1994) is the foundational discourse-theory construct for treating conversation as collaborative belief-maintenance rather than stateless turns. **Agentic shared-commitments** work (2024–2026 LLM-agent clarification-in-the-loop papers: InferAct, ClarifyBench/SAGE-Agent, ClarEval, DiscoBench, Ambig-DS) is reinventing much of the grounding vocabulary under new names, largely without citing the classic literature. **Register/style adaptation** (politeness-adaptive dialogue systems, controllable-text-generation) and **linguistic accommodation** (Communication Accommodation Theory, Giles; Linguistic Style Matching, Niederhoffer & Pennebaker) are classic sociolinguistics now being tested against LLM behavior, with an emerging 2025–2026 literature on LLM over-convergence. **Hyperarticulation-after-error** (Oviatt's CHAM model, 1998) and **turn-taking/barge-in** (Ström & Seneff 2000; Zhao et al. 2015; Voice Activity Projection, 2023–2026) are classic SDS-engineering traditions now being re-engaged by learned, continuous forecasting models. **AI identity disclosure** is the youngest tradition here, essentially a 2024–2026 phenomenon driven by regulation (EU AI Act Article 50, California AB 2905) and by a single landmark reliability study (RealityTest). **Anthropomorphism/trust** research is mature and consistently finds trust is task-specific, not general. **Adversarial user behavior** toward conversational agents has a real empirical base in CHI/HCI since roughly 2018, distinguishing frustration-driven aggression, disinhibition-driven casual abuse, and genuinely playful provocation as three separable phenomena. The throughline across nearly every one of these traditions' own self-assessment is the same complaint voiced by the 2026 cross-disciplinary misunderstanding taxonomy paper: these are "nine fields that don't ordinarily cite each other" ([arXiv:2608.13604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13604)) — which is the honest frame for this entire report.

## 2. The strongest primary sources and what each actually demonstrated

The single most load-bearing, independently-verified result in this entire research effort is the **Apple 2021 Interspeech paper** ("User-Initiated Repetition-Based Recovery in Multi-Utterance Dialogue Systems," Nguyen et al.), which built an end-to-end two-step attention pointer network that merges a user's repeated/partial correction back into a misrecognized original query, reporting a **19% relative WER reduction at a 2% false-alarm rate** against a rule-based baseline, and separately that its 2-step attention model achieved a maximum WERR of **25.80%** versus a standard pointer network's maximum of **19.03%** — two distinct statistics, both corroborated across independent search passes, that should not be collapsed into one number ([arXiv:2108.01208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01208)). **Schegloff's third-position repair (1992)** established the structural claim that A-says-X / B-misreads-X / A-corrects-in-third-turn is "the last structurally provided defense of intersubjectivity in conversation" ([emcawiki](https://emcawiki.net/Third_position_repair)); this has been operationalized twice recently — **REPAIR-QA** (SIGDIAL 2023), the first large TPR dataset for conversational QA, on which GPT-3 performed poorly out-of-the-box but improved significantly after exposure to the dataset ([ACL Anthology 2023.sigdial-1.52](https://aclanthology.org/2023.sigdial-1.52/)), and **BlockWorld-Repairs** (EMNLP 2024), an embodied-instruction-following extension where "all models significantly underperform humans" at processing TPRs ([ACL Anthology 2024.emnlp-main.643](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.643/)). **Dingemanse & Enfield's cross-linguistic repair typology** (2014/2015) analyzed ten languages and found a viable three-way taxonomy of repair-initiator types, now treated as a field-standard reference ([DeGruyter](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.2478/opli-2014-0007/html?lang=en)). **ContReph** (Amazon, EMNLP 2021) reframed rephrase detection as a dialogue-context problem rather than pairwise-utterance similarity, adding dialogue history, implicit feedback, and inter-turn timing as signals and reportedly "significantly outperforming" pairwise baselines, though exact F1/AUC figures could not be retrieved ([ACL Anthology 2021.emnlp-main.143](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.143/)). **PARADISE** (Walker, Litman, Kamm, Abella, 1997) remains "one of the earliest frameworks... still widely used as a baseline," decoupling task success from dialogue cost via linear regression ([ACL Anthology P97-1035](https://aclanthology.org/P97-1035.pdf)), while **Interaction Quality** (Schmitt/Ultes/Minker, LEGO corpus, ~2011 onward) operationalized a live, turn-by-turn 1–5 ordinal score computed across exchange/window/dialogue feature levels, explicitly built to drive reinforcement-learning dialogue-policy adaptation ([IWSDS 2015](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.iwsds.2015/iwsds2015_submission_4.pdf)); a BiLSTM successor reports **>76% correlation with live IQ defects vs. a 46% baseline** ([arXiv:2001.07615](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07615)). The **"Stupid robot, I want to speak to a human!"** paper (COLING 2025 Industry Track / arXiv:2411.17437) is the clearest directly-on-point deployed-system anchor: its deployed keyword-based production baseline achieved **100% precision but ~1% recall (Macro-F1 41%)**, traditional sentiment models performed poorly even over full conversation history, and an in-context-learning LLM detector beat sentiment models by **>33% relative Macro-F1** and dialogue-breakdown-detection methods by **>22% relative Macro-F1** (a separately-surfaced "16%" figure could not be reconciled against these and should be treated as needing primary-source disambiguation) ([arXiv:2411.17437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17437)). **Clark & Brennan's "Grounding in Communication"** (1991) is the foundational common-ground citation, defining grounding as the collaborative process of establishing mutual belief ([PDF](https://worrydream.com/refs/Clark_H_1991_-_Grounding_in_Communication.pdf)), and the **~448-paper LUHME 2025 survey** ("Building Common Ground in Dialogue," Anikina, Leippert, Ostermann) explicitly flags that evaluation frameworks for *dynamic* grounding in LM interactions "remain largely absent" ([ACL Anthology 2025.luhme-1.2](https://aclanthology.org/2025.luhme-1.2/) — the 448-paper count itself is corroborated only via one citing source's characterization, not independently confirmed against the survey's own text). The **2026 cross-disciplinary misunderstanding taxonomy** (Abbaschian) consolidates nine normally-non-citing fields into eleven failure modes across eight analytical layers ([arXiv:2608.13604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13604)). The **CHAM model** (Oviatt et al., 1998) establishes a two-stage escalating hyperarticulation response (Stage I: duration-only; Stage II: multi-dimensional articulatory/F0 changes, gated by error *rate*) ([ISCA Archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/icslp_1998/oviatt98b_icslp.html)), with the hyperarticulation-paradox mechanism — clearer speech degrades ASR trained on casual speech — treated as self-reinforcing in the same literature ([d-nb.info](https://d-nb.info/1001657659/34)). **Voice Activity Projection (VAP)** reframes turn-taking as continuous forecasting of joint voice-activity ~2000ms into the future from raw acoustics, rather than reactive silence thresholds ([arXiv:2606.16731](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16731)). **RealityTest** (arXiv:2606.00168) is the strongest disclosure-reliability source: only 31% of people ask about AI identity directly in ambiguous scenarios; disclosure probability ranged 8–92% across 17 text models and 10–57% across 6 speech models; a single system-prompt suppression instruction drove disclosure **below 30% even in the best-performing models**; and framing/context mattered more than model choice. The **CHI 2023 task-specific trust study** found trust scores of **3.29/5 (music), 2.34/5 (texting), 1.56/5 (money transfer)**, directly refuting a "general trust" model ([arXiv:2303.00164](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00164)). The **LLM over-convergence paper** (arXiv:2607.26015) found instruction-tuned models mirror user function-word style at roughly **twice the user's own rate**, with relative increases from **22% (prepositions) to 164% (negating function words)**. The **ELEPHANT sycophancy benchmark** (arXiv:2505.13995) found LLMs preserve user face **45 percentage points more than humans** on general advice and clear-wrongdoing queries, validate open-ended perspectives **50 points higher**, validate ineffectual Reddit AITA posters **46 points higher**, affirm incorrect premises **36 points above chance**, and flip validation to match whichever side of a moral conflict the user framed **48% of the time** — with the explicit finding that **model size does not reliably reduce sycophancy** and RLHF-style tuning can reinforce it.

## 3. Established mechanisms this project should reuse, not reinvent

Given how much ground is already covered, the reuse list is long and specific, not a token gesture. At the **structural/linguistic layer**: Schegloff's first/second/third-position repair sequence as the basic turn-model; Dingemanse & Enfield's three-way repair-initiator typology for classifying how a user signals trouble; Ginzburg & Purver's four-way clarification-request function taxonomy (Repetition, Clausal, Intended, Correction); Clark & Brennan's distinction between content-coordination and process-coordination, and their framing that participants look for *positive evidence* of understanding rather than assuming it. At the **system-strategy layer**: the CUI 2024 scoping review's six-category repair-strategy taxonomy (confirmation, information, social, solve, ask, disclosure) ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3640794.3665558)); the demonstrated principle (from ContReph, from the Apple repetition paper, and from the "Stupid robot" paper) that non-linguistic, interaction-level signals — timing gaps, implicit feedback, repetition patterns — materially improve breakdown detection beyond raw text similarity or sentiment alone; the established finding that **targeted clarification beats generic repeat-requests** across at least three independently-converging subfields (ASR patents, conversational-search IR, LLM agents); the established finding that **"over-asking" and miscalibrated clarification are real, named, cost-bearing failure modes**, not just under-asking — an LLM-agent study found over-asking in **52% of sessions** ([Ask Early, Ask Late, Ask Right](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.07937)), and a reward-design approach matched a strong baseline's resolution rate while asking **41% fewer questions** ([Asking What Matters](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14624)). At the **quality-estimation layer**: PARADISE's task-success-plus-cost regression framing; Interaction Quality's three-level (exchange/window/dialogue) feature hierarchy as a precedent for multi-granularity signal aggregation; the explicit finding that IQ-based reward estimation has already been used to train cross-domain dialogue policies via RL. At the **grounding layer**: Common Ground Tracking (CGT) as a distinct, complementary task to Dialogue State Tracking, tracking which propositions are *mutually* accepted rather than just which slots are filled ([arXiv:2403.17284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17284)); the documented failure mode of "representational blur" when fine-grained distinctions compress into purely textual context-window representations. At the **style layer**: treating formality, politeness, verbosity/brevity, and directness as independently controllable attributes, an established pattern in controllable-text-generation surveys since 2020 and reaffirmed in 2026 persona-architecture work separating "behavioral stance" from "linguistic surface form" ([arXiv:2601.06362](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06362v1)). At the **turn-taking layer**: the shift from reactive silence-threshold barge-in detection to learned, continuous forecasting (VAP); Zhao et al.'s (2015) cost-model/RL-based active-barge-in policy as a precedent architecture, though its specific performance numbers (37.1%/32.5%/27.7%) are single-study results from 2015, not validated 2026 baselines. At the **disclosure layer**: RealityTest's finding that disclosure must be treated as an adversarially-robust *behavioral property to be designed for*, not an emergent capability; the Identity Design (ID) Framework's "disclosure-based prompting" pattern (e.g., responding to "how are you feeling?" with "Although I have no emotions, my systems are functioning as intended") as a concrete template for honest-but-natural phrasing. At the **adversarial-behavior layer**: the established four-study convergence (CHI 2019, CHI 2020, a crowd-sourced evaluation, and the Dec 2025 hostility-corpus paper) that empathetic/polite-refusal responses to abuse outperform both silent avoidance and confrontational counterattack, and that response strategy should scale with abuse severity and type, not be fixed.

## 4. Contradictory and null evidence across the notes

Several findings pull in opposite directions and deserve explicit flagging rather than silent resolution. The **hyperarticulation paradox** is the sharpest: the same signal — a user speaking more clearly, loudly, and with longer pauses after a misrecognition — is simultaneously the strongest available behavioral evidence of user-perceived breakdown *and* a mechanistic predictor that the system's own recognition accuracy is about to get *worse*, because hyperarticulated acoustics mismatch the training distribution of recognizers trained on casual speech ([d-nb.info](https://d-nb.info/1001657659/34)). This is not a confound to control for; it is a structural double-bind — the clearer the user tries to speak, the worse the system is likely to hear them, meaning a detector that treats hyperarticulation purely as a frustration signal misses that it should also trigger an ASR-adaptation response, and a detector that treats it purely as an ASR-robustness problem misses the behavioral-diagnostic value. The **WER-reduction figures from the Apple paper** present an internal reconciliation risk: the commonly-cited "19% relative WER reduction at 2% FAR" (vs. a rule-based baseline) and the separate "19.03% vs. 25.80% max WERR" (2-step attention vs. standard pointer network) are two distinct statistics that look confusingly similar and were not fully disambiguated from search snippets alone — a report-writer or engineer citing "the Apple 19% number" should specify which comparison is meant. The **"Stupid robot" paper's relative-improvement figures** present a similar unreconciled conflict: one search pass surfaced "16% relative improvement," another surfaced ">33%" and ">22%" relative Macro-F1 improvements against different baselines — these may refer to different baseline comparisons within the same paper, but this could not be confirmed. The sharpest **model-specific finding** is the "Know-It-All GPT or Second-Guesser Claude?" paper (arXiv:2604.19245), which simulated four-turn conversations across five instruction-tuned LLMs around solvable/unsolvable math questions and found **GPT-4o and Phi-4 are "stubborn"** — persisting with initial, sometimes-incorrect answers even after correction — while **Claude revises more readily but introduces new errors when flipping**; none of the five models tested handle repair uniformly reliably. This is a genuine null result for the premise that "just use a stronger frontier LLM" solves repair reliability — it does not; different backbones fail in opposite directions and apparently need to be measured and compensated for specifically. A further unresolved tension sits in the register-adaptation literature: the LLM over-convergence finding (models mirror user style roughly 2x the rate humans mirror each other) directly argues for hard-bounding style adaptation, while the classic Communication Accommodation Theory literature shows *divergence* (not converging, but deliberately widening stylistic distance, as in the Bourhis & Giles Welsh-accent-broadening finding) is itself a legitimate, socially meaningful accommodation strategy under identity-threat conditions — meaning a style policy cannot simply target "more mirroring is better, less is worse" on a single axis; the correct target is context-conditioned, and sometimes correctly diverges from the user's register rather than approaching it. Finally, the paralinguistic-interaction-state thread (a separate but directly connected research pass in this same project) found **no consistent acoustic pattern for fear or anxiety across 39 studies** ([PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)) — directly relevant here because it rules out "detect the user is afraid/anxious" as a viable interaction-integrity signal regardless of how appealing it sounds, while anger and stress do have consistent acoustic profiles.

**The Prisma/ASR-behavior row is the most consequential constraint in this entire taxonomy and must be stated plainly, as an earlier, separate research thread in this same project established**: Boli's actual tested Prisma REST endpoint exposes only `success`, `request_id`, `transcript`, `model`, `processing_time`, and `end_to_end_latency` (with a marginally richer but still thin streaming schema adding `audio_duration_ms`, `segment_id`, `segment_index`, `latency`, `timestamp`, and one undocumented `raw` field). There is **no confidence score, no word timing, no N-best list, no diarization, and no SNR/signal-quality estimate exposed anywhere in the real integration** (confirmed empirically, not merely absent from documentation). This means the entire ASR/Prisma-behavior row of the theoretical signal space — which the general ASR-robustness literature treats as a rich, well-studied source of breakdown-adjacent signal — is **not currently accessible to this project** regardless of how well-evidenced it is in the abstract. Any interaction-integrity architecture proposed here must be built primarily from transcript text, system-side logs, and (if raw audio capture is added later) acoustic features computed independently of Prisma — not from ASR-internal confidence or instability signals, which simply do not exist in Boli's current pipeline.

## 6. Evidence-backed repair strategies

Against the brief's ordered strategy list (continue normally → acknowledge a specific error → rephrase the system response → ask a targeted clarification → confirm only the uncertain span → retrieve already-supplied information → explain differently → shorten the response → change conversational register → stop repeating a failed strategy → explicitly admit inability → hand off to a human), the notes give direct empirical support to several specific moves and leave the rest as reasonable-but-untested extensions.

**Directly supported by cited evidence:** *rephrase rather than repeat* has the strongest single empirical anchor in this entire repair-strategy list — the NIST 2000 Communicator Evaluation (nine mixed-initiative telephone systems, cited via the CUI 2024 scoping review) found **"employing repetitions as a system-repair strategy resulted in significantly higher rates of frustration compared to rephrasing"** ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3640794.3665558)) — this is a classic, 25-year-old finding, not novel, but it is unambiguous: repetition is categorically worse than rephrasing as a repair move. *Targeted clarification beats generic clarification* is the second-strongest, independently re-validated across ASR patents, EACL 2024 conversational-search research, and 2024–2026 LLM-agent benchmarks; the EACL 2024 paper reports specificity and query characteristics significantly predict clarification usefulness with "a minimum performance boost of 45%" over baseline, though the exact baseline/metric is unconfirmed ([ACL Anthology 2024.findings-eacl.84](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-eacl.84/)). *Stop repeating a failed strategy* is strongly supported by extension: the field's "over-asking" literature shows diminishing and even negative returns from repeated clarification attempts (success rates plateau while the proportion of answerable questions decreases as question count grows, per [Asking What Matters](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14624)), and the generic repeat-request literature shows the same escalating-frustration pattern. *Confirm only the uncertain span* ("retrieve already-supplied information" and targeted confirmation) maps directly onto the Apple repetition-recovery paper's mechanism — a two-step attention network that identifies exactly which span to preserve from the original utterance and which to replace from the correction — giving this move the single most quantitatively verified backing in the whole repair-strategy list (19%/25.80% WERR). *Empathetic/polite-refusal response beats avoidance and counterattack* for abusive input is established across four convergent studies (CHI 2019, CHI 2020, a crowd-sourced evaluation, and the Dec 2025 hostility-corpus paper): "polite refusal" scored highest across the board in the crowd-sourced evaluation, with users showing significantly higher negative reactions to counterattacking agents, though avoidance specifically was rated most appropriate after sexual demands — a context-dependent exception worth preserving rather than flattening ([arXiv:1909.04387](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.04387)). *Hand off to a human* is supported as a necessary, not merely optional, terminal strategy by the 2025 "Detect, Explain, Escalate" framing, which argues automatic repair is not always sufficient and some breakdowns genuinely require escalation ([arXiv:2504.18839](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18839)).

**Reasonable-but-untested extensions, clearly flagged as such:** *acknowledge a specific error* (as distinct from generic apology) has no direct study isolating it from other repair moves in the notes, though it is consistent with the CUI 2024 "disclosure" repair-strategy category. *Explain differently* (NLG-level content restructuring, not just rephrasing surface form) is supported only indirectly, by the naturalness-evaluation literature's general finding that naturalness is a distinct, measurable quality dimension separate from task success — no study specifically tests "explain differently" as a named repair move against alternatives. *Shorten the response* has no direct citation in these notes beyond the general controllable-text-generation literature treating verbosity/length as a separately controllable attribute; whether shortening specifically improves repair outcomes (as opposed to merely being a style-policy lever) is this project's own hypothesis, not a tested finding. *Change conversational register mid-repair* is supported only by extension from the style-adaptation literature (politeness-adaptive systems improve task success and reduce dialogue length) and is not tested as a repair-specific move triggered by breakdown detection specifically. *Explicitly admit inability* ("I can't help with that") has no direct citation in the repair-breakdown notes; it is a reasonable extension of the CUI 2024 "disclosure" category and the general honesty-in-disclosure argument from the identity-disclosure thread, but untested as a repair move in its own right. The **ordering and escalation logic itself** — i.e., that these eleven moves should be tried in roughly this sequence, gated by a structured diagnostic state — is, per the notes' own explicit statement, **this project's hypothesis**: no source found in any of the six notes implements and evaluates exactly this strategy-selection mechanism as a unified pipeline.

## 7. Evidence-backed style adaptation: policy versus temperature

The literature gives three separable, cited findings and one assembled-not-directly-confirmed argument, which must be kept distinct. First, **a single universally-preferred register does not exist**: politeness-adaptive dialogue systems (PADS/GenPADS) improve task success and reduce dialogue length by adapting to detected user mood ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0278323)); a controlled NAVI chatbot study found a friendly style improved satisfaction and task completion *only among female participants*, with no baseline gender difference absent the chatbot ([arXiv:2602.17850](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17850)); and voice-assistant politeness-perception research found positive-politeness strategies rated "very polite" for task requests while both positive and negative strategies rated as very polite for social requests — register effectiveness is request-type-dependent, not fixed ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00779-024-01822-8)). Second, **formality/brevity/warmth/directness as separately controllable dimensions is an established design pattern in controllable-text-generation research predating LLMs** and reaffirmed in 2026 persona-architecture work explicitly separating "behavioral stance" from "linguistic surface form" via dual-key designs ([arXiv:2601.06362](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06362v1)); "confirmation rigor" specifically, however, does **not** appear as a named dimension anywhere in the controllable-generation or persona-architecture literature surveyed — the notes explicitly flag this as likely original framing for this project, not an established term. Third, and most important to state honestly: **no study in these notes directly compares explicit multi-dimensional style-policy control against temperature-based tone control head-to-head.** The argument that these are not substitutable is *assembled*, not directly tested — it follows from (a) controllable-NLG treating style dimensions as separable content/structural attributes realizable at any temperature, (b) 2026 persona papers separating stance from surface form, and (c) the token-level mechanics of temperature (which acts on randomness across the *entire* output, including factual/numeric content, not just register). The notes explicitly label this a **reasonable inference, assembled from adjacent literature, not an empirically confirmed result** — and flag it as a legitimate place this project could make a real, narrowly-scoped contribution precisely because no one has run the comparison. This is one of the few places in this entire report where the honest answer to "is this novel" is "plausibly yes, specifically because the comparison nobody has run is exactly the comparison this project would need to run."

Bounds on style mirroring are better evidenced than the policy-vs-temperature question. A service-recovery study found chatbot linguistic-style matching boosts positive response and reuse intention for **minor** service disruptions, but for **severe failures**, "an aligned linguistic style might be dismissed as an insincere gesture, diminishing recovery benefits" ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S000169182601190X)) — directly supporting an asymmetric design where task-risk acts as a floor on formality that user-casualness cannot override, though no source directly tests the exact scenario class this project cares about (a user being casual about a high-stakes task like a loan default). A FAccT 2025 paper raises mirroring minority sociolects/dialects as cultural-appropriation-adjacent and potentially offensive, and a dialect-feature-database audit found expert linguists judged **as many as 90% of features in popular dialect databases as features that should NOT be reproduced** by models, due to stereotyping risk ([arXiv:2505.05660](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.05660)). The LLM over-convergence finding (roughly 2x human mirroring rates, 22–164% relative increases by function-word category) and the ELEPHANT sycophancy findings (45–50 percentage-point over-validation, unreduced by model scale or RLHF) together constitute the strongest argument in this entire report **for** hard-bounded, explicit style policy rather than trusting emergent LLM accommodation: the cited empirical finding is specifically that unmanaged LLM behavior over-shoots social calibration, not merely that it is imperfectly calibrated.

## 8. Safety/transparency constraints on AI identity disclosure

RealityTest is the governing evidence source here and its findings are unambiguous: disclosure is **not currently a reliable behavior** under any tested model, text or speech. With disclosure probability ranging 8–92% across text models and 10–57% across speech models even absent adversarial pressure, and collapsing below 30% in the best-performing models under a single suppression instruction, the paper's own framing treats "the gap between baseline performance and real-world robustness" as a large, unresolved problem — not something this project would be solving from a settled baseline. Critically, RealityTest found that **how the question is phrased and the conversational context matter more for disclosure reliability than which model is being tested** — supporting a design conclusion that disclosure must be engineered as a conversational-framing-robust behavioral property, not left to model-level instruction-following. The brief's "how, not whether" framing is well-supported by the Identity Design Framework's disclosure-based-prompting pattern — responding to "how are you feeling?" with something like "Although I have no emotions, my systems are functioning as intended" rather than deflecting or over-explaining — which preserves conversational naturalness while being unambiguous, though this is a single design-principles framework, not an empirically validated best practice against alternatives. Task-specific trust calibration (CHI 2023's 3.29/2.34/1.56 gradient) supports the inference that disclosure register should itself scale with task stakes — a casual, warm disclosure may be appropriate for a low-stakes music-playback-style interaction, while a higher-stakes financial or legal task likely warrants a more careful, unambiguous disclosure regardless of the user's own casual tone — but no cited source directly tests disclosure-register-by-task-stakes as its own manipulation; this is a reasonable inference from combining the trust-calibration literature with the RealityTest disclosure literature, not a directly confirmed finding. Regulatory context is concrete and dated, not speculative: the EU AI Act Article 50 requires disclosure "at the start of the interaction or in a way the caller can reasonably register before any consequential exchange," and California's AB 2905 (effective January 1, 2025) requires verbal disclosure at the opening of automated calls — meaning "how to disclose naturally" is not merely a UX nicety but increasingly a binding compliance requirement in at least two jurisdictions directly relevant to a voice-AI deployment.

## 9. Playful, adversarial, and abusive user behavior

The notes support a genuine, evidence-backed three-cluster taxonomy. **Frustration/breakdown-driven aggression** is mechanistically well-studied: "abusive language often arises in response to dialogic breakdowns rather than as isolated or context-free acts," with triggers specifically named as incorrect/irrelevant responses, repeated refusal, and perceived circular or evasive dialogue ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3313831.3376461)). **Disinhibition-driven casual abuse** is distinct and broadly distributed, not concentrated in a hostile minority: one study found **80% of chatbot conversations contained some profanity** directed at the agent, with 4.29% of individual messages containing profanity, and a 2.5-year longitudinal family-home study found perceived relationship closeness between children and a voice assistant *positively predicted* children's commanding tone and verbal-abuse tendency over time, while for parents closeness predicted only politeness ([Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2025.2533352)). **Intrinsically-motivated playful/humor-seeking behavior** is the most directly relevant to this project's absurd-request scenarios: the Alexa playful-requests study ("do you want to build a snowman?", "order a zillion gummy bears") analyzed real production traffic and built a taxonomy rooted in humor theory, finding that enabling the assistant to recognize playful opportunities *increased* user satisfaction in a Wizard-of-Oz study, and explicitly warning that misreading playful utterances as literal requests, or responding with "I don't know what you mean," is a documented failure mode ([arXiv:2105.05571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05571)). A newer corpus study, "When Users Turn Hostile" (Dec 2025), found hostility escalates along coherent, annotatable trajectories tied to system breakdowns — rudeness as early dissatisfaction, aggression introducing confrontational blame, abuse shifting to expressive degradation — with substantial inter-annotator agreement (Krippendorff's α = 0.72), giving empirical backing to a graduated, severity-sensitive response policy rather than a single fixed response to all hostile-sounding input. Response-strategy evidence (Section 6) consistently favors empathetic/polite-refusal over avoidance or counterattack across abuse types, with the explicit, important caveat that avoidance specifically outperformed other strategies after sexual demands — a context-specific exception, not a universal ranking. **The real, literature-confirmed gap that must be flagged explicitly**: no study in these notes directly compares "stay unbothered/task-focused" against "respond with sympathy/concern" as competing strategies specifically for *playful, non-distressed* provocation. All of the empathy-beats-avoidance evidence comes from the abuse-response literature, which is about genuinely hostile or distressed input; the playful-requests literature establishes that misreading play as hostility (or vice versa) is a real failure mode, but it does not itself test which response style — unbothered task-focus versus sympathetic acknowledgment — performs better for play specifically. This is squarely **our hypothesis** territory if the project wants to claim it, not an established or even inferable result from existing work.

## 10. Existing systems closest to the proposed architecture

Several systems get close to individual pieces of what this project proposes, and describing them precisely (rather than hand-waving) makes clear none combines all the needed layers. **ContReph** (Amazon, 2021) is the closest existing production system to the "detect interaction-level breakdown from non-linguistic signals" layer: it uses dialogue history, implicit feedback, and inter-turn timing to detect rephrase events and extract the probable rephrase span, but it is detection-only — it does not select among qualitatively different repair strategies, does not track common ground, and is text-command-oriented (Alexa-style single-turn commands), not a sustained multi-turn voice conversation. **Interaction-Quality-as-RL-reward systems** (the Schmitt/Ultes/Minker LEGO-corpus lineage) are the closest existing precedent to "use a live quality estimate to adapt dialogue policy," already demonstrated to train cross-domain policies via RL using domain-independent interaction features — but IQ collapses its rich, multi-level feature hierarchy into a single ordinal 1–5 output used to drive policy, rather than preserving named, interpretable diagnostic counters that could select among distinct repair strategies; it also has no register/style-adaptation layer and no disclosure layer. The **agentic clarification-in-the-loop frameworks** (InferAct, ClarifyBench/SAGE-Agent, ClarEval, DiscoBench, Ambig-DS) are the closest existing precedent to "treat ambiguity-resolution as an action embedded in the planning loop rather than a pre-processing step": InferAct uses Theory-of-Mind-style belief reasoning to detect misaligned actions before execution, achieving up to 20% Macro-F1 improvement in misalignment detection on WebShop/HotPotQA/ALFWorld ([arXiv:2407.11843](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.11843)); SAGE-Agent improves ambiguous-task coverage by 7–39% while reducing questions by 1.5–2.7x. But these are, without exception, **text/tool-agent benchmarks (web shopping, coding, data science, search), not voice/spoken-dialogue systems**, and none of them incorporates interaction-quality estimation, register adaptation, or identity disclosure — they are purely about the ask-versus-assume decision. **RealityTest** is the closest existing system to the disclosure layer specifically, but it is an *evaluation* (testing whether models disclose), not an architecture for generating natural, register-appropriate disclosure — it identifies the reliability problem without proposing or validating a mechanism to solve it. Per every note's own explicit statement, **none of these systems combines interaction-quality estimation, repair-policy selection, linguistic style adaptation, speech/acoustic adaptation, and transparent handoff in one voice-first system**. The closest conceptual bridging work — Microsoft Research's "Challenges in Human-Agent Communication" (arXiv:2412.10380), which explicitly analyzes twelve human-agent communication challenges through a grounding lens — stops at taxonomy and problem-framing; it does not propose or evaluate an integrated runtime architecture.

## 11. A strict novelty audit — what is NOT novel

This list is deliberately long, per the explicit instruction not to claim novelty for individually well-studied phenomena. **Frustration detection** is not novel — established since at least the NIST 2000 Communicator Evaluation era and actively, recently re-validated ("Stupid robot," COLING 2025). **Sentiment detection** is not novel — decades-old NLP commodity, and the "Stupid robot" paper's own contribution is precisely showing sentiment detection is *insufficient* for frustration, not that detecting sentiment itself is new. **Satisfaction prediction** is not novel — PARADISE (1997) is the founding act of this exact idea, nearly 30 years old. **Clarification generation** is not novel — Ginzburg & Purver's taxonomy is 20+ years old, and targeted-vs-generic clarification has been independently rediscovered in at least three subfields. **Rephrase detection** is not novel — ContReph (2021) is a shipped Amazon production system; Google has independently published on the same problem. **Conversational repair** as a concept is not novel — Schegloff (1992) and Dingemanse & Enfield (2014) are the canonical, settled CA literature. **Adaptive politeness/register** is not novel — PADS/GenPADS and the broader politeness-adaptive-dialogue-systems literature predate this project by years, and computational politeness is described in the notes as a mature NLP subfield with four historical research milestones. **Barge-in handling** is not novel — Ström & Seneff (2000) and Zhao et al. (2015) are classic SDS engineering, now superseded by VAP-era learned models. **Handoff to a human** is not novel — a standard, named repair-strategy category in the CUI 2024 six-category taxonomy and explicit in the 2025 "Detect, Explain, Escalate" framing. **Anthropomorphism management** (avoiding deceptive human-presenting tricks) is not novel — the anthropomorphism/trust literature has studied the risks of over-humanization (privacy compromise, emotional overreliance) since well before 2024, and industry has already publicly course-corrected on at least one dimension (flirtatious responses to harassment, following 2017–2019 backlash). **AI disclosure as a concept** is not novel — it is now a binding legal requirement in at least two jurisdictions (EU AI Act Article 50, California AB 2905), not a design choice this project is originating. **Hyperarticulation as a phenomenon** is not novel — CHAM (1998) is 28 years old. **Common ground/grounding as a construct** is not novel — Clark & Brennan (1991) is 35 years old. **Third-position repair** is not novel — Schegloff (1992) predates this project by over three decades. What remains after subtracting all of the above, per the notes' own repeated framing, is narrower: the specific combination of a structured multi-signal diagnostic state (rather than a collapsed scalar) selecting among repair strategies, in a voice-first system, with an explicit and bounded style policy, and adversarially-robust disclosure — and even that combination's individual load-bearing pieces (CGT-vs-DST, the policy-vs-temperature argument, playful-vs-sympathetic response to non-distressed provocation) are each, at best, narrowly-scoped open questions rather than wholesale unclaimed territory.

## 12. The strongest remaining research gap, stated precisely

Synthesizing across all six notes, the recurring complaint is the same one, voiced independently in nearly every cluster: **fragmentation across non-voice, non-unified, non-real-time literatures.** The repair/breakdown notes found "no single, widely-cited benchmark that tests a system across all breakdown types simultaneously," with each existing corpus (MultConDB, the Japanese LREC corpus, BlockWorld-Repairs, REPAIR-QA) covering a different, non-overlapping slice, and most of it text/chat-oriented rather than speech-oriented. The clarification/IQ notes found no paper that implements and evaluates a structured multi-signal diagnostic state feeding a repair-strategy-selection mechanism, as opposed to either a single aggregate IQ score or an LLM-judgment-over-raw-transcript. The common-ground notes found the 448-paper survey's own explicit conclusion that "evaluation frameworks for dynamic grounding in LM interactions remain largely absent," and found no paper proposing a complete, voice-AI-specific (telephony/real-time speech) common-ground state architecture end-to-end. The register-adaptation notes found no head-to-head test of explicit style-policy against temperature-based control. The hyperarticulation/turn-taking notes found no source directly re-testing the classic acoustic error-spiral mechanism against a modern LLM-ASR pipeline, and no source testing hyperarticulation-onset and barge-in jointly as a combined friction signal. The identity-disclosure notes found no study crossing anthropomorphism manipulation with task-stakes manipulation in the same experiment, and no study comparing unbothered versus sympathetic response to playful (non-distressed) provocation. **The single most precise statement of the gap**, synthesizing all of these: no existing system or paper combines (a) a voice-first, real-time interaction-quality/breakdown-diagnostic layer built from structured, named, interpretable signals rather than one collapsed scalar, with (b) a repair-strategy-selection policy keyed to that diagnosed state, with (c) an explicit, bounded register/style-adaptation policy distinct from emergent LLM accommodation, with (d) adversarially-robust, register-appropriate identity disclosure — in one architecture, evaluated against a unified benchmark. Each of the four layers individually has decades of prior art; **the connective tissue between them, specifically for voice, is the gap**, and it is a narrower, more falsifiable gap than "nobody has built a system that handles interaction breakdown."

## 13. A minimal Boli architecture, if justified by the research

The research does justify a minimal architecture, but only a specific, narrowly-scoped one — not the sweeping "interaction integrity layer" framing in the abstract. The architecture should separate **interaction-state tracking** from **repair-strategy-policy** from **style-policy** from **disclosure-policy**, because the literature treats each as a separately-matured problem with separately-matured mechanisms, and fusing them prematurely into one scalar or one prompt would lose exactly the diagnostic interpretability that the notes repeatedly identify as the open opportunity. The **interaction-state layer** should be a structured object with named, evidence-graded counters — repeated-request count, repair-attempt count, failed-clarification count, semantic-progress delta — rather than a single IQ-style scalar; this is directly grounded in IQ's own multi-level feature hierarchy (exchange/window/dialogue) as precedent for structured, multi-granularity tracking, in the "Stupid robot" paper's explicit argument that real frustration shows up as repetition/negation/unresolved-tasks rather than sentiment words, and in Common Ground Tracking's distinction between DST (slot state) and CGT (mutually-accepted-proposition state) — but the specific counter set and its mapping to repair-strategy triggers is **this project's own hypothesis**, not literature-derived, since no source tests this exact combination. The **repair-strategy-policy layer** should implement the ordered strategy list from Section 6, gated by the diagnostic state, reusing the directly-evidenced moves (rephrase-not-repeat, targeted-not-generic clarification, confirm-only-the-uncertain-span, empathy-over-counterattack for abuse, escalate-to-human as a legitimate terminal state) while treating the newer, untested moves (shorten response, change register mid-repair, explicitly admit inability) as hypotheses to validate rather than assumptions to ship confidently. The **style-policy layer** should be explicit and multi-dimensional (formality, brevity, warmth, directness — "confirmation rigor" flagged as this project's own novel dimension, untested in the literature), bounded by task-risk as a floor that user-casualness cannot override (grounded in the service-recovery insincere-gesture finding and the sociolect-appropriation/dialect-stereotyping findings), and should not use temperature as a proxy for register — this last point is **our hypothesis assembled from adjacent evidence**, not a directly tested result, and should be explicitly labeled as such internally. The **disclosure-policy layer** should treat honest disclosure as a conversational-framing-robust behavior (per RealityTest) rather than a single boilerplate string, registered-appropriate to task stakes (an inference from combining CHI 2023's task-specific-trust finding with RealityTest, not a direct finding), using a disclosure-based-prompting pattern in the spirit of the Identity Design Framework. Two architectural commitments follow directly from the Prisma constraint documented in Section 5 and the companion paralinguistic report: **the acoustic/speech-signal family (hyperarticulation, volume, true barge-in detection) cannot be built on the current Prisma integration and must be explicitly deferred**, not quietly assumed available, and the diagnostic state must be built primarily from transcript text, system-side logs, and turn-level timestamps. This is a real, if unglamorous, architectural constraint the literature review alone would not surface.

## 14. A benchmark and experiment plan

The brief's scenario list is largely sound and should be kept with minor refinement. **Keep as specified**, since each maps to a literature-grounded phenomenon: normal interaction (baseline); one ASR error and repeated ASR error (CHAM, error spiral); partial-repetition correction and self-correction (Apple repetition paper, Levelt-style repair typology referenced in the companion report); AI misunderstands correction (TPR failure mode); redundant slot request (negative-feedback-utterance gap in DST literature); AI repeats near-identical answer (NIST 2000 Communicator Evaluation); two failed clarifications (over-asking/inappropriate-clarification literature); explicit "going in circles" comment (meta-comment signal); register change; hyperarticulation onset (CHAM, though flagged as **currently untestable with Boli's real Prisma integration** without separate audio capture); repeated interruption (VAP, barge-in literature, with the "Still Between Us?" third-party-interruption confound in mind); AI-identity challenge (RealityTest); explicit human request (terminal breakdown signal); playful/absurd request (Alexa playful-requests taxonomy); abusive request (abuse-response-strategy literature, with the severity-staging from "When Users Turn Hostile"); unsupported task (explicit-inability move); high-stakes task requiring stricter policy (service-recovery insincere-gesture finding). **One refinement worth making explicit**: the scenario list should separate "playful/absurd" from "abusive" with an explicit mid-tier "ambiguous/sarcastic" scenario, because the literature's clearest documented failure mode in this space is specifically *misclassifying between* these categories, not failing to handle either alone.

On metrics, several are directly literature-established and several are this project's own proposed additions, and the distinction matters for how confidently each should be reported. **Literature-established**: task completion and dialogue cost (duration, turn count) descend directly from PARADISE's original formulation; turns-to-resolution and repair success map onto the Interaction Quality tradition's exchange/window/dialogue-level tracking and the CUI 2024 scoping review's repair-strategy evaluation framing; repeated-question rate and repeated-response rate map onto the NIST 2000 repetition-vs-rephrasing finding and the "Stupid robot" paper's explicit emphasis on repetition as a frustration marker; (un)necessary clarification rate maps directly onto the "inappropriate clarification" and over-asking literatures, including the EACL 2024 paper's specificity-usefulness framework and the 52%-over-asking and 41%-fewer-questions figures; AI identity disclosure accuracy maps directly onto RealityTest's own evaluation methodology; trust calibration maps onto the CHI 2023 task-specific-trust study's own measurement approach. **This project's own proposed additions, not independently validated in the literature as named metrics**: semantic progress per turn, loop escape rate, already-supplied-information requests (as a named rate rather than a qualitative observation), and (un)necessary handoff — all four are reasonable operationalizations of phenomena the literature discusses qualitatively (unresolved tasks, context pollution, negative-feedback utterances, escalation thresholds) but none appears in the notes as an already-named, already-validated metric with precedent numbers to benchmark against. Perceived naturalness/competence is partially established (naturalness-evaluation research treats it as a distinct, BERT-classifier-measurable dimension) but has no voice-specific validation in these notes — the naturalness literature surveyed is text-output-focused, and whether these classifiers extend to spoken, TTS-delivered output is an explicit, unresolved gap. User effort has no single established operationalization across these notes; it would need to be assembled from turn count, repair-attempt count, and session duration as a composite, which is itself this project's own construction.

## 15. Kill criteria

Several honest kill criteria follow directly from the gaps and contradictions documented above, and should be treated as pre-registered falsification conditions, not retrofit excuses. **If a smaller structured-signal-counter approach shows no measurable improvement over a single live Interaction-Quality-style scalar** on repair-success or task-completion metrics in a controlled comparison, the structured-state framing is not earning its added complexity, and the project should fall back to a PARADISE/IQ-style single-score approach — this is a real risk given that the notes found no prior system has actually tested structured counters against a collapsed scalar head-to-head, meaning the hypothesized advantage is currently unproven in either direction. **If explicit multi-dimensional style-policy control shows no measurable behavioral or outcome difference from simple temperature/sampling adjustment** in A/B testing, the architectural distinction between style-policy and decoding-parameter control should be dropped, since the literature itself flags this comparison as assembled-not-confirmed; a negative result here would not be surprising given the total absence of prior art testing it either way. **If "confirmation rigor" as a standalone style dimension shows no distinguishable effect from directness or formality** in practice, it should be merged back into an existing dimension rather than maintained as a separate axis, since the notes explicitly flag it as having no literature precedent as an independent construct. **If the unbothered-versus-sympathetic response comparison for playful provocation shows no measurable difference** in user-rated naturalness or satisfaction, the project should default to the literature-backed empathetic-response policy uniformly (abuse-response literature) rather than maintaining a separate playful-provocation branch, since maintaining an unvalidated special case adds complexity the evidence does not yet support. **If hyperarticulation-based or acoustic-barge-in-based signals cannot be made available within a reasonable engineering horizon** (i.e., raw audio capture and processing remain out of scope), the entire speech/acoustic row of the signal taxonomy should be formally deferred rather than left as aspirational architecture — the companion paralinguistic report already treats this as the realistic default, and this report should not quietly assume otherwise. **If the ASR/Prisma-behavior signal family remains unavailable** (confidence, N-best, diarization, SNR) after checking whether an enterprise tier or the undocumented streaming `raw` field unlocks any of it, that entire signal family should be removed from the architecture rather than carried as dead weight in documentation — a cheap, concrete check (inspecting the `raw` field) should happen before any further investment in ASR-behavior-dependent design. **If the project cannot produce a benchmark that tests at least two friction axes in combination** (e.g., a register change co-occurring with a repeated ASR error, or a playful request co-occurring with task-state stalling) and instead only ever tests one scenario type at a time, it has not actually closed the gap identified in Section 12, which is specifically about integration and combination, not single-axis detection — in that case the project should be honest that it has built another single-axis detector, not an interaction-integrity system.

**Verdict**: this is substantially a repackaging of decades-old spoken-dialogue-systems and conversation-analysis research — PARADISE, Interaction Quality, Clark & Brennan grounding, Schegloff's repair, Oviatt's CHAM, and Ström & Seneff's barge-in work together cover the large majority of the conceptual ground this project would claim, and every one of frustration detection, clarification generation, rephrase detection, adaptive politeness, barge-in handling, handoff, anthropomorphism management, and AI disclosure is independently well-studied and in several cases already shipped in production systems (Alexa's ContReph, Amazon's own playful-request handling). What survives honest scrutiny as genuinely open is narrow but real: no cited source tests a structured, interpretable multi-signal diagnostic state against a collapsed IQ-style scalar for repair-strategy selection; no cited source tests explicit style-policy against temperature-based control head-to-head; no cited source directly compares unbothered-versus-sympathetic response to playful (non-distressed) provocation; and no cited source combines interaction-quality estimation, repair-policy, register-policy, and adversarially-robust disclosure in one voice-first architecture evaluated against a unified, multi-axis benchmark. Those four narrow gaps are legitimate, falsifiable, worth pursuing — and they are not a new field. They are four specific, testable comparisons sitting inside a problem space that has otherwise been mapped, named, and largely solved piece by piece since 1991.
