# Observe the signal, defer the verdict

Boli's speech pipeline can today return exactly six fields from a REST call to Prisma — `success`, `request_id`, `transcript`, `model`, `processing_time`, `end_to_end_latency` — and a slightly richer but still thin set from the streaming endpoint (`audio_duration_ms`, `segment_id`, `segment_index`, `latency`, `timestamp`, plus one undocumented `raw` field); it returns **no confidence score, no word timings, no N-best list, no partial-hypothesis history, no VAD events, no diarization, no overlap flag, and no SNR estimate**, confirmed empirically rather than assumed. That hard constraint reframes the whole project: the richest fingerprint Boli can build in v1 is not an emotion classifier but a disciplined, three-tier catalogue — **directly observed** acoustic/textual measurements, **cautiously derived** interaction-state estimates built from them with explicit evidence grading, and a firmly excluded third tier of **speculative downstream outcomes** (churn, default, escalation, dissatisfaction) that should not be operationalized at all in this phase. The strongest single constraint on tier B is a 2025 PLOS ONE systematic review of 39 studies finding consistent, direction-specific acoustic patterns for anger and stress, a partially consistent pattern for cognitive load, and **no consistent acoustic pattern whatsoever for fear or anxiety** ([PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)) — a finding independently corroborated across two separately phrased search queries and therefore treated with materially higher confidence than almost any other specific number in this report. Nearly everything else below rests on WebSearch-synthesized snippets rather than full-text reads, because this session's network egress policy blocked direct `WebFetch` to essentially every primary academic domain tested (PMC, PLOS, arXiv, ISCA Archive, ACL Anthology, audeering.github.io, gnani.ai) — a constraint that should make the report's numbers indicative, not load-bearing, with the PLOS ONE fear/anxiety null as the one clearly-verified exception. The most important adversarial finding is about Prisma itself, not about emotion: the apparent gradient in which diarization/turn-taking literature connects richly to interaction state, VAD connects moderately, and noise/SNR estimation connects to almost nothing, is just as plausibly an artifact of which topics happen to have more published papers as it is a genuine signal-richness ordering — and throughout, every acoustic or ASR-behavior signal is at permanent risk of measuring that **Prisma is struggling with the channel**, not that the **speaker is struggling with the conversation**, a confound this report treats as live and unresolved rather than already handled.

## Tier A, tier B, tier C — and why tier C stays out of v1 entirely

Every marker, finding, and proposed field in this report sorts into exactly one of three tiers, and the tiering is the organizing spine of the whole document, not a side classification. **Tier A — directly observed signals** — are things physically measured from raw audio or explicitly returned by an API: an F0 track from a pitch tracker, a pause's duration in milliseconds, the transcript string itself, Prisma's `processing_time` field, a diarization turn boundary if one existed. These are facts about the recording, not interpretations of the speaker. **Tier B — derived interaction-state estimates** — are constructs built *from* tier-A signals through a model or a documented heuristic: an `arousal_score`, a `hesitation_score`, a `stress_like_pattern_score`, a `cognitive_load_proxy`. Every tier-B field in this report carries an explicit evidence-strength label, because the tier-A-to-tier-B mapping is exactly where the literature's generalization failures live. **Tier C — speculative downstream outcomes** — are business- or risk-relevant end states: conversation abandonment, escalation, churn, dissatisfaction, and, in any higher-stakes domain, default or creditworthiness. Tier C does not appear anywhere in this report as something to build, measure, or even name as a candidate field, because operationalizing it requires a categorically different research program — out-of-sample validation on data never seen during feature design, calibration analysis (not just rank-ordering accuracy), temporal validation that a correlation holding six months ago still holds today, cross-language/cross-dialect/cross-channel/cross-speaker-demographic testing at a scale no Indian-language corpus surveyed here reaches, and a fairness, legal, and consent/governance review — none of which this report attempts, and none of which Boli v1 should attempt either. No automated action of any kind — routing, retry logic, tone adjustment, prioritization, approval, or denial — should ever read a tier-B field, let alone a hypothetical tier-C one; a passive observability layer writes provenance-tagged observations, it does not act on them.

## Prisma's real surface is six fields; nearly everything else is theory

The brief's six-way source taxonomy — raw waveform, Prisma metadata, Prisma recognition behaviour, transcript, dialogue history, and speaker-relative longitudinal history — only becomes useful once it is cross-checked against what Prisma *actually* returns, as opposed to what ASR systems in general sometimes expose. The table below draws that line explicitly for every category the brief asked about.

| Source category | What it theoretically could contain (general ASR literature) | What Prisma/Gnani's tested integration actually returns |
|---|---|---|

| Prisma metadata (REST) | — | `success`, `request_id`, `transcript`, `model`, `processing_time`, `end_to_end_latency` — confirmed empirically on `api.vachana.ai/stt/v3`. No confidence, no timestamps, no diarization |
| Prisma metadata (streaming) | — | `text`, `audio_duration_ms`, `segment_id`, `segment_index`, `latency`, `timestamp`, and an undocumented `raw` field, per Gnani's documented `StreamTranscriptEvent` schema (WebSearch synthesis of docs.gnani.ai; not independently fetched). Still no confidence, word timing, N-best, or diarization in anything itemized |
| Prisma recognition behaviour | Word/utterance confidence, N-best alternatives, partial-hypothesis revision ("flicker"), deletion/substitution error-type prediction — all well-established in the general E2E-ASR literature ([arXiv:2204.04288](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04288); [arXiv:2006.01416](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01416); [arXiv:2104.12870](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.12870)) | **Not available at all**, confirmed empirically rather than inferred from absence of documentation. Whether Hindi/Indic filler tokens ("matlab," "pata nahi") survive verbatim transcription with the fidelity Switchboard-style detectors assume is untested on Boli's own data |
| Dialogue history | Per-turn aggregates within one call: turn count, cross-turn pause/latency pattern, repair frequency, topic/intent shifts | Theoretically computable from successive transcript calls and their timestamps today; not currently aggregated or stored as a structured feature anywhere in the pipeline |

Gnani does operate a wholly separate voice-biometrics product, **Armour365**, that builds persistent per-speaker voiceprints from "300+ proprietary audio features" with sub-2% Equal Error Rate ([IoT For All](https://www.iotforall.com/news/gnani.ai-launches-armour365--voice-biometrics-software-based-on-patented-technologies)) — demonstrating the technical capacity exists at the vendor, but no evidence was found that it is integrated with or shares a backend with Prisma's ASR engine |

The governing rule this table establishes is blunt: almost every acoustic marker discussed anywhere in the paralinguistics literature requires raw audio that Boli does not currently capture, and almost every ASR-behavioral marker discussed in the confidence/instability literature requires API fields Prisma does not currently expose. The only source family that is both scientifically well-studied *and* actually populated today is the transcript text itself.

## The marker inventory: breadth first, reduction later

The point of this section is deliberately *not* to narrow down to a handful of labels. It is to lay out the widest scientifically defensible observable feature space, tier it, and grade each entry on the dimensions the brief specifies, so that later design decisions start from an honest map of what exists rather than from a pre-selected shortlist.

### Tier C — explicitly excluded from v1

Conversation abandonment, escalation, churn, dissatisfaction, and any financial or legal outcome (default, creditworthiness, fraud risk) are not catalogued here as markers at all, because they are not observations — they are claims about a relationship between an observation and a future event, and that relationship has not been tested, calibrated, or governed. They belong to a separate, later, offline research track, discussed on its own terms below.

## Fear has no acoustic signature — and that is the headline, not a footnote

The single most important finding in this entire research effort is negative, and it should be presented first, not buried under positive-sounding findings. The 2025 PLOS ONE systematic review by Schewski, Magimai Doss, Beldi, and Keller screened 38 articles across PubMed, PsycInfo, Web of Science, and Scopus and found that anger shows a consistent three-feature profile (elevated F0, increased volume, faster rate), stress shows a consistent profile of its own (increased F0 and intensity, reduced duration), cognitive load is "linked to increased F0 and intensity" but with F0 evidence explicitly rated "less clear than for negative emotions and stress," and then: **"no consistent acoustic patterns were identified for fear or anxiety"** ([PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/); [PubMed 40705747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40705747/)). This is not a search-snippet artifact — it was returned identically by two independently-phrased queries, which is as close to verified as anything in this report gets given the egress constraints. Even the review's framing of its *best* constructs is modest: the overall conclusion is that "research remains inconsistent in identifying reliable acoustic markers, providing limited guidance for researchers and practitioners," meaning anger/stress are *relatively* more consistent than fear/anxiety, not absolutely settled science.

This null result does not stand alone; it sits inside a much broader methodological critique of categorical emotion recognition from voice that this report takes seriously rather than treats as a disclaimer. Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez, and Pollak's review concluded that external emotional expression "varies substantially across cultures, situations, and even across people within a single situation" ([DOI 10.1177/1529100619832930](http://eprints.gla.ac.uk/178385/7/178385.pdf)). Mohammad's "Ethics Sheet for Automatic Emotion Recognition" allows that categorical emotion labels can be a legitimate communicative convenience but warns that "careless endorsement of theories can lead to the perpetuation of ideas that are actively harmful (such as suggesting we can determine internal state from outward appearance — physiognomy)" ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.cl-2.1.pdf)). Paul Ekman, the architect of Basic Emotion Theory itself, is on record calling commercial emotion-recognition technology "pseudoscience" ([The Algorithmic Bridge](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/ai-emotion-recognition-is-a-pseudoscientific)). And a 2026 benchmark, VocalAffectBench, measured real-world reliability directly and found the leading model misses more than half of its clips, with average performance across models at only 35.1%, concluding current emotion embeddings are "fundamentally unreliable and structurally biased by acoustic distractors" ([arXiv:2608.28932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28932)).

Beyond fear specifically, the generalization-failure evidence is broad and should be surfaced with equal prominence to any positive finding, construct by construct. On **language and corpus type**: three of the four most widely used SER benchmarks (IEMOCAP, CREMA-D, RAVDESS) are acted or elicited studio recordings, not naturalistic speech, and IEMOCAP's own creators published a methodological follow-up acknowledging that elicitation technique shapes the "emotion" being measured; a 2023 critique found the scripted portion of IEMOCAP "yields high within-dataset accuracy but poor cross-dataset generalization because of strong sentence overlap and lexical redundancy," recommending it be discarded entirely for generalization claims ([arXiv:2304.00860](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.00860)). On **cross-lingual transfer**: SSL models outperform humans within their native language, but "both humans and SSL models show a significant accuracy drop in the cross-lingual setting," with humans retaining a relative advantage over models cross-lingually ([arXiv:2409.16920](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.16920)). On **dialect and accent**: a regional UK accent (South Yorkshire) was documented as being misclassified as more severe cognitive decline in an adjacent speech-based clinical model (per search synthesis; no primary source could be independently located to cite directly) — the same confound mechanism SER is structurally vulnerable to, since nothing about SER's architecture prevents an unfamiliar prosodic or rhythmic pattern from being read as a spurious affect signal rather than as ordinary dialectal variation. On **individual speaker and demographic characteristics**: jitter and shimmer are confounded by vocal intensity, F0, vowel identity, and speaker age/sex in non-uniform, non-additive ways — "shimmer increased with age for males, but neither jitter nor shimmer increased with age for females" — and these measures degrade in validity precisely on the aperiodic voices that stress is supposed to produce ([stcloudstate.edu](https://repository.stcloudstate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?params=/context/stcloud_ling/article/1155/&path_info=1_Koffi2025ComprehensiveReviewOfJitterShimmerHNR.pdf)). On **microphone/device and channel**: "worse results are obtained when a model is tested with patterns at different SNRs than training, and the effect of codification also makes results worse" — SNR mismatch and codec/compression are separate, compounding confounds, not one problem ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3498851.3499022)). On **telephone codecs and noise specifically**: narrowband 8kHz telephony audio degrades ASR WER by roughly 8–12% absolute compared to wideband, independent of any confidence question, and modern E2E ASR becomes *overconfident* rather than merely less accurate under low SNR — exactly the operating regime Prisma targets ([arXiv:2509.07195](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.07195)). On **speaking style**: the 2025 review itself reports "heterogeneous" results for speech rate and duration under stress, with some studies finding increases, others no effect at all, and direct contradictions on direction for anxiety specifically. Each of these is a documented failure mode, not a hypothetical one, and each should weigh as heavily in Boli's design posture as any single positive finding above.

## A speaker's own baseline beats a population threshold — probably, by analogy, not by direct proof

The brief's instinct toward longitudinal, speaker-relative tracking deserves real space, because the evidence for the *general principle* is genuinely stronger than the evidence for almost any specific tier-B construct discussed above — but it must be reported with precision about what kind of evidence it actually is. Within-patient longitudinal speech monitoring in clinical contexts (Multiple Sclerosis progression tracking, COPD screening) is explicitly framed around "each patient serv[ing] as their own control, holding age, sex, anatomy, and habitual voice quality fixed by construction" ([arXiv:2608.21599](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21599)), with changepoint-detection algorithms invoked to flag when a speaker's vocal characteristics shift over time ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S221103482400467X)). Speaker-verification research independently validates the normalization half of the same method: adaptive score normalization using a per-speaker cohort for mean/variance computation is standard practice there too. The single closest literature analog to Boli's actual ambition is a paper on "Speaker Embeddings as Individuality Proxy for Voice Stress Detection," which reports that concatenating pretrained audio representations with speaker embeddings (Resemblyzer, ECAPA) as a personalized baseline "significantly improves voice stress detection" compared to speaker-agnostic models ([arXiv:2306.05915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05915)) — but this is a deliberately engineered personalization scheme tested within a study, not a demonstration of long-running longitudinal tracking across many real calls over months.

The honest statement that must be made explicitly, and repeated rather than softened: **the method — within-speaker z-scoring and changepoint detection against a rolling baseline — is well-precedented by analogy from clinical voice monitoring and speaker verification; its direct application to affect or interaction-state tracking specifically has not been validated anywhere found in this research.** This report does not assume any particular example marker (pause density, speech rate, repair frequency, pitch variability, ASR instability, interruption/overlap behavior) actually works for this purpose — it evaluates only whether the *general principle* of within-speaker baselining has support, and the answer is "yes, by analogy; no, not directly." A defensible longitudinal design built from that analogy would maintain a rolling per-speaker distribution (mean, variance, sample count) for each tier-A feature, require a minimum sample count before trusting any z-score (early calls should report low-confidence or null rather than a noisy baseline), and run changepoint detection against the rolling baseline rather than reacting to single-call noise — exactly the architecture the clinical-monitoring literature uses for disease progression, repurposed here. Two qualifications follow directly. First, the baseline itself is channel-dependent: reverberation and SNR drift also degrade speaker-ID/diarization robustness, so a baseline built across inconsistent channel conditions risks being contaminated by channel variation rather than purely speaker-state variation, and baseline updates should carry their own channel-quality gate. Second — and this is the payoff worth arguing explicitly rather than asserting — "this speaker's arousal-adjacent features have shifted relative to their own six-month baseline" is a defensible, falsifiable observation about deviation from a documented norm, while "this speaker is becoming more anxious" is not, because it smuggles back in exactly the categorical-emotion leap the fear/anxiety null finding warns against, and it does so using a within-speaker method that was never validated for affect in the first place. The former framing is more defensible for a second, independent reason beyond the categorical-label problem: it sidesteps most of the demographic confounds (sex, age, dialect) documented throughout this report by design, since the comparison is always against the same person's own distribution rather than a population threshold that bakes in whichever demographic group dominated the training data.

## Is the diarization-beats-noise-estimation gradient real, or just a literature-density artifact?

The broadened audit of Prisma-adjacent subsystems — VAD, diarization, speaker/channel modeling, noise estimation — found what looks like a clean gradient: diarization/turn-taking has the richest connecting literature to interaction state, VAD has a thinner but real connection via purpose-built classifiers, and noise/SNR estimation has essentially none. That pattern is worth presenting, and then actively trying to break, rather than accepted as a tidy finding.

On the **diarization** end, overlapping speech is reported as "strongly correlated with high conflict levels" in a 2013 Interspeech study on automatic conflict detection via speaker overlap ([ISCA Archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2013/grezes13_interspeech.pdf)), and call-center conversation-analytic work finds conflict "mirrored in the prosodic features of the interaction." The caveat is structural: raw overlap detection cannot by itself distinguish a true interruption from benign floor-exchange, since turn-taking theory defines interruption by whether a Transition Relevance Place was imminent and by communicative intent, not by acoustic simultaneity ([arXiv:2603.24144](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24144v1)). On the **VAD** end, the literature connects VAD-adjacent features to disfluency and clinical state generally, and one escalation-detection paper explicitly uses VAD output as a feature inside a purpose-built classifier, reporting 81.5% UAR versus a 72.2% baseline ([arXiv:2104.06004](https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/arxiv:2104.06004)) — but this is VAD-as-ingredient-in-a-trained-classifier, not VAD-output-alone-as-a-freestanding-signal, and nothing documents Prisma exposing VAD-adjacent output beyond streaming's `segment_id`/`audio_duration_ms`, whose relationship to actual speech/silence boundaries is inferred, not documented. On the **noise/SNR** end, SNR estimation is well-established as an internal technique for predicting ASR WER and gating recording usability, and Gnani explicitly markets a "denoise-first streaming pipeline" implying such an estimate exists internally ([Gnani technology page](https://www.gnani.ai/technology)) — but no literature anywhere in this research repurposes signal-quality metrics as a behavioral or interaction-state proxy. This is the clearest "no" in the entire audit.

The adversarial question this report is obligated to ask is: does this gradient reflect genuine signal richness, or merely that more researchers happen to study conflict/overlap for reasons having nothing to do with whether it is actually more informative about speaker state (for instance, overlap is easier to label from a transcript-and-audio pair than signal quality is, or conflict detection has more obvious commercial and call-center funding than noise-estimation-as-behavioral-signal ever would)? The evidence that would distinguish these two explanations — a systematic, quantitative comparison of effect sizes or predictive power across all four subsystems on a shared held-out dataset — does not exist in anything surfaced by this research. What does exist is a weaker, indirect tell: this audit found no vendor-neutral, cross-cutting survey that catalogues "secondary behavioral uses of ASR-adjacent engineering signals" as a named research area at all, unlike speech emotion recognition itself, which is a mature, well-surveyed field. That absence cuts both ways — it is consistent with noise/SNR genuinely carrying less signal, and it is equally consistent with this whole niche simply being under-explored because nobody has looked, in which case the apparent gradient is exactly the kind of artifact the adversarial framing warns about. Given that most of this section's sourcing is WebSearch snippets of papers this environment could not fetch directly, the honest position is: **the gradient is plausible, but the current notes do not distinguish a real signal-richness ordering from a literature-density artifact, and this should be treated as an open, unresolved risk rather than a settled finding.**

The distinction that must be hammered on throughout this entire section, and that the current evidence does not resolve, is whether any of these signals reveal something about the **speaker's** state or merely reveal that **Prisma itself** is struggling because the audio or channel is objectively harder — a regional dialect it handles poorly, a noisy line, code-switching it was not tuned for. These two explanations are easily confounded and currently indistinguishable with the data this research could access: elevated ASR instability, degraded confidence (if it existed), or poor diarization accuracy on a given call could just as easily mean "Prisma is failing on this Hindi-English code-switched, 8kHz, noisy-line call" as "this speaker is more hesitant than usual." Gnani's own benchmarking guidance makes essentially this point about WER generally, noting "the audio profile of Indian contact center calls (8kHz telephony, GSM codec compression, code-switching, background noise) is not represented in any major public benchmark dataset" and recommending practitioners build their own test sets rather than trust published numbers ([Gnani benchmarking guidance](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/research/how-to-benchmark-stt-api-indian-languages)) — which is itself evidence that Prisma's recognition difficulty varies systematically with channel/dialect conditions that have nothing to do with speaker affect. Separately, and worth flagging as the single most concrete piece of infrastructure discovered anywhere in this audit: Gnani operates **Armour365**, a wholly separate voice-biometrics product building persistent, encrypted per-speaker voiceprints from "300+ proprietary audio features" with sub-2% Equal Error Rate ([IoT For All](https://www.iotforall.com/news/gnani.ai-launches-armour365--voice-biometrics-software-based-on-patented-technologies)) — demonstrating the organizational and technical capacity for speaker-relative, longitudinal infrastructure already exists at the vendor, even though it is architecturally unconnected to Prisma's ASR engine today and no evidence was found of the two sharing a backend.

## The ablation Boli needs before any construct graduates past "stored observation"

Before any tier-B construct is trusted for anything beyond passive storage, the comparison the brief asks for — raw acoustic features only, Prisma-derived features only, transcript/linguistic features only, acoustic+Prisma, and acoustic+Prisma+transcript/context — needs to run as a controlled experiment with held-out speakers and held-out languages, and the specific question it answers is narrow and falsifiable: **does the Prisma fingerprint contribute information beyond what ordinary acoustic analysis of raw audio already contains?** Given how thin Prisma's exposed surface was found to be throughout this report, the honest expectation going in, stated plainly rather than discovered as a surprise after running the experiment, is that the Prisma-only arm is close to a **null condition** on every construct except raw latency, and that acoustic-only and acoustic+transcript will do essentially all of the useful work. The experiment should still be specified and run properly rather than skipped on the strength of that prediction, because (a) the `raw` streaming field is a genuine unknown that could overturn the prediction cheaply, and (b) a documented null result for the Prisma-only arm is itself a citable, reusable finding for the project rather than a foregone conclusion that needs no record.

Concretely: the **Prisma-only arm** (confidence/instability metadata) should be logged as "not testable with the current integration" rather than silently dropped, and re-tested the moment the `raw` streaming field or an enterprise tier is inspected — this is one of the cheapest, highest-value follow-ups identified anywhere in this research. The **transcript-only arm** should perform respectably on hesitation/uncertainty specifically, given the mature Switchboard-lineage evidence, but should be expected to be weak on arousal/stress/anger, where the evidence base is overwhelmingly acoustic rather than lexical. The **acoustic-only arm** should be the strongest single-modality arm for arousal-adjacent constructs, consistent with the 2025 review, but should be evaluated specifically on its ability to *not* produce a usable fear/anxiety signal — a null result there replicates the review's own conclusion and should be logged as evidence the system is behaving correctly, not as a failure needing more work. The **acoustic+transcript arm** (no Prisma-internal metadata, since there is none to add) is the practically achievable ceiling for Boli today and should be benchmarked against acoustic-only specifically to quantify whether textual disfluency markers add incremental value once acoustic features already exist — a test this research did not find already run anywhere in the literature. Held-out speakers and held-out languages are not optional refinements to this design; given the cross-lingual and cross-speaker generalization failures documented above, and given that Indian-language SER resources are fragmented and small (tens of hours — ERIL, BhavVani, IITKGP-SEHSC/SESC — relative to MSP-Podcast's 409 hours ([Springer, ERIL](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11277-022-09829-1))), any construct validated primarily on Western data should be assumed structurally likely to misfire on Indian-accented or code-switched speech until proven otherwise on held-out Indian-language speakers specifically. The second design requirement worth calling out is that testing **longitudinal within-speaker change against absolute cross-speaker classification** is the single most promising axis this research surfaced, precisely because it is the axis with the least direct precedent and the most to gain — if a speaker-relative z-score reliably outperforms a population-absolute threshold on held-out speakers, which the stress-detection personalization literature suggests it should, that is a genuinely novel, defensible contribution rather than a restatement of existing SER benchmarks.

## Six sibling observation families, not one fused classifier

Nothing in this report should be read as implying that Boli's fingerprint needs to collapse into a single score, a single workflow, or a single use case. The evidence instead points to several parallel, loosely coupled observation families that can be built, stored, and validated independently: a **linguistic/regional** family (dialect and code-switching patterns surviving in the transcript, language-specific filler forms, ITN behavior); an **acoustic/channel** family (the raw-waveform markers in the tables above, gated by estimated channel quality); an **ASR-behavior** family (currently empty for Prisma, reserved rather than populated, re-checked when richer endpoints surface); a **repair/disfluency** family (the one source family that is both mature in the literature and actually available today via the transcript); an **interaction-state** family (the tier-B constructs — arousal-like, stress-like, hesitation, cognitive-load-proxy — each carrying its own evidence tag); and a **longitudinal speaker-relative** family (rolling baselines and deviation flags, built by analogy from clinical monitoring). None of these families requires fusing into one classifier to be useful, and none of them is designed around a specific workflow such as collections, support, fraud, or commerce — the taxonomy is deliberately task-, language-, and dialect-agnostic, because the evidence for which families generalize and which don't is itself still being gathered.

## What this means for Boli v1

1. **Everything potentially observable** spans a wide, well-charted feature space — F0, intensity, rate, pause structure, voice quality (jitter/shimmer/HNR/spectral tilt), formants, MFCCs, overlap/diarization-derived turn metrics, and filled-pause/repair disfluency markers — and the breadth of this space is itself the useful output of this research, not a preamble to a narrower list.

2. **Everything Prisma can give us today, verified against our actual access**, is exactly six REST fields (`success`, `request_id`, `transcript`, `model`, `processing_time`, `end_to_end_latency`) plus a slightly richer but still thin streaming schema (`audio_duration_ms`, `segment_id`, `segment_index`, `latency`, `timestamp`, an unexamined `raw` field) — confirmed empirically, not assumed from documentation.

3. **Everything requiring raw-audio processing** is, in practice, almost the entire acoustic tier: F0, intensity, jitter, shimmer, HNR, spectral tilt, formants, MFCCs, and reliable overlap/pause detection all require audio Boli does not currently capture or process alongside the Prisma call.

4. **The strongest interaction-state variables we could plausibly derive** are arousal-like, anger-like, and stress-like acoustic patterns (elevated F0, elevated intensity, rate/duration changes) and hesitation/uncertainty patterns (pause duration plus pre-pause lengthening, filled-pause presence/location, F0/rate modulation) — each built from tier-A features with multiple, methodologically distinct, converging sources.

5. **The variables that should not be exposed because the evidence is too weak** lead with fear and anxiety, for which the 2025 systematic review found no consistent acoustic pattern across 39 studies at all — not "weak," but absent. Valence/sentiment should be treated the same way, since the eGeMAPS literature itself states there are no strong discriminative speech features for positive-versus-negative speech. Jitter/shimmer-as-generic-stress-index and raw ASR-instability-as-uncertainty-proxy belong in the same excluded category: plausible-sounding, unvalidated, and confound-laden.

6. **What is language/dialect independent versus confounded by them**: the broad *direction* of core prosodic cues (low/falling pitch and reduced variability with confidence; longer pauses, filled pauses, and F0 variability with uncertainty) recurs across independently-conducted single-language studies and has some direct cross-population support (Goupil et al.'s reverse-correlation study found no in-group listener advantage), but the specific numbers (91% word-localization accuracy, RMSE 1.6, exact additive-effect orderings) are each single-study results, and cross-lingual SER accuracy drops substantially even for strong SSL models — meaning direction is more portable than magnitude, and nothing here should be assumed to transfer to Indian-language or code-switched speech without separate validation.

7. **Which signals become more meaningful longitudinally within one speaker**: in principle, any tier-A feature — pause density, speech rate, repair frequency, pitch variability, overlap/interruption behavior, and, if it ever became available, ASR instability — benefits from a within-speaker baseline rather than a population threshold, because this is how the method addresses demographic confounds by construction. This is extrapolation by analogy from clinical voice monitoring and speaker-verification normalization, not a direct finding for affect tracking, and no single one of these example markers is assumed to work; only the general principle is evaluated as well-supported.

8. **What Prisma adds beyond conventional acoustic features is, honestly, very little to nothing on the current integration.** Every field actually returned is either ASR-engineering metadata (latency, processing time) or the transcript itself; none of it is confidence, timing, diarization, or signal-quality data. The one thing Prisma does add — the transcript text, under verbatim mode — is valuable precisely because it feeds the mature, portable disfluency-detection literature, not because of anything specific to Prisma's architecture.

9. **The smallest experiment that tells us whether this thread is worth adding to Boli** is the acoustic-only vs. transcript-only vs. acoustic+transcript ablation on held-out speakers and held-out languages, logging the Prisma-behavior arm as explicitly untestable today. The expected result, stated honestly rather than discovered as a surprise, is that acoustic+transcript is close to the achievable ceiling and the Prisma-only arm is close to null — but the experiment should still be run and recorded, because the undocumented streaming `raw` field is a cheap, concrete way that prediction could be wrong.

10. **Surprising marker families not obviously anticipated** include diarization/turn-taking as the single best-evidenced "adjacent subsystem" connection to interaction state (via conflict/overlap research), and the discovery that Gnani already operates a completely separate, technically mature voice-biometrics product (Armour365, 300+ proprietary features, sub-2% EER) that has no documented connection to Prisma's ASR engine but demonstrates the underlying speaker-embedding infrastructure for longitudinal tracking already exists somewhere inside the vendor.

11. **The literature does suggest a better abstraction than "emotion" altogether, and the answer is yes, directly**: continuous, operationally-defined constructs (arousal, hesitation, stress-like pattern, cognitive-load proxy) are better justified than discrete categorical emotion labels, for three convergent reasons — the field's own flagship challenge series (ComParE) moved progressively away from basic emotion toward conflict, cognitive load, deception, and atypical affect since 2013; the best single evidence source in this report (the 2025 systematic review) found its *strongest* acoustic accuracy for stress and cognitive load, not fine-grained emotion categories; and the field's best-annotated naturalistic corpus (MSP-Podcast) treats continuous valence/arousal/dominance ratings as core, quality-controlled infrastructure rather than a fringe alternative to categorical labels. The honest caveat carried the whole way through this report applies here too: no single study was found proving dimensional models generalize *better* cross-culturally than categorical ones in head-to-head comparison — this is the strongest methodological bet the evidence supports, not a settled empirical fact, and it is exactly the degree of confidence a passive observability layer should carry into production.
