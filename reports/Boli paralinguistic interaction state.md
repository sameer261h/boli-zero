# Capture the signal, not the verdict

Speech carries real, measurable information about how a person is speaking — pitch, loudness, pausing, and disfluency patterns show direction-consistent links to arousal, stress, anger, and cognitive load across a 2025 systematic review of 39 studies — but the single most actionable finding for Boli is negative: **that same review found no consistent acoustic pattern for fear or anxiety at all**, and Boli's actual Prisma/Gnani integration, confirmed firsthand, returns only a transcript string plus model name and timing — no confidence score, no word timestamps, no diarization, no N-best, nothing ASR-internal to mine. Those two facts together point to one architecture: capture continuous, operationally-defined signals (hesitation_score, arousal_score, cognitive-load proxies) from raw audio and from text-survivable disfluency markers, store them as passive, provenance-tagged observations, and explicitly refuse to expose categorical emotion fields — especially fear — that the evidence does not support. This report inventories the broadest scientifically defensible set of markers, grades each on evidence strength, Prisma availability, audio dependence, language/noise/speaker sensitivity, and proposes a storage schema built around that grading. It also finds that Prisma's non-transcription subsystems (diarization, VAD, speaker/channel modeling, noise estimation) sit on a real gradient of connecting literature — diarization/turn-taking has substantive ties to interaction state, VAD has some via purpose-built classifiers, noise/SNR estimation has essentially none — and that longitudinal, speaker-relative tracking is well-precedented by analogy (clinical voice monitoring, speaker verification) but not yet directly validated for affect. A verification caveat applies throughout: this session's network egress policy blocked direct `WebFetch` to nearly every primary academic domain (PMC, PLOS, arXiv, ISCA Archive, ACL Anthology, audeering.github.io, gnani.ai), so most findings below were sourced through WebSearch's synthesized snippets rather than full-text reads — exact numbers should be treated as indicative pending primary verification, with one exception: the 2025 PLOS ONE review's fear/anxiety null finding was independently corroborated across two separately-phrased queries and can be reported with materially higher confidence than the other specific figures in this report.

## Fear has no acoustic signature; anger, stress, and cognitive load do

The strongest single piece of evidence in this entire research effort is a 24 July 2025 PLOS ONE systematic review by Schewski, Magimai Doss, Beldi, and Keller, which screened 38 articles across PubMed, PsycInfo, Web of Science, and Scopus and produced a graded, construct-by-construct reliability verdict rather than a flat "emotion recognition works" claim ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0328833); [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)). Anger showed a consistent, direction-specific three-feature profile — elevated fundamental frequency, increased volume, faster speech rate — and stress showed its own consistent profile of increased F0 and intensity with reduced speech duration. Cognitive load sat one notch down: linked to increased F0 and intensity, but the review explicitly rated the F0 evidence "less clear than for negative emotions and stress," establishing a three-tier gradient (anger/stress clearest, cognitive load partially clear) rather than a binary reliable/unreliable split. Then comes the finding that should anchor Boli's entire design posture: **"no consistent acoustic patterns were identified for fear or anxiety"** — a verdict corroborated identically across two independently-phrased search queries, which is as close to verified as anything in this report gets given the egress constraints ([PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/); [PubMed 40705747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40705747/)).

This is not an isolated result. It converges with a much broader methodological critique of categorical emotion recognition that spans affective science, computational-linguistics ethics, and policy. Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez, and Pollak's *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* review concluded that external emotional expression "varies substantially across cultures, situations, and even across people within a single situation," undermining the premise that fixed signals reliably reveal discrete internal categories ([DOI 10.1177/1529100619832930](http://eprints.gla.ac.uk/178385/7/178385.pdf)). Mohammad's "Ethics Sheet for Automatic Emotion Recognition and Sentiment Analysis" takes the more balanced, citation-worthy position for Boli: categorical emotion *labels* can be a legitimate communicative convenience, but "careless endorsement of theories can lead to the perpetuation of ideas that are actively harmful (such as suggesting we can determine internal state from outward appearance — physiognomy)" ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.cl-2.1.pdf)). Paul Ekman himself — the architect of Basic Emotion Theory, the foundation most commercial emotion-AI rests on — is quoted calling what he saw in commercial emotion-recognition technology "pseudoscience" ([The Algorithmic Bridge](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/ai-emotion-recognition-is-a-pseudoscientific)), and Access Now's EU policy paper explicitly calls to prohibit emotion recognition in the AI Act ([Access Now](https://www.accessnow.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Prohibit-emotion-recognition-in-the-Artificial-Intelligence-Act.pdf)). A 2026 benchmark, VocalAffectBench, measured real-world reliability directly and found "the leading model is roughly three times the random baseline but misses more than half of the clips, with average performance across models at only 35.1%," concluding current emotion embeddings are "fundamentally unreliable and structurally biased by acoustic distractors" ([arXiv:2608.28932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28932)).

Three independent lines of evidence converge on the opposite, constructive conclusion: operational, continuous constructs are the better-justified target. First, the INTERSPEECH ComParE challenge series — the field's standard venue for defining new paralinguistics tasks since 2009 — moved progressively away from basic emotion toward conflict (2013), cognitive/physical load (2014), deception and sincerity (2016), and atypical affect (2018), an implicit field-wide retreat from categorical emotion as the most tractable target ([ComParE 2013](https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~vincia/papers/compare.pdf); [ComParE 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259889707_The_INTERSPEECH_2014_computational_paralinguistics_challenge_Cognitive_physical_load)). Second, the 2025 review itself found prosodic features showed their *greatest* accuracy for stress and cognitive load, not fine-grained discrete categories. Third, MSP-Podcast — the largest naturalistic SER corpus (409 hours, 3,641+ speakers) — annotates every turn with both categorical labels and continuous valence/arousal/dominance ratings on a 1–7 scale from at least five raters, demonstrating that the dimensional alternative is not a fringe add-on but core, quality-controlled infrastructure in the field's best corpus ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403305016_The_MSP-Podcast_Corpus); [lab-msp.com](https://lab-msp.com/MSP/MSP-Podcast.html)). The honest caveat: no single head-to-head study was found proving dimensional models generalize *better* cross-culturally than categorical ones — this is the strongest methodological bet the evidence supports, not a settled empirical fact. For Boli, the operational conclusion is unambiguous: a `fear_detected` or `anxiety_score` field is not scientifically defensible and should not exist. An `arousal_score`, `hesitation_score`, or `stress_like_acoustic_pattern` field, built from the specific features the review validated (F0, intensity, rate/duration), is defensible — provided it is stored as a continuous observation, speaker-relative where possible, and never treated as a verdict on the person's inner state.

## A thin ASR integration meets a thick acoustic literature

Boli's actual Gnani/Prisma integration was tested empirically, not assumed, and the result is a hard constraint that should shape every downstream design choice: the REST `api.vachana.ai/stt/v3` endpoint returns exactly `success`, `request_id`, `transcript`, `model`, `processing_time`, and `end_to_end_latency` — no confidence score, no word-level timestamps, no N-best alternatives, no diarization. The documented streaming endpoint (`wss://api.vachana.ai/stt/v3/stream`) adds `audio_duration_ms`, `segment_id`, `segment_index`, `latency`, and `timestamp`, plus an unitemized `raw` field whose contents are an open empirical question — but even this richer schema contains nothing resembling confidence, timing-within-word, or speaker labels. A specific "ECE 0.091" calibration figure surfaced in search results but could not be traced to any confirmed primary source and should be treated as unverified, not cited as fact. Gnani's own public glossary content discusses "confidence score" as a generic ASR concept pitched at a sales/literacy level, not as a documented field on the tested API ([Gnani STT Glossary](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/blog-speech-to-text-glossary-asr-terminology) — content per WebSearch summary, unverified against the live page). This means the general ASR literature on confidence scores, hypothesis instability ("flicker," measured via Levenshtein distance between successive streaming hypotheses) ([arXiv:2006.01416](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01416)), and overconfidence under noise — a serious concern specifically for Prisma's 8kHz telephony target regime, where other end-to-end ASR systems show "10–20% of tokens incorrectly predicted with confidence above 0.7" at -18 to -5 dB SNR ([arXiv:2509.07195](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.07195)) — is real, well-established science about ASR *correctness*, but it is not operationalizable against the API Boli actually has.

Critically, no peer-reviewed paper was found anywhere in this research that treats ASR's own behavioral output — confidence, hypothesis revision, error location — as a *direct, validated proxy for speaker-side hesitation or emotional uncertainty*, independent of the underlying audio. The closest match is a 2021 patent family describing the use of co-located "signs of potential uncertainty," including prosody and hesitation-vocalization information, to flag words a speaker was likely uncertain about ([USPTO 9685153](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9685153)) — patent-application language, not peer-reviewed research, and its signal basis is acoustic paralinguistics, not ASR-internal behavior in isolation. The adjacent SER-robustness literature studies the inverse question — how *robust* emotion recognition is *to* ASR transcription errors (it is: one 2024 study found ~12% WER had "minimal impact on emotion recognition performance compared to ground-truth transcripts," [arXiv:2406.08353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.08353)) — not whether the error pattern itself is a usable signal. Combined with the Prisma-specific finding that no confidence/instability data is actually exposed, "ASR behavior as an interaction-difficulty fingerprint" is **not currently operationalizable** with the real integration, regardless of whether the underlying research idea has scientific merit. This answers point 13 directly: Prisma's behavior layer, as integrated today, contains essentially no incremental paralinguistic information beyond the final transcript text — the honest finding is a thin, underdeveloped research area meeting an even thinner API surface. What does survive, and survives well, is text-based disfluency detection from the transcript itself: this is a mature 20+-year NLP sub-field with a canonical corpus (Switchboard), a standard annotation scheme (reparandum / interruption point / interregnum / repair), and modern BLSTM/CRF taggers reaching 85.9% F-score ([arXiv:1604.03209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.03209)) — but it depends on Prisma's `format=verbatim` mode actually preserving filler tokens with fidelity in Hindi/Indic speech, which is an empirical question on Boli's own pulled transcripts, not something resolvable from literature alone.

## The broadest defensible marker inventory, construct by construct

Pulling together the acoustic, prosodic, and voice-quality literatures yields a reasonably complete inventory of markers a passive observability layer could capture, each gradeable on what it physically measures, what it has been associated with, and how strong that association is. The table below consolidates this (points 1–4 of the brief); the narrative that follows adds the confound and dependence dimensions (points 6–9).

| Marker | What it physically measures | Constructs associated in the literature | Evidence strength/consistency |
|---|---|---|---|
| F0 (pitch) level/variability | Vocal-fold vibration rate, tracked via a pitch tracker | Arousal; anger (elevated); stress (elevated); confidence/uncertainty (low/falling = confident) | Well-established for anger/stress ([PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)); moderately supported for cognitive load; corroborated for confidence by three independent methods — corpus correlation, controlled perception synthesis, cross-population reverse correlation ([Pon-Barry 2008](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2008/ponbarry08_interspeech.pdf); [Kirkland et al. 2022](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/kirkland22_interspeech.pdf); [Goupil et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-20649-4)) |
| Intensity/loudness (RMS energy) | Sound pressure level / signal amplitude | Anger (elevated volume); stress (elevated intensity); cognitive load (elevated, less clear) | Well-established to moderately supported, consistent direction across the 2025 review for all three ([PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)) |
| Speech/articulation rate | Syllables or phones per second, with/without pauses excluded | Stress, anxiety (claimed, direction inconsistent) | Weakest timing construct: the 2025 review reports "heterogeneous" results, including direct contradictions on direction for anxiety specifically ([PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)) |
| Pause duration + pre-pause (final) lengthening | Silence duration and slowing immediately before a pause | Hesitation, uncertainty | Moderately-to-well supported; explicitly a *joint* cue (duration plus lengthening), not either alone ([AISV disfluency paper](https://www.aisv.it/StudiAISV/2017/vol_3/019_Lickley.pdf)); continuous hesitation scales show filled-pause count and vowel duration both increase with annotated hesitation degree ([Wottawa et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.190/)) |
| Turn-taking gap / response latency | Time between end of one turn and start of the next | Processing difficulty, hesitation (long gap); interruption/agitation (negative gap) | Well-established population baseline: modal gap ~200ms, replicated across ten languages ([journalofcognition.org](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.268); [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0903616106)) — strong prior for *deviation* detection |
| Filled pauses / prolongations (acoustic) | "um"/"uh"-type vocalized pausing, vowel lengthening | Uncertainty, hesitation | Moderately-to-well supported, converging across three independent studies ([Kirkland et al. 2022](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/kirkland22_interspeech.pdf); [Wottawa et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.190/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221484233_Cues_for_hesitation_in_speech_synthesis)) |
| Jitter / shimmer | Cycle-to-cycle variation in F0 period / amplitude | Generic "stress"/voice-perturbation (claimed) | Single-study/inconsistent as emotion proxies; well-established only as clinical voice-perturbation measures, and explicitly *least reliable* on the highly aperiodic voices stress is supposed to produce ([stcloudstate.edu review](https://repository.stcloudstate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?params=/context/stcloud_ling/article/1155/&path_info=1_Koffi2025ComprehensiveReviewOfJitterShimmerHNR.pdf)) |
| HNR / spectral tilt / CPP | Harmonic-to-noise ratio, harmonic amplitude decay | Breathy vs. creaky phonation type | Well-established for phonation classification specifically — spectral tilt plus periodicity explain 70–85% of variance in breathiness ratings ([ICPhS 2023](https://www.internationalphoneticassociation.org/icphs-proceedings/ICPhS2023/full_papers/772.pdf)) |
| Formants | Vocal-tract resonance peaks | Vowel/articulation identity more than affect | Moderately supported for vocal-tract characterization; weak as a pure affect marker since entangled with linguistic content ([MDPI SER review](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/4/839)) |
| MFCCs / spectral envelope | Auditory-perception-inspired spectral shape | General emotion-classifier performance | Well-established for classifier accuracy; weak as an individually-interpretable construct — a black box that works, not an explainable mechanism |
| Acoustic overlap/interruption detection | Simultaneous voice activity from two speakers | Conflict, escalation (if labeled "interruption") | Overlap detection itself is reliable; labeling it "interruption" vs. benign floor-exchange is explicitly *not* possible from acoustics alone and needs structural/semantic context ([arXiv:2603.24144](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24144v1)) |

Three cross-cutting qualifications matter more than any single row. First, the GeMAPS/eGeMAPS feature set (18/88 parameters, from Eyben et al., maintained by audEERING's openSMILE toolkit) was *selected* specifically for established theoretical links to affective physiology, and its F0/loudness descriptors are specifically validated for **arousal** (~80–93% UAR across six affective databases) and markedly weaker for **valence** ([PMC9571288](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9571288/)) — which matters because hesitation/uncertainty/stress are arousal-adjacent constructs, so this feature family is reaching for the right axis of variation for Boli's purposes, not the axis (positive/negative sentiment) where acoustic features are known to be weak. Second, openSMILE runs at a Real Time Factor of roughly 0.08 (12.5x faster than real time) even on its largest ~27,000-feature configuration ([SIGMM tutorial](https://records.sigmm.org/2015/01/05/opensmile-the-munich-open-source-large-scale-multimedia-feature-extractor-a-tutorial-for-version-2-1/)), so extracting the much smaller 18–88-feature GeMAPS/eGeMAPS set from short clips on CPU is computationally trivial — feasibility is not the bottleneck here, evidential grounding is. Third, self-supervised representations (wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, WavLM, emotion2vec) generally outperform classical features on full emotion-classification benchmarks, reaching 73–92% weighted accuracy depending on corpus ([arXiv:2111.02735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02735); [ACL emotion2vec](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.931.pdf)), but no study was found benchmarking SSL embeddings specifically against eGeMAPS on a hesitation/uncertainty task, and the active hesitation-specific literature still uses classical, explainable acoustic-prosodic features, not SSL embeddings. For a passive, auditable observability layer — one that needs to justify *why* a signal fired, not just that a classifier output a number — the explainable eGeMAPS-family features are the more epistemically honest building block than an opaque SSL embedding.

## Availability, audio dependence, and sensitivity to language, noise, and speaker

Points 5–9 of the brief ask for a systematic grading of each marker's Prisma availability/proxy status, raw-audio requirement, language/dialect dependence, noise/channel sensitivity, and speaker dependence. The table below consolidates this; the governing principle throughout is that Prisma, as integrated, exposes *nothing* that qualifies as a proxy for any of these markers beyond the bare transcript text — so "Prisma availability" below means "theoretically computable from the raw audio Boli can capture alongside the Prisma call," not "returned by the API."

| Marker | Prisma availability (hard constraint: transcript+model+timing only) | Raw audio required? | Language/dialect dependence | Noise/channel sensitivity | Speaker dependence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F0, intensity | Not exposed; requires separately captured audio | Yes | F0 cue for confidence/doubt generalizes across accents in one study, but the intensity cue does not ([SpeechProsody 2018](https://www.isca-archive.org/speechprosody_2018/jiang18_speechprosody.pdf)) | High — SNR mismatch and codec/compression each independently degrade reliability ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3498851.3499022)) | High — requires per-speaker z-score normalization; raw F0 confounds affect with sex/physiology |
| Speech/articulation rate, pauses, response latency | Approximable from transcript word timing *only if* word-level timestamps existed — they do not on the tested endpoint; otherwise requires raw audio or streaming segment timing | Partially — segment/turn timing could proxy pause structure if Prisma's `segment_id`/`timestamp` fields were exploited, but word-level timing is absent | Pausing norms are language-specific; turn-taking gap (~200ms modal) replicates across ten languages as a population baseline ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0903616106)) | Reverberation smears the amplitude envelope that rate/pause detection relies on ([PMC11384524](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11384524/)) | Moderate — speaking-task type (read vs. spontaneous) matters more than individual speaker trait |
| Filled pauses, textual disfluency | Partially available **today**, via the transcript text itself, if `format=verbatim` preserves filler tokens | No — this is the one marker family that survives a transcript-only integration | Untested for Hindi/Indic fillers specifically ("matlab," "pata nahi") — the entire Switchboard-based lineage is English-centric | Low direct sensitivity (it's post-ASR text), but inherits whatever the ASR itself dropped under noise | Language/culture-specific filler forms |
| Jitter, shimmer, HNR, spectral tilt | Not exposed; requires raw audio | Yes, always | Not separately evidenced as dialect-dependent in this review | Very high — noise directly corrupts HNR; validity "deteriorates during assessment of highly aperiodic voice signals," i.e., exactly when researchers most want to use it ([stcloudstate.edu](https://repository.stcloudstate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?params=/context/stcloud_ling/article/1155/&path_info=1_Koffi2025ComprehensiveReviewOfJitterShimmerHNR.pdf)) | Very high — confounded by loudness, F0, vowel identity, and speaker age/sex non-uniformly |
| Formants, MFCCs | Not exposed; requires raw audio | Yes | Entangled with phoneme content, hence language-dependent by construction | High — channel bandwidth and codec directly distort spectral envelope | Moderate |
| Acoustic overlap/interruption, diarization-derived turn metrics | Not exposed — no diarization feature documented on Prisma's STT endpoints at any tier found | Yes, for overlap detection itself; "interruption" labeling additionally needs non-acoustic structural/semantic context | Turn-taking structure (TRPs) is theorized as near-universal, but no direct cross-linguistic transfer study was found | Reverberation also degrades speaker-ID/diarization robustness, compounding the problem for any longitudinal use | High — overlap vs. backchannel ambiguity requires per-speaker/per-culture calibration |
| ASR confidence / hypothesis instability | **Not available at all** on the tested endpoint — confirmed empirically, not speculated | N/A (would be ASR-internal, not audio) | Unknown | Known to become *overconfident* under low SNR in other E2E ASR systems ([arXiv:2509.07195](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.07195)) — directly relevant to Prisma's 8kHz telephony target | Unknown |

The pattern that falls out of this table is not subtle: every marker that requires raw audio is currently unavailable from the Prisma integration as tested, and the one marker family that *is* available today — textual disfluency surviving in the transcript — is also the one with the most mature, most portable methodology (Switchboard-style reparandum/repair tagging), but its specific transfer to Hindi/Indic verbatim transcripts is an untested, empirical, and cheap-to-run first step for Boli rather than a literature question.

## The adjacent-subsystem gradient: diarization beats VAD beats noise estimation

The brief specifically asked whether any Prisma-adjacent subsystem — VAD, diarization, speaker/channel modeling, noise estimation — carries incidental paralinguistic signal even though its original purpose was ASR engineering, not behavioral inference. The audit of this question found a genuine, reportable gradient rather than a uniform "yes" or "no." At one end, **diarization and turn-taking** have the richest connecting literature: overlapping speech is reported as "strongly correlated with high conflict levels" in a 2013 Interspeech study on automatic conflict detection via speaker overlap ([ISCA Archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2013/grezes13_interspeech.pdf)), and call-center conversation-analytic work finds conflict "mirrored in the prosodic features of the interaction." The caveat is structural, not fatal: raw overlap detection cannot by itself distinguish a true interruption from benign floor-exchange or backchannel — turn-taking literature explicitly defines interruption by whether a Transition Relevance Place was imminent and by communicative intent, not by acoustic simultaneity alone ([arXiv:2603.24144](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24144v1)) — so any exposed overlap metric would need intent-aware modeling layered on top before it could be labeled "conflict" rather than merely "simultaneous speech."

**VAD** sits in the middle. It is a narrow, well-defined binary per-frame speech/non-speech classifier, and the general literature connects VAD-adjacent features (pause count/length, speech rate, hesitation ratio) to disfluency and clinical state; one escalation-detection paper explicitly uses VAD output as a feature inside a purpose-built classifier, reporting 81.5% unweighted average recall versus a 72.2% baseline ([arXiv:2104.06004](https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/arxiv:2104.06004)). But this is "VAD as an ingredient inside a trained classifier," not "VAD output alone as a freestanding incidental signal" — and nothing found documents Prisma exposing VAD-adjacent output beyond the streaming endpoint's `segment_id`/`audio_duration_ms` fields, whose relationship to actual speech/silence boundaries is inferred, not documented. At the far end, **noise/SNR/signal-quality estimation** is the clearest "no" in this entire audit: SNR estimation is well-established as an internal technique for predicting ASR word-error-rate and gating recording usability, and Gnani explicitly markets a "denoise-first streaming pipeline" implying such an estimate exists internally ([Gnani technology page](https://www.gnani.ai/technology)) — but no literature anywhere in this research repurposes signal-quality/SNR metrics as a behavioral or interaction-state proxy. This is the one subsystem where the underlying engineering signal has essentially no documented secondary life as a paralinguistic marker; the plausible-sounding idea that very poor audio quality might correlate with a caller's distressing environment is pure, unsupported speculation. Separately, and worth flagging as the single most concrete piece of infrastructure discovered in this audit: Gnani operates **Armour365**, a wholly separate voice-biometrics product that builds persistent, encrypted per-speaker voiceprints from "300+ proprietary audio features" with sub-2% Equal Error Rate ([IoT For All](https://www.iotforall.com/news/gnani.ai-launches-armour365--voice-biometrics-software-based-on-patented-technologies)) — demonstrating that the organizational and technical capacity for speaker-relative, longitudinal infrastructure already exists at Gnani, even though it is architecturally unconnected to Prisma's ASR engine today and no evidence was found of the two being shared on any backend.

Beyond the four named subsystems, the unexplored research space worth flagging is exactly where this gradient points: a purpose-built study correlating diarization-derived turn/overlap statistics with independently-labeled interaction difficulty in a conversational-AI (not dyadic human) context; a direct test of whether Prisma's streaming `raw` field contains nested confidence or timing data not itemized in public documentation (a concrete, low-effort empirical check Boli can run itself rather than a literature question); and — the clearest gap of all — no vendor-neutral survey was found that systematically catalogs "secondary behavioral uses of ASR-adjacent engineering signals" as a named research area, unlike the well-surveyed field of speech emotion recognition itself. That absence is itself a finding: this is a genuinely under-surveyed niche, not a solved one.

## Designing the passive schema: fields, not verdicts

The literature converges on a specific schema philosophy, not just a feature list. Three structural lessons should govern the design over any single fixed set of named buckets. First, low-level descriptors and inferred constructs must be stored as *distinct, separately versioned* layers — eGeMAPS-style parameters (F0 stats, loudness stats, jitter, shimmer, HNR, spectral tilt, pause/rate timing) are the raw observation layer, and any continuous construct score (arousal_score, hesitation_score, stress_like_pattern_score, cognitive_load_proxy_score) is a derived, model-dependent summary that must carry its own provenance — which features fed it, what evidence tier backed the association (anger/stress-tier "well-established," cognitive-load-tier "partially consistent," fear/anxiety-tier "do not expose"), and a channel-quality gate flag, since SNR, codec, clipping, and reverberation all directly corrupt the underlying descriptors before any construct score is computed ([PMC11384524](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11384524/); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3498851.3499022)). The right design is a *gating* problem, not a correction problem: estimate channel quality first, and suppress or down-weight a construct score's confidence under poor-channel conditions rather than attempting to mathematically "correct for" channel effects after the fact.

Second, every feature or score should carry an explicit `source_modality` tag — `raw_audio` versus `asr_text_proxy` — because Boli's current Prisma integration makes the text-proxy path (disfluency markers surviving in the verbatim transcript) the only one actually populated today, while every acoustic-feature field remains schema-defined but empty until raw audio capture is added. This prevents silent conflation of "we measured this" with "we could theoretically measure this." Third, and most importantly, speaker-relative fields deserve their own namespace, separate from absolute/population values: a `baseline_mean`, `baseline_variance`, `baseline_n` (sample count backing the baseline), and `z_score_vs_self` per feature, following the established clinical-monitoring and speaker-verification pattern of z-score normalization against a speaker's own historical distribution (F0_norm = (f − μ_speaker)/σ_speaker) ([arXiv:2111.10168](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.10168)) — this directly addresses the gender/age/accent confounds documented throughout the acoustic literature by design, rather than requiring a separate correction model.

Given these three lessons, the brief's suggested field groupings — `acoustic_features`, `prosodic_features`, `ASR_behavior_features`, `disfluency_features`, `interaction_features`, `candidate_paralinguistic_dimensions`, `confidence_uncertainty` — are a reasonable starting taxonomy but should be adjusted in two ways rather than hard-coded as-is. `ASR_behavior_features` should be renamed or merged away: given the confirmed absence of any ASR-internal metadata from Prisma, a dedicated bucket for it invites a false impression of availability; it is better represented as an empty/future-reserved namespace explicitly tagged "not currently populated — no Prisma field exists" than as a populated-looking schema section. And `candidate_paralinguistic_dimensions` should be split into two tiers that mirror the 2025 review's own gradient: a `validated_dimensions` tier (arousal_score, anger_like_score, stress_like_score, cognitive_load_proxy — each carrying an evidence_tier field) and a strictly separate, clearly-labeled `research_only_dimensions` tier for anything weaker (e.g., valence, which the eGeMAPS literature itself flags as having "no strong discriminative speech features" to support it) — with no tier, ever, for fear/anxiety as a product-facing field. Every record, regardless of tier, is an observation with a timestamp, a confidence/evidence tag, and a provenance chain — never an action trigger, never a field with write access from any downstream decision system.

## Longitudinal speaker-relative tracking is extrapolation by analogy

The brief flagged this as a genuine gap, and the research confirms it precisely: the *method* of within-speaker baselining is well-precedented in adjacent fields, but its *direct application to affect or interaction-state tracking* has not been validated in anything found in this research. Within-patient longitudinal speech monitoring in clinical contexts (Multiple Sclerosis progression, COPD screening) is explicitly framed around "each patient serv[ing] as their own control, holding age, sex, anatomy, and habitual voice quality fixed by construction" ([arXiv:2608.21599](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21599)), with changepoint-detection algorithms invoked to flag when a speaker's vocal characteristics shift over time ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S221103482400467X)). Speaker-verification research independently validates the normalization half of this method: adaptive score normalization using a per-speaker cohort for mean/variance computation is standard practice there too. A dedicated paper, "Speaker Embeddings as Individuality Proxy for Voice Stress Detection," reports that concatenating pretrained audio representations with speaker embeddings (Resemblyzer, ECAPA) and using them as a personalized baseline "significantly improves voice stress detection" compared to speaker-agnostic models ([arXiv:2306.05915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05915)) — the single closest literature analog to Boli's speaker-relative ambition, though it is a deliberately-designed personalization scheme, not a demonstration of long-term longitudinal tracking across many calls over time.

What a defensible longitudinal design would look like, built by analogy from these adjacent fields rather than from any directly-validated affect-tracking precedent: maintain a rolling per-speaker distribution (mean, variance, sample count) for each core feature (F0 level/variability, intensity, rate, pause structure), update it incrementally as new calls accumulate, require a minimum sample count before any z-score is trusted (early calls should report low-confidence or null rather than a noisy baseline), and run changepoint detection against the rolling baseline to flag durable shifts rather than single-call noise — exactly the architecture the clinical-monitoring literature uses for disease progression, repurposed here for interaction state. Two honesty requirements follow directly from the evidence already surfaced. First, the baseline itself is channel-dependent: because reverberation and SNR drift also degrade speaker-ID/diarization robustness, a baseline built from inconsistent channel conditions risks being contaminated by channel variation rather than purely speaker-state variation, so baseline updates should themselves carry a channel-quality gate. Second, and this should be stated plainly rather than implied: **this entire longitudinal design is extrapolation by analogy, not a directly validated finding for affect/interaction-state tracking.** It should be built, tested, and reported as a hypothesis-generating instrument — "this speaker's arousal-adjacent features have shifted relative to their own six-month baseline" is a defensible, falsifiable observation; "this speaker is becoming more anxious over time" is not, because it smuggles back in exactly the categorical-emotion leap the 2025 review's fear/anxiety finding warns against.

## The experiment that must come before any product claim

Before any construct score graduates from "stored observation" to anything resembling a trusted signal, the brief's requested comparison — audio-only vs. Prisma-only vs. transcript-only vs. audio+Prisma vs. audio+Prisma+transcript — needs to be run as a controlled ablation, and the literature gives a clear expectation of what each condition should and should not be able to do. Prisma-only (confidence/instability metadata) is **currently a null condition** given the confirmed absence of such fields — this comparison arm should be explicitly logged as "not testable with current integration" rather than silently dropped, since re-testing it the moment Gnani's documented `raw` streaming field or an enterprise tier is inspected is one of the cheapest, highest-value follow-ups identified in this research. Transcript-only (textual disfluency markers) should perform respectably on hesitation/uncertainty specifically, given the mature Switchboard-lineage evidence, but is expected to be weak on arousal/stress/anger, where the evidence base is overwhelmingly acoustic (F0, intensity) rather than lexical. Audio-only should be the strongest single-modality arm for arousal-adjacent constructs (anger-like, stress-like, cognitive-load-proxy), consistent with the 2025 review's own findings, but should be evaluated specifically on its ability to *not* produce a usable fear/anxiety signal — a null result there is not a failure, it is a replication of the review's own conclusion and should be reported as evidence the system is behaving correctly, not evidence the feature needs more work. Audio+transcript (no Prisma-internal metadata, since there is none to add) is the practically achievable ceiling for Boli today, and should be benchmarked against audio-only to quantify whether textual disfluency markers add incremental value once acoustic features are already present — a test this research did not find already run anywhere in the literature.

Two design requirements make this experiment scientifically meaningful rather than a vanity benchmark. First, **held-out speakers and held-out languages are mandatory**, not optional, given the repeatedly-documented cross-speaker and cross-lingual generalization failures in this literature: SER accuracy drops substantially cross-lingually even for SSL models, humans retain a relative advantage over models in the cross-lingual setting, and a concrete, citable example exists of a regional UK accent being systematically misclassified as more severe cognitive decline by an adjacent speech-based clinical model ([arXiv:2409.16920](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.16920); accent-bias cognitive-decline finding per search synthesis). Given that Indian-language SER resources are fragmented and small (tens of hours, not hundreds) relative to English-centric corpora like MSP-Podcast's 409 hours, any construct score trained or validated primarily on Western data should be assumed structurally likely to misfire on Indian-accented or code-switched speech until proven otherwise on held-out Indian-language/dialect speakers specifically. Second, the brief's instinct to test **longitudinal within-speaker change against absolute cross-speaker classification** is the single most promising experimental axis this research surfaced, precisely because it is the axis with the least direct precedent (see the prior section) and the most to gain: if a speaker-relative z-score reliably outperforms a population-absolute threshold on held-out speakers — which the stress-detection personalization literature suggests it should ([arXiv:2306.05915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05915)) — that is a genuinely novel, defensible contribution for Boli to claim, rather than a restatement of existing SER benchmarks.

## Passive observability now; outcome research later, offline, governed

Everything in this report describes a capture → quantify → store → validate pipeline, and that boundary is architectural, not incidental. A passive observability layer writes continuous, provenance-tagged, speaker-relative observations to a store; it does not read from that store to approve, reject, escalate, prioritize, or otherwise change what happens next in a conversation. No field described above — arousal_score, hesitation_score, stress_like_pattern_score, or any future addition — should have a code path that lets it influence routing, agent tone, retry logic, or any other runtime decision at this stage. This is not a conservative hedge; it is the only posture the evidence supports, given that even the best-evidenced constructs in the 2025 review (anger, stress) are described by the review's own authors as showing the field's "greatest accuracy" while the overarching review-level conclusion is still that "research remains inconsistent in identifying reliable acoustic markers, providing limited guidance for researchers and practitioners" ([PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)). Acting on a signal this uncertain — even a well-evidenced one — conflates "measurable" with "actionable," and the fear/anxiety null finding is the clearest possible warning against assuming the gap between those two is small.

Any future question of whether these signals *predict outcomes* — conversation abandonment, escalation, churn, or especially high-consequence outcomes like financial default or creditworthiness — is a categorically separate, later, offline research problem, and must be treated as such explicitly rather than folded into this capture layer's scope. That later work would require out-of-sample validation on data never seen during feature or threshold design; calibration analysis (not just discrimination/accuracy, since a model can rank-order well while still being badly miscalibrated in absolute terms); temporal validation (does a signal that correlated with an outcome six months ago still correlate today, given documented drift in channel conditions, codecs, and speaker populations); cross-language, cross-dialect, cross-channel, and cross-speaker-demographic testing at a scale this research could not find any existing Indian-language SER corpus large enough to support with confidence; and a fairness, legal, and consent/governance review before any such correlation is operationalized — precisely because the same accent/dialect confounds documented throughout this report (the South Yorkshire-accent-to-cognitive-decline misclassification being the starkest cited example) make it entirely plausible that a naively-built outcome model would encode demographic bias as "risk." No automated action — approval, denial, pricing, escalation, or prioritization — should ever be taken from inferred affective or interaction state, and this is categorically truer, not less true, for high-consequence financial or legal outcomes than for routine conversational ones. The capture layer this report describes should be built to make that later research *possible to do responsibly* — by preserving provenance, evidence tiers, and speaker-relative context — not to make it *easy to skip*.

## Conclusion

The evidence assembled here supports a specific, narrow, and defensible claim: speech contains real information about arousal-adjacent interaction state, that information is best captured as continuous operational constructs rather than discrete emotions, and Boli's own Prisma integration currently makes almost none of the richest audio-level signal available — which means the nearest-term, highest-leverage work is not a smarter classifier but closing the gap between what the literature says is measurable and what the actual pipeline captures (raw audio alongside Prisma, Indic-language verbatim-transcript fidelity testing, and a speaker-relative baseline store). The most genuinely novel opportunity this research surfaced is not any single marker but the combination the literature itself has not yet tried: a diarization/turn-taking-informed, speaker-baselined, continuously-scored interaction-state layer, built on India-specific telephony audio, evaluated against held-out speakers and languages rather than within-corpus accuracy — a combination that sits at the intersection of three separately mature literatures (acoustic paralinguistics, within-speaker clinical monitoring, and turn-taking/conflict detection) that, as far as this research could determine, nobody has yet fused into one system. Building that system honestly requires accepting what the evidence rules out as much as what it permits: fear and anxiety do not have a defensible acoustic signature, Prisma's "behavior" is presently just a transcript, and any claim to predict real-world consequential outcomes from these signals is a wholly separate, much harder, and much more carefully governed research program than simply recording what the voice is doing.
