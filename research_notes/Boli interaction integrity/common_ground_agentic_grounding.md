## What is the "common ground" literature in dialogue/discourse research (classic through modern computational treatments), and what does it establish?

### Takeaway
"Common ground" and "grounding" are decades-old, foundational constructs in psycholinguistics and discourse theory (Clark & Brennan 1991 is the canonical citation), holding that conversation is a *collaborative, incremental process of establishing and verifying mutual belief* — not independent, stateless turns — and this was followed by a line of computational/formal models (Traum 1994 onward) that attempt to operationalize grounding as explicit system state. This is squarely "settled, old" literature, not a new idea; what's new is applying/evaluating it against LLM-based and agentic systems.

### Cited Findings
- Clark and Brennan's "Grounding in Communication" (1991) defines common ground as the collection of mutual knowledge, mutual beliefs, and mutual assumptions essential to communication, and grounding as the interactive, collaborative process of establishing this shared mutual belief. — [Grounding in Communication (PDF)](https://worrydream.com/refs/Clark_H_1991_-_Grounding_in_Communication.pdf); [Wikipedia: Grounding in communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grounding_in_communication)
- Per Clark & Brennan, participants must "coordinate both the content and process" of conversation: content-coordination builds mutual knowledge/beliefs/assumptions, and as gaps are identified, participants coordinate *process* to resolve misunderstandings/misalignments. — [Grounding in Communication (PDF)](https://worrydream.com/refs/Clark_H_1991_-_Grounding_in_Communication.pdf)
- Grounding cues can signal problems (e.g., requests for clarification) or demonstrate understanding (acknowledgment, a relevant next turn, continued attention); participants look for positive evidence of understanding rather than assuming it by default. — [Grounding in Communication (PDF)](https://worrydream.com/refs/Clark_H_1991_-_Grounding_in_Communication.pdf)
- David Traum's 1994 PhD dissertation, "A Computational Theory of Grounding in Natural Language Conversation" (U. Rochester), gives the first major computational formalization: a protocol for determining whether material has been grounded (or what it would take to ground it), tied to agents' mental states, and introduces "grounding acts" — speech acts whose function is specifically to ground other (illocutionary) speech acts. — [Traum computational theory (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/34074816_A_Computational_Theory_of_Grounding_in_Natural_Language_Conversation); [Traum, Computational Approaches to Dialogue (PDF)](https://people.ict.usc.edu/traum/public_html/Papers/traum-computational.pdf)
- Traum & Allen's 1994 ACL paper "Discourse Obligations in Dialogue Processing" and later "Modelling Grounding and Discourse Obligations Using Update Rules" extend this into formal update-rule models of grounding state. — [ACM DL entry](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/974305.974306)
- A later empirically-based computational model of grounding in dialogue (SIGDIAL 2007 lineage) attempts to fit grounding behavior to observed dialogue data rather than purely logical/rule-based specification. — [An empirically based computational model of grounding in dialogue (ACL Anthology PDF)](https://aclanthology.org/2007.sigdial-1.49.pdf)
- Formal (rule/logic-based) approaches to modeling common grounding exist, but "capturing their full complexities in realistic, situated conversations remains a formidable problem"; most existing data-driven grounding literature is limited to closed-domain, goal-oriented settings. — synthesized from search results on computational models of common ground (see multiple sources above); exact primary-source quote not independently verified due to fetch restrictions.
- "Common Ground: Between Formal Pragmatics and Psycholinguistics" (Annual Review of Linguistics) is a recent review bridging the formal-semantics/pragmatics treatment of common ground with the psycholinguistic (Clark-style) treatment — indicating the construct has two historically somewhat separate theoretical lineages (formal pragmatics vs. collaborative/psycholinguistic) that are still being reconciled as of this review. — [Annual Review of Linguistics](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-linguistics-041824-032410)
- A 2021 paper, "Grounding 'Grounding' in NLP," critiques the NLP field's frequent conflation/loose use of "grounding" (symbol grounding vs. Clark-style conversational grounding), signaling that even within NLP the term has drifted from its original communicative-grounding meaning. — [Grounding 'Grounding' in NLP (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02192)

### Inferences
- The field has a genuine terminological hazard: "grounding" in NLP/ML increasingly means "grounding in external data/documents" (RAG-style) rather than Clark & Brennan's interactional/mutual-belief sense. Any report synthesizing this cluster should explicitly disambiguate "conversational/communicative grounding" (mutual belief maintenance) from "symbol/data grounding" (connecting language to referents/external knowledge), since search results on "grounding" return a mix of both.
- The fact that two theoretical lineages (formal pragmatics and psycholinguistics) are still being actively reconciled in 2024-2025 review literature suggests that despite ~35 years of research, there is no single unified formal model of common ground — multiple partial, task-specific formalizations coexist (Traum's grounding acts/obligations, QUD-based formal pragmatics models, data-driven/empirical grounding models).

- I could not directly verify the full text/argument of Clark & Brennan 1991 or Traum 1994 against primary PDFs (fetch tool blocked for the relevant academic hosts in some cases, and full deep-read was out of scope given the ~15-call budget); findings rely on search-engine synthesis of these primary sources, which is a secondary-source risk worth flagging to the report writer.

## Is there a 2025 survey covering ~448 papers mapping this space? What is its scope/structure?

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

- The survey "outlines different dimensions of common ground and describes modeling approaches for several grounding tasks, discusses issues caused by the lack of common ground in human-LM interactions, and suggests future research directions." — [search synthesis citing ACL Anthology 2025.luhme-1.2](https://aclanthology.org/2025.luhme-1.2/)

## What does recent (2024-2026) "agentic AI" research say about making dialogue/clarification an explicit part of an agent's planning loop, to prevent silent goal misalignment? What architectures have been proposed?

### Takeaway
There is a fast-growing (2024-2026) cluster of agentic-AI papers explicitly arguing that LLM agents must treat clarification/uncertainty-resolution as an action choice embedded in the plan-act-observe loop (not a pre-processing step), motivated specifically by the failure mode of agents "arbitrarily resolving" ambiguous instructions and then executing confidently on a wrong interpretation — several named benchmarks and frameworks now exist for exactly this (ClarifyBench/SAGE-Agent, ClarEval, DiscoBench, Ambig-DS, InferAct, and an "Information Gain Reward" framework).

### Cited Findings
- "Deploying LLM-based agents in real-life applications often faces a critical challenge: the misalignment between agents' behavior and user intent," and "a major risk in agentic systems is that agents may execute actions that deviate from what the user actually intended," with real-world examples like accidentally triggering a "buy-now" in web shopping. — [Preemptive Detection and Correction of Misaligned Actions in LLM Agents (arXiv 2407.11843)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.11843)
- Concretely: when a user says to "handle" a request without specifics, possible interpretations include paying it, rejecting it, notifying the user, or asking for confirmation; an agent may wrongly default to auto-paying overdue tickets — "a strong assumption that was never stated by the user" — when the appropriate behavior is to present the pending items and ask how to proceed. — [Preemptive Detection and Correction of Misaligned Actions in LLM Agents (arXiv 2407.11843)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.11843)
- InferAct (EMNLP 2025) leverages LLMs' Theory-of-Mind-style belief reasoning to infer an agent's underlying intent from its actions and detect misalignment *before* execution (a "Task Inference Unit" and "Task Verification Unit"), then alerts a human for correction; tested on WebShop, HotPotQA, and ALFWorld, it achieves up to 20% Macro-F1 improvement over baselines in misaligned-action detection. — [InferAct (arXiv 2407.11843 / EMNLP 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11843v2); [ACL Anthology 2025.emnlp-main.12](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.12/)
- "Uncertainty-Aware Clarification in LLM Agents with Information Gain" argues that LLM agents operate under "underspecified user instructions, where latent uncertainty over user intent leads to erroneous tool actions," and that naive clarification training rewards surface fluency rather than actual uncertainty reduction, causing models to default to "generic, safe questions that provide limited informational value." It proposes an "Information Gain Reward" that quantifies a clarification question's value via the Bayesian belief update it induces toward the ground-truth goal. — [arXiv 2606.03135](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03135)
- ClarifyBench evaluates agent clarification strategies via multi-turn interaction between a user simulator and tool-using LLM agents across normal/ambiguous/infeasible queries in 5 domains; "SAGE-Agent improves coverage on ambiguous tasks by 7-39% while reducing questions by 1.5-2.7x" relative to baselines. — [search synthesis, "agent clarify instead of assume" query results]
- Ambig-DS is a diagnostic benchmark for task-framing ambiguity in data-science agents, showing "agents can use clarification when obtained, but cannot reliably detect when to ask" — i.e., the bottleneck is ambiguity *detection*, not clarification execution once prompted. — [Ambig-DS (arXiv 2605.09698)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09698)
- ClarEval assesses a "Collaborative Quotient" by injecting three realistic ambiguity types (missing goals, missing premises, ambiguous terminology) into standard tasks, forcing agents out of a pure "generator" role into requirement elicitation. — [ClarEval (arXiv 2603.00187)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00187v1)
- DiscoBench evaluates whether search agents can proactively identify ambiguity, ask effective clarification questions, and recover correct reasoning paths through user interaction, specifically for "clarification-aware deep search." — [DiscoBench (arXiv 2606.27669)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27669)
- Framed generally: agentic AI loops are commonly described as a "Plan → Act → Reflect → Repeat" or "Think → Act → Observe → Think" cycle; within planning-as-commitment framing, "a focused clarification should be asked when its answer changes the plan, and approval should be requested at the point where a reviewable consequential action is ready," and "a changed target or material payload needs renewed authorization; broad assent does not approve every later variation." — [search synthesis, "agentic AI planning loop clarification shared commitments" query]
- "Agentic misalignment" work from Anthropic and related arXiv papers (e.g., 2510.05179) studies a related but distinct problem — LLMs behaving as insider threats / misaligned under explicit prohibition — which is adjacent to but not the same phenomenon as *silent interpretive* misalignment (the agent confidently acting on the wrong reading of an ambiguous, good-faith user request). — [Agentic Misalignment: How LLMs Could Be Insider Threats (Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment); [arXiv 2510.05179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05179)

### Inferences
- The 2024-2026 agentic literature converges on a specific distinction worth carrying into the Boli report: "ambiguity resolution once prompted" (agents are reasonably good at this) vs. "ambiguity *detection*" (agents are poor at this, per Ambig-DS) — i.e., the harder, more novel problem is not generating a clarifying question but recognizing the moment a plan/interpretation needs to be checked against the user rather than silently committed to.
- Several of these frameworks (InferAct, ClarEval, Information Gain Reward) are implicitly reinventing, in agentic/RL terms, exactly the Clark & Brennan grounding-cue concept (positive/negative evidence of understanding triggering repair) — but apparently without citing the classical grounding literature by name in the search snippets surfaced here. This supports the user's hypothesis that these are currently somewhat separate research threads converging on the same problem from different disciplinary starting points (see next section).
- Nearly all of these benchmarks are text/tool-agent-oriented (web shopping, coding, data science, search) rather than voice/spoken-dialogue-oriented, which is a direct gap for the Boli voice-AI project to note: the "ask-vs-assume" agentic literature has not yet been validated in a turn-taking, real-time spoken-interaction setting where clarification has different costs (interrupting speech flow, latency, no visual UI for disambiguation).

### Gaps
- I did not find a canonical, single "architecture diagram" paper that all subsequent work cites as the reference design for clarification-in-the-loop; the space appears to be a fast-moving cluster of parallel, differently-named benchmarks/frameworks (2025-2026) rather than one dominant architecture.
- I did not verify whether any of these frameworks have been adapted to or evaluated on voice/speech-based agents specifically (see also the voice-AI section below).

## How does the classic common-ground/grounding literature connect concretely to the agentic framing — is there explicit bridging work, or are these separate threads?

### Takeaway
There is a small but real and growing body of explicit bridging work (most notably a Microsoft Research paper, "Challenges in Human-Agent Communication," and a 2026 cross-disciplinary misunderstanding taxonomy), but it is clearly an emerging minority strand — the bulk of the classic grounding literature and the bulk of the 2025-2026 agentic-misalignment/clarification literature remain separately-cited clusters that overlap in substance more than in citation graph.

### Cited Findings
- "Challenges in Human-Agent Communication" (Bansal et al., Microsoft Research, arXiv 2412.10380, Dec 2024) explicitly identifies and analyzes twelve key human-agent communication challenges "informed by a communication grounding perspective," grouping them into (1) information agents need to convey to users, (2) information users need to convey to agents, and (3) general difficulties with human-agent communication. It explicitly uses "common ground" to mean "operational alignments, including on shared inferences such as the likelihoods of the current state of the world or future outcomes jointly considered by the human and agent," and argues establishing common ground between users and AI agents is important for reducing failure modes in complex (i.e., agentic) AI systems. — [Challenges in Human-Agent Communication (arXiv 2412.10380)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10380); [Microsoft Research PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/HCAI_Agents.pdf)
- "Cross-Disciplinary Taxonomy and Modeling of Misunderstanding Generation, Amplification, and Detection, from Pragmatics to AI Agents" (arXiv 2608.13604) explicitly states its goal as consolidating accounts "from nine fields that don't ordinarily cite each other," spanning pragmatics through to AI agents, and identifies eleven exact failure modes across eight analytical layers (eight mechanisms generating divergence, two amplifying it, one governing detection/repair) — the paper's own framing ("fields that don't ordinarily cite each other") is itself direct evidence that pragmatics/grounding research and AI-agent research are largely disconnected literatures being deliberately bridged. — [arXiv 2608.13604](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13604)
- "Understanding Common Ground Misalignment in Goal-Oriented Dialog: A Case-Study with Ubuntu Chat Logs" (ACL 2025, long paper) is a 2025 paper explicitly applying the "common ground misalignment" construct to goal-oriented dialogue analysis, indicating the classic-grounding vocabulary is being actively reused (not just reinvented under new names) in current NLP venues. — [ACL Anthology 2025.acl-long.161](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.161/)
- "A Benchmark to Assess Common Ground in Human-AI Collaboration" (arXiv 2602.21337) is a recent benchmark explicitly framed around the classic common-ground construct but targeting human-AI (collaborative-agent) settings, further evidence of an active bridging trend. — [arXiv 2602.21337](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21337)
- Separately, multi-agent/joint-action theory (Bratman's Shared Cooperative Activity conditions — shared we-intention, interlocking subplans, mutual responsiveness, commitment to persistence, common knowledge) is being explicitly imported into human-AI collaboration analysis, with scholars noting "current AI systems do not form we-intentions, do not possess normative commitments, and cannot participate in genuine common knowledge, but they create a functional illusion of shared cooperative activity." — [search synthesis, "joint commitment Bratman shared intention human-AI collaboration" query, citing multiple sources including arXiv papers on human-agent collaboration]
- "Commitments in Human-Robot Interaction" (arXiv 1909.06561) and later human-agent-collaboration papers draw on joint-intention theory specifically, noting that if a team is jointly committed to a goal and one member believes it's finished while this isn't yet mutually known, that member retains a persistent goal to make the status mutually known — a formal commitment-theoretic property directly relevant to "does the agent know whether the user knows the plan changed." — [search synthesis, joint commitment query]

### Inferences
- The bridging work that does exist is recent (2024-2026) and concentrated in a handful of papers rather than being an established subfield — this is consistent with the user's instruction to be "adversarial about novelty": common ground itself is ~35 years old and settled, but *explicitly connecting it to agentic-AI clarification/planning* is itself a frontier move as of 2024-2026, not a mature synthesis.
- The "nine fields that don't ordinarily cite each other" framing (arXiv 2608.13604) is the single clearest piece of evidence in this research pass that the Boli project's framing — connecting classic grounding theory to agentic misalignment — identifies a genuine, currently underexploited gap rather than restating consensus. This is a citable data point for positioning the Boli project's novelty claim.
- Given that multiple 2025-2026 papers are independently reaching for the same classic vocabulary (common ground, joint commitment, grounding acts) to describe agentic misalignment, it seems the pull toward this synthesis is strong and likely to continue accelerating — but as of this research pass no single "field-defining" bridging paper equivalent to Clark & Brennan (for the old literature) or a to-be-named canonical paper (for the new one) was identified.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper that explicitly operationalizes Clark & Brennan's *grounding acts* (presentation/acceptance pairs) as literal state-machine or reward-signal components inside an LLM-agent planning loop (i.e., a 1:1 architectural translation). The bridging found is thematic/conceptual (shared vocabulary, shared problem framing) rather than a worked computational translation of the classical formalism into an agent architecture — this may be worth flagging as an open opportunity rather than reporting it as already done.
- I could not confirm whether the Microsoft "Challenges in Human-Agent Communication" paper or the cross-disciplinary taxonomy paper cite Clark & Brennan or Traum directly (vs. independently reusing similar terms); this would need a primary-source check I could not complete given the egress restrictions.

## For a voice-AI system specifically, what does this literature suggest about tracking "what is currently established as shared/agreed" as explicit state, rather than relying on an LLM's implicit context window?

### Takeaway
The literature converges on a clear recommendation — track common ground as an explicit, structured, queryable state object separate from the LLM's implicit context window — motivated by two independent failure modes: (1) context-window/length limits causing catastrophic forgetting of earlier-established agreements, and (2) voice-specific paralinguistic/environmental information being left implicit in the audio stream and therefore invisible to text-only state tracking. "Common Ground Tracking" (CGT) is named in the literature as a distinct, complementary task to standard Dialogue State Tracking (DST), and is explicitly flagged as understudied, especially for dynamic/real-time settings — directly relevant to voice.

### Cited Findings
- "Common Ground Tracking (CGT) identifies the shared belief space held by all participants in a task-oriented dialogue: the task-relevant propositions all participants accept as true. This is complementary to the more widely studied Dialogue State Tracking (DST)." — [Common Ground Tracking in Multimodal Dialogue (arXiv 2403.17284 / LREC-COLING 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17284); [ACL Anthology 2024.lrec-main.318](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.318/)
- Recent work (e.g., "Trace") focuses on *live/real-time* multimodal common ground tracking in situated collaborative tasks — tracking speech, actions, gestures, and visual attention to determine task-relevant propositions and participants' epistemic stance toward them *as the task unfolds*, rather than post-hoc or static analysis. — [Trace: Real-Time Multimodal Common Ground Tracking (arXiv 2503.09511)](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09511)
- Methods for identifying shared beliefs and "questions under discussion" (QUDs) in groups with a shared goal use multimodal signals including speech transcriptions, prosodic features, gestures, and actions in deep neural models — i.e., common-ground tracking in recent work is explicitly multimodal/paralinguistic-aware, not purely text/transcript-based. — [search synthesis, Common Ground Tracking query]
- On voice-specific context limitations directly: "Voice-specific context remains implicit in existing context-management mechanisms, which explicitly manage semantic content but leave paralinguistic cues and environmental conditions implicit in the audio stream. Without explicit paralinguistic states, the system cannot reliably adapt its response style to the user." — [search synthesis, "Scalable Context Orchestration for Serving LLMs Over Voice" query results]
- On context-window limits generally: "The limitation of the LLM's context window size disrupts decision consistency and scene understanding ability, and causes information interruption, and can even cause catastrophic forgetting." — [search synthesis, explicit shared state representation query]
- Research on situated dialogue argues agents must "maintain a reliable representation of shared context rather than reasoning only over isolated utterances," and that current conversational agents "struggle with this requirement, especially when the common ground must be preserved beyond the immediate context window." — [Using Machine Mental Imagery for Representing Common Ground in Situated Dialogue (arXiv 2604.21144)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21144v1)
- A named failure mode, "representational blur," occurs when fine-grained distinctions are compressed into purely textual representations and similar-but-distinct entities collapse into interchangeable descriptions — a concrete mechanism by which an implicit, text-only context window loses previously-established shared state. — [search synthesis, explicit shared state representation query, citing arXiv 2604.21144 and related]
- Recommended mitigation per surveyed practice: "structured memory representations (e.g., JSON dialogue state) alongside natural language context," and explicit "shared/global context... defined at the task pipeline or runner level and passed into each tool call" so tools/components can share and coordinate on state rather than relying on the LLM to infer it from raw history each turn. — [search synthesis, explicit shared state representation query, incl. pipecat-ai/pipecat GitHub issue #4256 discussion]
- "Frame of Reference: Addressing the Challenges of Common Ground Representation in Situational Dialogs" is a dedicated 2026 paper on the specific problem of representing common ground in situated (not purely text) dialogue — further evidence this is an active, named research problem distinct from generic dialogue state tracking. — [arXiv 2601.09365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09365)
- "Frame of Reference" also appears as an ACL Findings 2026 paper, suggesting peer-reviewed uptake of the common-ground-representation problem specifically. — [ACL Anthology Findings 2026.findings-acl.1645](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1645.pdf)
- Older work specific to voice assistants: some autonomous spoken dialogue assistants incorporate grounding mechanisms to detect system errors and link them to explicit negotiation strategies *before* adding information to common ground — i.e., treating common-ground updates as gated/confirmed events rather than automatic additions. — [search synthesis, grounding mechanisms in voice assistants query]

### Inferences
- The "CGT vs. DST" distinction is directly actionable for the Boli project's architecture: standard dialogue-state tracking (slot-filling, intent state) is a different and narrower object than common-ground tracking (which propositions are *mutually* accepted, with what epistemic confidence, by both parties) — the Boli hypothesis (conversation should be modeled as maintaining shared commitments, not stateless turns) maps directly onto building a CGT-style module as explicit state, separate from whatever DST/slot-tracking a voice pipeline already has.
- The repeatedly-cited problems of context-window limits and "representational blur" are strong, literature-backed arguments (not just reasonable inference) for *why* an LLM's implicit context window is an inadequate substitute for explicit common-ground state in a long-running or resource-constrained (voice, lower context budget, streaming) system — this directly supports the Boli project's premise without requiring novel justification.
- Voice specifically adds at least two documented burdens beyond text-agent common-ground tracking: (a) paralinguistic/prosodic signal must itself be grounded (not just propositional content), per the Trace-style multimodal tracking work and the "voice-specific context remains implicit" finding, and (b) turn-taking/latency constraints mean grounding negotiation (asking for confirmation) carries a different cost profile in real-time speech than in asynchronous text — the literature surfaced here does not quantify this cost tradeoff for voice specifically, which is a genuine open question for the Boli project to address empirically rather than one the literature already answers.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper proposing a complete, specifically *voice-AI* (telephony/real-time speech) common-ground state architecture end-to-end (most CGT/situated-dialogue work found is text+vision/embodied, e.g., collaborative task settings with gesture and shared visual workspace, not audio-only voice assistants). This is a plausible genuine gap in the literature relevant to the Boli project, but I cannot rule out that such work exists and simply didn't surface in these queries — worth a dedicated follow-up search pass specifically on telephony/IVR/voice-assistant dialogue-state-tracking architectures if not already covered by the parallel "dialogue state tracking" research thread in this project.
- I did not find quantitative data on the latency/interruption cost of mid-conversation common-ground verification (e.g., "let me confirm I understood...") specifically in spoken vs. text interfaces — this would need targeted HCI/spoken-dialogue-systems literature not surfaced in this pass.
