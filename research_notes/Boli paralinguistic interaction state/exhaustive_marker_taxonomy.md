## Prosody: F0/pitch, intensity/loudness, energy dynamics, intonation contour

### Takeaway
Pitch (F0) and intensity/loudness are the most consistently studied and most reliably construct-associated acoustic markers in the literature — robustly linked to arousal and to anger/stress specifically — but both are strongly confounded by speaker sex/physiology and require speaker-relative (not population-absolute) interpretation to be trustworthy; "energy dynamics" and "intonation contour" are real constructs in the literature but are less standardized as discrete named features (they are typically operationalized as F0/intensity *variability*, *range*, or *slope* features, e.g., in eGeMAPS) rather than independently validated markers.

### Cited Findings
- eGeMAPS/GeMAPS (Eyben et al., 2015/2016, *IEEE Trans. Affective Computing*) formally defines F0 (via a robust pitch tracker) and a pseudo-auditory loudness measure as the two "core" prosodic parameters, selected specifically for their established link to affective physiological changes in voice production, prior empirical track record, automatic extractability, and theoretical grounding — not just empirical popularity. — [GeMAPS paper (TUM mirror)](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1523509/file.pdf); [SAIL/USC listing](https://sail.usc.edu/publications/html/b2hd-Eyben2015TheGenevaminimalisticacoustic.html)
- GeMAPS (62 params) and eGeMAPS (88 params) both include F0-derived statistics (mean, and in eGeMAPS additional percentile/range/slope-style descriptors) and loudness/energy statistics as part of the "frequency-related" and "energy/amplitude-related" parameter groups. — [GeMAPS search synthesis](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Geneva-Minimalistic-Acoustic-Parameter-Set-for-Eyben-Scherer/10f992779c2601af8a33f53c813cb6342791873f) (snippet-only)
- The 2025 PLOS ONE systematic review (Schewski, Magimai Doss, Beldi, Keller) found anger is "associated with elevated fundamental frequency (F0), increased speech volume, and faster speech rate," and stress is "associated with increased F0 and intensity, and reduced speech duration" — i.e., both F0 and intensity show direction-consistent effects for anger and stress specifically, out of 39 studies reviewed. — [PLOS ONE 10.1371/journal.pone.0328833](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0328833); [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)
- The same review found cognitive load "linked to increased F0 and intensity, although the results for F0 were overall less clear than those for negative emotions and stress" — i.e., F0's reliability as a cognitive-load marker is explicitly rated lower than its reliability as an anger/stress marker within the same review. — [PLOS ONE / PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)
- The review states "prosodic features were the most investigated and showed the greatest accuracy in detecting negative emotions, stress, and cognitive load" among all acoustic feature classes examined (prosodic, spectral, voice-quality). — [PLOS ONE / PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)
- Z-score speaker normalization of F0 (F0_norm = (f − μ_speaker)/σ_speaker) is an established technique specifically because raw F0 confounds speaker/gender identity with any affective signal; it is used to map each speaker's F0 distribution onto a common "prosodic space" before cross-speaker comparison or modeling. — [speaker-normalization search synthesis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.10168) (snippet-only, representative of a broader normalization literature)

### Inferences
- "Energy dynamics" (as named in the assignment) most plausibly corresponds to the eGeMAPS loudness/energy *slope, range, and rate-of-change* descriptors rather than a separately validated construct; no standalone "energy dynamics" literature distinct from F0/intensity dynamics was found in this pass, so this is treated as a sub-component of the loudness/intensity family rather than an independent marker.
- "Intonation contour" likewise is not surfaced in these searches as a single discretely-validated feature; it is operationalized in practice via F0 slope/range/rising-falling classification within pitch-tracking pipelines (consistent with the hesitation-prosody literature from the prior research pass on Pon-Barry/Wottawa/Kirkland, where rising terminal intonation was linked to uncertainty).
- Because F0 and intensity are the *best-evidenced* features for anger/stress specifically (per the 2025 review) but *weaker* for cognitive load, a passive-observability system should weight F0/intensity-based "arousal/anger-like" inferences higher in confidence than F0/intensity-based "cognitive-load" inferences, and should always apply within-speaker baselining before any cross-speaker comparison, given the gender/physiology confound noted separately below.

### Gaps
- Could not get full-text access to the GeMAPS/eGeMAPS paper (TUM/semanticscholar snippets only) to extract the complete, exact list of all 25 (GeMAPS) / 88 (eGeMAPS) parameter names with their precise formulas; this limits the granularity of the prosody-to-feature mapping to what search snippets surfaced (see the Spectral/Cepstral section below for the fuller descriptor-group breakdown that was retrievable).
- No source found that separately validates "energy dynamics" or "intonation contour" as named, independently-studied constructs distinct from F0/intensity statistics; flagged as a possible terminology mismatch between the assignment's framing and how the literature actually names these features.

## Timing: speech rate, articulation rate, syllable rate, pauses, silence ratio, response/turn latency

### Takeaway
Pause-related and turn-timing measures are mechanistically well-characterized (precise definitions, sub-200ms universal turn-taking gap statistics), but speech-rate/articulation-rate as *affect or stress markers specifically* are the least reliable timing construct in the literature — multiple studies report null or contradictory effects for stress/anxiety — making rate-based inferences the weakest-evidenced timing marker for interaction-state purposes even though the signal itself is easy and robust to measure.

### Cited Findings
- Articulation rate is formally defined as "the pace at which speech segments are produced without accounting for pauses," while speech rate includes pause intervals — i.e., these are not interchangeable and conflating them is a common methodological error in the literature. — [search synthesis, Frontiers/PMC sources](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01994/full)
- The 2025 PLOS ONE systematic review reports that "results for speech rate and speech duration were heterogeneous — some studies reporting a decrease in speech duration and an increase in speech rate, while others found no changes" under stress, and that "some studies observed an increase in speech rate under stress while three other studies found no effect." — [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11939969/) / [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0328833)
- For anxiety specifically: one study found patients with panic disorder spoke significantly *slower* during autobiographical talking than during reading, while another study found articulation rate showed no significant correlation with generalized anxiety — a direct within-literature contradiction on direction of effect. — [PMC article on anxiety/speech parameters](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11939969/)
- Cross-linguistic turn-taking research finds the modal gap between conversational turns is ~200ms (about one syllable duration), with median gaps across ten languages between 0–300ms; this is treated as a near-universal timing constant of human conversation, not an affect marker per se. — [Timing in Conversation](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.268); [PNAS universals paper](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0903616106)
- The cognitive mechanism behind this timing is that language production take ~600–1500ms depending on priming, meaning speakers must begin planning their response turn *before* the incoming turn ends, using prosodic cues (e.g., final lengthening) near the end of the incoming turn as a "go" signal. — [Timing in Conversation](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.268)
- ComParE/Interspeech 2014's Cognitive Load Sub-Challenge used reading-span and Stroop-task-induced cognitive load (low/medium/high) as ground truth and treated speech acoustics (including timing features) as the classification signal, establishing cognitive load as a recognized "beyond basic emotion" paralinguistic construct with a dedicated shared-task benchmark. — [INTERSPEECH 2014 ComParE paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2014/schuller14_interspeech.pdf)
- Hesitation-pause duration is described (consistent with the prior Pon-Barry/Wottawa/Kirkland research pass) as a strong, well-replicated cue to perceived hesitation/uncertainty, with "final lengthening" (pre-pause slowing) combining with raw pause duration as the valid joint cue rather than either acting alone. — [AISV disfluency paper](https://www.aisv.it/StudiAISV/2017/vol_3/019_Lickley.pdf) (snippet-only)

### Inferences
- Pause *duration and placement* (hesitation pauses, especially combined with pre-pause lengthening) appear substantially better-evidenced as a marker of uncertainty/hesitation than raw *speech rate* is as a marker of stress/anxiety — these are timing sub-constructs with meaningfully different reliability and should not be lumped into a single "timing" confidence tier in the report.
- "Response latency" (turn-taking gap) is a well-characterized universal baseline (~200ms modal), which means a passive-observability system has a strong population prior to detect *deviation* (unusually long gaps as possible hesitation/processing difficulty, unusually short/negative gaps as possible interruption) — this is a case where population-level priors plus within-call deviation detection is more defensible than population-absolute "stress thresholds" on rate features.
- "Turn latency" vs. "response latency" vs. "silence ratio" are not separately disambiguated as distinct validated constructs in what the searches surfaced; they appear to be operational variants of the same underlying pause/gap-timing family (turn-level silence, inter-turn gap, and proportion-of-call silence respectively) rather than independently-validated markers with their own literatures.

### Gaps
- No direct source found specifically validating "syllable rate" as distinct from speech/articulation rate, or "silence ratio" (proportion of a call that is silence) as an independently studied construct with its own reliability literature; both are treated as derived/aggregate measures of the rate and pause constructs above rather than separately verified.
- Could not retrieve the PLOS ONE full text to get the exact per-construct study counts for speech-rate/pause findings (e.g., exactly how many of the 39 studies addressed rate vs. pause vs. latency); the "heterogeneous" / "three studies found no effect" language is as granular as the snippets allowed.

## Voice quality: jitter, shimmer, HNR, spectral tilt, breathiness, creakiness/roughness

### Takeaway
Jitter, shimmer, and HNR are the textbook clinical/affective voice-quality triad but have well-documented reliability problems even before any affect question is asked — they are first-order confounded by vocal intensity (loudness), fundamental frequency, vowel identity, and speaker sex/age, and they degrade in validity on aperiodic (e.g., highly stressed, disordered, or creaky) voices precisely where researchers most want to use them; spectral tilt plus HNR/CPP, by contrast, are specifically well-validated (with quantified variance-explained numbers) for breathy vs. creaky voice-quality classification, making them more trustworthy than jitter/shimmer for the "voice quality as marker" use case.

### Cited Findings
- Jitter and shimmer are evaluated "under the assumption that the analyzed signal is nearly periodic," and "the validity and reliability of measures deteriorate during the assessment of highly aperiodic voice signals" — a direct, citable statement that these measures are *least* reliable exactly when a voice is most perturbed (e.g., under high emotional/physiological load), which is a structural limitation for using them as stress/arousal markers. — [search synthesis of jitter/shimmer reliability literature](https://repository.stcloudstate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?params=/context/stcloud_ling/article/1155/&path_info=1_Koffi2025ComprehensiveReviewOfJitterShimmerHNR.pdf)
- "Voice sound pressure level has a dramatic confounding effect on jitter and particularly shimmer"; additionally "shimmer increased with age for males, but neither jitter nor shimmer increased with age for females, whereas noise measures were similar for both ages" — i.e., age/sex interact with these measures in non-uniform (not simply additive) ways. — [ScienceDirect: Reliable Jitter and Shimmer Measurements in Voice Clinics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199709001106)
- "Core measures such as jitter, shimmer, and harmonics-to-noise ratio vary significantly across gender, vowel type, and pitch conditions in healthy speakers" — confirming vowel identity itself (not just speaker trait) is a confound, meaning phoneme content of an utterance can shift these features independent of speaker state. — [search synthesis](https://www.scielo.br/j/rboto/a/cszRNmqnmdYfJyM6kV3LJtM/?format=pdf&lang=en)
- The 2025 PLOS ONE review notes "perturbation measures like jitter are a reliable indicator of stressor-provoked anxiety" in at least some of the reviewed literature — this is a narrower, more specific claim (stressor-provoked anxiety specifically) than a general "jitter indexes stress" claim, and sits in tension with the broader review's finding of no consistent acoustic pattern for fear/anxiety overall (see dedicated section below) — flagged as an internal tension in the source material rather than resolved. — [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11939969/)
- Spectral tilt is defined as "the steepness of harmonic amplitude decline with frequency" — low tilt (strong high-frequency harmonics) sounds brighter; high tilt (weak high-frequency harmonics) sounds breathier/weaker. — [UCSD phonetics-of-voice handbook](https://idiom.ucsd.edu/~mgarellek/files/Garellek_Phonetics_of_Voice_Handbook_final.pdf)
- For breathy voice specifically, commonly used parameters are CPP (cepstral peak prominence), HNR, and spectral-tilt-family measures (H1−H2, H1*−H2*, H1−A1, H1*−A1*); breathy voice shows *high* spectral tilt and *low* CPP/HNR. In continuous speech, "both signal periodicity and spectral tilt provided accurate predictions of breathiness ratings, accounting for 70%–85% of the variance" — a specific, quantified, well-evidenced result (rare in this domain). — [ICPhS 2023 paper](https://www.internationalphoneticassociation.org/icphs-proceedings/ICPhS2023/full_papers/772.pdf)
- For creaky voice, the most salient acoustic correlates are "higher mean H2*, lower mean f0, and HNR below 500 Hz," with a profile of low F0, shorter glottal pulses, and shallow (not steep) spectral tilt — the opposite tilt direction from breathy voice, which is a useful discriminating fact. — [UCLA/ICPhS creaky voice paper](https://idiom.ucsd.edu/~mgarellek/files/Keating_etal_2015_ICPhS.pdf)

### Inferences
- Within "voice quality," there is a meaningful reliability split: breathy/creaky *phonation-type* classification via spectral-tilt+HNR/CPP is comparatively well-validated (quantified variance-explained), whereas jitter/shimmer as *generic affect/stress indices* are confound-laden and degrade on the very signals (aperiodic, highly perturbed voice) they are meant to detect. A report should not treat "voice quality" as a single reliability tier — phonation-type description is moderately-to-well supported; jitter/shimmer-as-emotion-proxy is only weakly/inconsistently supported.
- Because jitter/shimmer are so strongly confounded by loudness, F0, and vowel content, any passive-observability use of these features for interaction-state inference should be read only as *relative, within-speaker, same-vowel-context* change, never as a population-absolute "jitter > X means stressed" threshold.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from available snippets exactly how large the jitter/shimmer gender/age effect sizes are in quantitative terms (beyond directional statements); full text of the cited clinical-reliability review was not retrievable due to the egress block.
- The apparent tension between "jitter is a reliable stressor-provoked-anxiety indicator" (one source) and "no consistent acoustic pattern for fear/anxiety" (the 2025 systematic review's own global conclusion) could not be reconciled with available snippets — this may reflect a distinction between "stress" and "fear/anxiety" as separate constructs in the review's own taxonomy (stress showed more consistency than fear/anxiety per the review), but the exact sourcing of the jitter claim relative to the review's own inclusion criteria is unclear and is flagged as unresolved rather than asserted.

## Spectral/cepstral features and the eGeMAPS/openSMILE descriptor mapping

### Takeaway
MFCCs, formants, and spectral-balance measures are the standard spectral/cepstral toolkit and are well-established as *generically useful* features for speech-emotion-recognition model performance, but the evidence for what each one specifically indexes *physiologically/perceptually* is much stronger for formants and spectral tilt (clear articulatory/phonatory interpretation) than for MFCCs (which are a general-purpose auditory-perception-inspired spectral envelope representation with strong empirical performance but a weaker specific-construct story — they work well but are less individually interpretable than GeMAPS-style parameters).

### Cited Findings
- eGeMAPS (88 low-level descriptors) is explicitly organized into named groups: frequency-related (F0, jitter, formant frequencies Fn and their bandwidths), energy/amplitude-related (shimmer, loudness, HNR), spectral (Alpha Ratio, Hammarberg Index, spectral slope, Fn relative energy, harmonic difference H1–H2 and H1–A3), and temporal features. This is the most directly retrievable mapping of "which eGeMAPS descriptor measures which physical quantity" available in this pass. — [GeMAPS synthesis](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Geneva-Minimalistic-Acoustic-Parameter-Set-for-Eyben-Scherer/10f992779c2601af8a33f53c813cb6342791873f) (snippet-only, cross-referenced across two independent search queries with consistent content)
- Formants are defined as "the peak frequencies in the speech spectrum that have higher energy and characterize the resonance phenomenon of the vocal tract," explicitly tied to articulatory/vocal-tract configuration rather than purely phonatory (voice-source) state — i.e., formants partly reflect what is being said (vowel identity/articulation) as much as or more than how it is said. — [MDPI speech emotion recognition review](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/4/839)
- Spectral centroid is "the power spectrum's center of mass," perceptually linked to a sound's "brightness," and has been shown to be a significant component specifically in *music* emotion perception; its evidence base for *speech* paralinguistic/affect inference specifically (vs. music) is thinner in what these searches surfaced. — [search synthesis, music/speech emotion feature papers](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/4/839)
- MFCCs are described as having "emerged as a benchmark standard due to their ability to approximate human auditory perception, effectively encoding spectral envelope information critical for emotion discrimination," with reported classification accuracies (e.g., 93–94% on EMODB/RAVDESS in one deep-learning study combining MFCC with other features) — strong *empirical model-performance* evidence, but this is evidence of discriminative utility within a trained classifier, not evidence of a specific, interpretable physiological/affective mechanism the way jitter or spectral tilt have. — [MDPI speech emotion recognition review](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/4/839)

### Inferences
- There is a real evidentiary asymmetry worth flagging explicitly to the report-writer: GeMAPS/eGeMAPS-style features (F0, loudness, jitter, shimmer, HNR, spectral slope/tilt, Alpha Ratio, Hammarberg Index) were *selected* for inclusion specifically because of established "theoretical significance" and prior affective-physiology linkage (per the GeMAPS paper's own stated selection criteria), whereas MFCCs are a general-purpose representation whose link to specific affective constructs is largely *inferred backward* from classifier performance rather than from a theory-driven physiological mechanism. For a passive-observability system that wants explainable, defensible markers (not just a black-box classifier), eGeMAPS-style parameters are the more epistemically honest building blocks than raw MFCCs.
- Formants and spectral centroid are more entangled with linguistic/phonetic content (what is said) than with paralinguistic state (how it's said) compared to F0/loudness/voice-quality features — this means formant- and centroid-based markers are more likely to be confounded by text/phoneme content and should be weighted lower as "pure" interaction-state markers unless normalized for phonetic content.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full enumerated 88-parameter eGeMAPS table (exact descriptor names, LLD vs. functional statistic distinctions, window sizes) due to the egress block on audeering.github.io and the TUM/arXiv mirrors; the descriptor-group-level mapping above is as granular as available snippets allowed. A report-writer needing the exact 88-item table should flag this as requiring either a cached/offline copy of the Eyben et al. 2015/2016 paper or a retry through a different access path.
- No direct source found specifically validating "spectral flux" (the suggested sub-feature) as a paralinguistic marker in speech (as opposed to music/audio-event detection); treated as an open gap.
- Spectral centroid's evidence base is strongest in *music* emotion, not speech paralinguistics specifically — the degree to which centroid findings generalize from music to conversational speech paralinguistics is unverified and should not be assumed.

## Disfluency and conversational behavior as acoustic (not textual) events

### Takeaway
Filled pauses, prolongations, and pre-pause lengthening have a reasonably mature acoustic-correlates literature (duration and F0/intonation cues are well-documented), but acoustic *interruption/overlap detection* is a comparatively newer, more engineering-driven literature (mostly 2020s turn-taking/dialogue-systems papers) with less consensus on a clean acoustic signature distinct from simple simultaneous-voice-activity detection — overlap/interruption is more reliably defined structurally (via turn-taking theory: Transition Relevance Places, TCUs) than acoustically.

### Cited Findings
- Hesitation disfluencies can be realized as silent pauses, prolongations, filled pauses, and repetitions, or combinations thereof; filled pauses ("um"/"uh" in English) are described as "the most recognizable sign of vocalized pausing." — [AISV disfluency paper](https://www.aisv.it/StudiAISV/2017/vol_3/019_Lickley.pdf)
- "Pause duration is a strong cue to perceived hesitation together with final lengthening... the total increase achieved by the combined effects of retardation and pause is the valid cue rather than the contribution by either factor alone" — i.e., the acoustic signature of hesitation is a *joint* duration+lengthening cue, not either alone; this is a specific, mechanistic, citable claim. — [AISV disfluency paper](https://www.aisv.it/StudiAISV/2017/vol_3/019_Lickley.pdf)
- "Along with rising intonation and increased pause duration, the insertion of filled pauses and prolongations have been linked to expressing the attitude of uncertainty in speech" — directly connects three acoustic-event types (filled pause, prolongation, rising intonation) to the uncertainty construct, consistent with and reinforcing the prior Pon-Barry/Wottawa/Kirkland research pass. — [ResearchGate: Cues for hesitation in speech synthesis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221484233_Cues_for_hesitation_in_speech_synthesis)
- A "true interruption" is formally defined in recent turn-taking literature as an event where a speaker begins a new turn with communicative intent that semantically warrants the other speaker yielding, specifically when there is no Transition Relevance Place (TRP) imminent and the second speaker starts speaking anyway — this is a structural/pragmatic definition, not a purely acoustic one. — [arXiv: Semantic-Aware Interruption Detection](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24144v1)
- "Overlapping speech towards the end of a turn is common during speaker floor exchanges and is not considered an interruption" — meaning raw simultaneous-voice-activity detection (a purely acoustic signal) is explicitly *not* sufficient to distinguish interruption from normal turn-exchange overlap; semantic/structural context is needed in addition to the acoustic overlap signal. — [arXiv: turn-taking/interruption detection papers](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14940)
- Acoustic-only models (e.g., multi-task LSTM networks over acoustic frame sequences) have been used to predict turn-transitions/turn-switches, and benefit from multi-task learning frameworks combining acoustic and syntactic cues — indicating acoustic cues alone are useful but are typically combined with other signals (syntax, semantics) in the current state of the art rather than used standalone. — [search synthesis of turn-taking prediction literature](https://emp.engin.umich.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/67/2022/06/2018_Aldeneh_ICASSP.pdf)

### Inferences
- Filled-pause/prolongation acoustic cues should be ranked as *moderately-to-well supported* for the uncertainty/hesitation construct specifically (multiple converging sources, mechanistically explained, consistent with the prior hesitation-prosody research pass).
- Acoustic interruption/overlap detection, by contrast, should be ranked *lower* in "pure acoustic reliability" — the literature itself states that raw overlapping-speech detection is explicitly insufficient and must be combined with structural/pragmatic information (TRP location, semantic intent) to correctly classify an overlap as an "interruption" versus benign floor-exchange overlap. A passive-observability system that flags "overlap detected" from audio alone is flagging a real acoustic event, but should not label it "interruption" (a loaded interactional-state inference) without the additional non-acoustic structural context the literature says is required.
- "Restart frequency" (the assignment's third named construct in this category) was not independently surfaced in these searches as a separately-studied acoustic event distinct from general disfluency/repetition literature; it is most likely treated in the literature as a sub-type of repair/repetition disfluency rather than its own acoustic construct.

### Gaps
- No source specifically validating "restart frequency" as an acoustically (not just textually) defined, separately measured construct was found in this pass; flagged as unresolved — a report-writer should treat restart/repair-rate markers as inferred from general disfluency literature rather than independently validated at the acoustic level.
- Could not find a source giving a quantified acoustic accuracy/reliability figure (e.g., precision/recall) for acoustic-only interruption detection as distinct from the semantic/structural approaches; the available snippets describe the field's definitions and approaches but not consensus performance numbers.

## Environment/channel confounds (what changes a feature for reasons unrelated to speaker state)

### Takeaway
Every signal category above has a documented channel/environment confound: SNR and codec/compression degrade or bias acoustic features (and measurably worsen downstream recognition accuracy under train/test SNR or codec mismatch), reverberation specifically smears temporal envelope structure and flattens formant transitions (directly corrupting voice-quality and formant-based measures), and clipping's effects can be partially masked by background noise — meaning a defensible passive-observability system needs an explicit channel-quality gate (estimated SNR/clipping/codec detection) before trusting any acoustic marker, not just a post-hoc caveat.

### Cited Findings
- "Worse results are obtained when a model is tested with patterns at different SNRs than training, and the effect of codification also makes results worse" — direct evidence that SNR mismatch and codec/compression ("codification") each independently degrade speech-emotion-recognition reliability, and that these are separate, compounding confounds. — [ACM: Noise and Codification Effect on Emotional Speech Classification Systems](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3498851.3499022)
- "ASR performance is strongly dependent on the type and bit-rate of the CODEC used to transmit or store the audio signal," and "training with mixed types of codification does not show any improvement" — establishing codec type/bitrate as a structural confound that cannot simply be averaged away by training on mixed codecs. — [search synthesis, ASR/codec training-data literature](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11482241)
- "The effects of speech signal clipping can at times be hidden by the presence of background noise, depending on the model chosen for the comfort noise synthesis" — meaning clipping and background-noise artifacts can mask or interact with each other, complicating any attempt to cleanly separate confound types. — [search synthesis, clipping/comfort-noise literature]
- Reverberation causes "temporal smearing, filling dips and gaps in the temporal envelope, increasing the prominence of low-frequency energy, and flattening the formant transitions," with a smearing effect where a single reverberant segment distorts features for several subsequent frames depending on reverberation time (RT) — a specific, mechanistic description directly relevant to formant- and envelope-based markers (e.g., articulation-rate measures that rely on amplitude envelope, and formant-transition-based measures). — [search synthesis, reverberation/ASR robustness literature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11384524/)
- Room reverberation "can severely degrade the performance of automatic speech recognition," and automatic speaker-identification performance "has been shown to severely degrade under reverberant mismatched conditions" — confirming reverberation is a confound not just for paralinguistic/emotion features but for the identity-tracking layer (speaker ID / diarization) that a longitudinal, speaker-relative system would also depend on.
- Regional accent/dialect is a documented confound specifically for *prosodic encoding of confidence/doubt*: "speakers systematically varied mean fundamental frequency to indicate confident and doubt regardless of accent, but out-group [differently-accented] speakers varied more on intensity height and variation in confidence encoding" — i.e., the F0 cue for confidence/doubt appears to generalize across accent groups in this study, but the intensity cue does not, meaning accent is a confound for *some* but not *all* sub-features within a single construct. — [ISCA SpeechProsody 2018: Predicting confidence and doubt in accented speakers](https://www.isca-archive.org/speechprosody_2018/jiang18_speechprosody.pdf)
- "A stronger impression of confidence was shown towards native speakers, demonstrating an in-group bias" in listener perception of accented speech — this is a *perceptual/labeling* confound (relevant if any ground-truth labels in training data were produced by human raters) distinct from an *acoustic production* confound. — [ISCA SpeechProsody 2018 paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/speechprosody_2018/jiang18_speechprosody.pdf)

### Inferences
- The channel/environment confound list should be treated as a *gating* problem, not a *correction* problem, for a passive-observability layer: SNR, codec/bitrate, clipping, and reverberation all directly alter the same low-level descriptors (F0 tracking robustness, HNR, spectral tilt, formant transitions, envelope-based rate/pause detection) that the construct-level markers are built from, so the most defensible design is to estimate channel quality first and suppress or down-weight marker confidence under poor-channel conditions, rather than attempting to "correct for" channel effects post hoc on construct outputs.
- Dialect/accent is a more subtle confound than channel noise because it does not uniformly bias all sub-features of a construct (per the F0-generalizes/intensity-does-not finding above) — this argues against a blanket "normalize for dialect" rule and for feature-by-feature confound auditing instead.
- Since reverberation also degrades speaker-ID/diarization robustness, the longitudinal/speaker-relative baselining strategy (next section) is itself channel-dependent: a system cannot reliably build a "speaker's own baseline" from audio collected under inconsistent or poor-reverberant-conditions without first verifying consistent-enough channel quality, or the baseline itself will be contaminated by channel variation rather than purely speaker-state variation.

### Gaps
- No single source was found that enumerates a complete, unified "confound checklist" (SNR + clipping + bandwidth + codec + reverberation + noise type, all in one place) with quantified effect sizes; the above is a synthesis across several separate, narrower sources rather than one comprehensive confound study. A report-writer should present this as "well-documented in aggregate across separate narrow literatures" rather than citing one review.
- "Bandwidth" (e.g., narrowband 8kHz telephony vs. wideband) specifically was not separately surfaced with its own dedicated source in this pass beyond the general codec/CODEC findings; it is treated as closely related to/overlapping with the codec confound rather than independently evidenced here.
- No quantified source on "noise type" (e.g., stationary HVAC hum vs. babble/crosstalk vs. transient noise) as a differentiated confound (i.e., whether different noise *types*, not just noise *levels*, bias features differently) was retrieved; flagged as a gap.

## Longitudinal/speaker-relative framing (within-speaker baselining)

### Takeaway
There is a real, if comparatively thin and mostly clinical/health-monitoring (not general-affect) literature on within-speaker longitudinal acoustic monitoring, built around the core logic of using each person as their own control (holding age, sex, anatomy, and habitual voice quality fixed "by construction"); the dominant *implementation* methodology that surfaced is z-score normalization against a speaker's own historical distribution, with changepoint-detection algorithms specifically invoked for identifying when a speaker's voice characteristics shift over time — but this pass did not find general-purpose, affect/interaction-state-specific (as opposed to clinical/disease-tracking) literature using this approach, which is a notable gap relative to the assignment's framing.

### Cited Findings
- Within-patient longitudinal speech monitoring research is explicitly framed around "each patient serv[ing] as their own control, holding age, sex, anatomy, and habitual voice quality fixed by construction" — i.e., the methodological rationale for within-speaker comparison is stated directly: it is a way of controlling for exactly the demographic/physiological confounds documented in the voice-quality and prosody sections above. — [search synthesis, COPD/MS speech-monitoring literature](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21599)
- Z-score normalization of F0 (and by extension other features) against a speaker's own mean/variance is an established technique: "F0 = (f − μ_i)/σ_i, with f being the unnormalized F0 and μ, σ being the mean and variance of the speaker's F0 values," explicitly used "to deal with gender and speaker variation in pitch." — [speaker-normalization literature synthesis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.10168)
- "Changepoint detection algorithms" are referenced as a method to "assess when vocal characteristics shift over time" in longitudinal clinical speech-monitoring research (e.g., Multiple Sclerosis, COPD screening applications). — [search synthesis, longitudinal clinical speech-monitoring literature](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S221103482400467X)
- Recent clinical research has "examined how speech changes longitudinally and day-to-day within individuals through automated acoustic analysis, with applications in conditions like Multiple Sclerosis and COPD screening" — confirming the within-speaker-longitudinal paradigm is an active research area, but the surfaced applications are disease-progression monitoring, not affect/interaction-state tracking. — [search synthesis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S221103482400467X)
- Adaptive score-normalization techniques from the (distinct) speaker-verification literature use "only part of the cohort... to compute mean and variance for normalization, with the selected cohort potentially changing for every speaker" — a related but distinct (verification-oriented, not affect-oriented) precedent for adaptive, per-speaker-relative normalization schemes that could plausibly be adapted.

### Inferences
- The within-speaker/z-score-against-own-history approach is methodologically well-precedented *in general* (clinical voice monitoring, speaker verification normalization) but its *specific application to affect/interaction-state tracking* (as opposed to disease progression or verification) appears to be an extrapolation this pass could not directly source — this is an important distinction for the report-writer to preserve: the *method* (within-speaker z-scoring, changepoint detection) is well-evidenced; its *specific use for emotional/interaction-state observability* is an inference by analogy, not a directly-cited finding.
- This within-speaker approach directly addresses several confounds flagged elsewhere in this document (gender/age effects on F0/jitter/shimmer, dialect/accent effects on prosody) by design, which is a strong methodological argument for Boli's "speaker's own baseline" framing even though direct affect-specific precedent is thin.

### Gaps
- No source was found describing within-speaker baselining applied specifically to emotion/arousal/uncertainty tracking in conversational speech (as opposed to disease/health monitoring or speaker verification); this is the single largest gap in this research pass relative to the assignment's explicit "is there literature on using a speaker's OWN baseline... rather than population-level absolute thresholds" question. The honest answer is: the *method* is well-established in adjacent fields (clinical monitoring, verification normalization), but a direct general-affect/interaction-state within-speaker-baseline paper was not located via WebSearch in this pass. This should be reported as a gap, not papered over.
- No specific changepoint-detection *algorithm names* (e.g., CUSUM, Bayesian online changepoint detection, PELT) were surfaced as applied to speech-specific longitudinal monitoring in the retrieved snippets; only the general concept was confirmed.

## MSP-Podcast annotation scheme (categorical + continuous VAD)

### Takeaway
Yes — MSP-Podcast provides both categorical emotion labels AND continuous valence/arousal/dominance (VAD) dimensional ratings on the same speaking turns, collected via large-scale crowdsourcing (Amazon Mechanical Turk) with at least five raters per turn and an explicit annotator-quality-control process (weekly performance tracking, feedback, and retraining below a quality threshold).

### Cited Findings
- MSP-Podcast is annotated with "primary (single dominant emotion) and secondary (multiple emotions perceived in the audio) emotional categories, as well as emotional attributes for valence, arousal, and dominance" — directly confirming both categorical and continuous-dimensional annotation exist on the same corpus. — [ResearchGate: The MSP-Podcast Corpus](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403305016_The_MSP-Podcast_Corpus); [lab-msp.com MSP-Podcast page](https://lab-msp.com/MSP/MSP-Podcast.html)
- "Each speaking turn was annotated on a scale from one to seven by at least five raters for arousal (1 = very calm, 7 = very active), valence (1 = very negative, 7 = very positive) and dominance (1 = very weak, 7 = very strong)" — this is the exact rating-scale methodology for the continuous VAD dimensions, collected via Amazon Mechanical Turk crowdsourcing. — [search synthesis of MSP-Podcast annotation methodology](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403305016_The_MSP-Podcast_Corpus)
- Primary categorical emotions curated for balanced representation include Anger, Sadness, Happiness, Surprise, Fear, Disgust, Contempt, and Neutral. — [lab-msp.com / search synthesis](https://lab-msp.com/MSP/MSP-Podcast.html)
- Quality control: "tracking the performance of annotators on a weekly basis, providing detailed feedback, and implementing a training strategy to improve their annotations if their quality falls below a given threshold" — a specific, citable crowdsourcing-QA methodology. — [search synthesis, MSP-Podcast corpus papers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403305016_The_MSP-Podcast_Corpus)
- Corpus scale: version 2.0 (final release) comprises 409 hours of annotated data from more than 3,641 speakers and 264,705 speaking turns; earlier versions ran from v1.0 (Nov 2017) through v1.12 (June 2024). — [lab-msp.com Busso 2025/202x papers](https://lab-msp.com/MSP/publications/Busso_202x.pdf); [emergentmind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2509.09791)

### Inferences
- The combination of categorical + continuous labels on the *same* turns (not separate sub-corpora) means MSP-Podcast supports both classification-style and regression/dimensional-style modeling approaches from one annotation effort — relevant if Boli's report wants to argue for a dimensional (VAD) framing over discrete emotion categories, since the continuous dimensions are not a separate, less-validated add-on but a core part of the same large-scale, quality-controlled annotation pipeline.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the exact current total number of annotators, inter-annotator agreement statistics (e.g., Cronbach's alpha) for the *categorical* labels specifically (as opposed to MSP-Conversation's continuous-trace alphas, which were retrievable — see next section) in this pass; this is a specific numeric gap.

## MSP-Conversation: time-continuous traces across multi-turn conversations

### Takeaway
Yes — MSP-Conversation provides genuinely time-continuous (not per-utterance) arousal, valence, and dominance *traces* across real conversations, annotated at ~16ms frame resolution (60fps) by at least 5-6 annotators, and this is explicitly positioned as methodologically distinct from MSP-Podcast's per-turn discrete ratings; the stated rationale is that continuous traces let researchers re-segment and analyze emotional displays at arbitrary temporal resolutions (phone/syllable/word/phrase/sentence) and study emotion dynamics *within* a conversation rather than only classifying isolated, already-segmented utterances.

### Cited Findings
- "MSP-Conversation corpus contains interactions annotated with time-continuous emotional traces for arousal (calm to active), valence (negative to positive), and dominance (weak to strong)." — [ISCA Interspeech 2020: The MSP-Conversation Corpus (Martinez-Lucas et al.)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2020/martinezlucas20_interspeech.pdf)
- "Time-continuous annotations offer the flexibility to explore emotional displays at different temporal resolutions while leveraging contextual information. The traces can be arbitrarily segmented according to the scope of the analysis, providing the resources to study emotions at various temporal resolutions (e.g., phone, syllable, word, phrase, sentence)." — this is the explicit, citable methodological rationale for why continuous traces matter relative to fixed per-utterance labels. — [arXiv: MSP-Conversation paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22536)
- Scale/versioning: the corpus originally contained "more than 15 hours of speech annotated by at least five annotators"; a more recent version (v1.1) includes 224 conversations totaling 59.6 hours, with continuous annotations for arousal and valence performed by at least 6 annotators at approximately 16ms frame-rate (60fps). — [search synthesis, MSP-Conversation papers](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22536)
- Inter-annotator reliability for the continuous traces: average Cronbach's alphas of α=0.50 (arousal), α=0.54 (valence), and α=0.41 (dominance) — these are moderate-to-low agreement levels by conventional reliability standards (commonly, α above ~0.7 is considered "acceptable" in psychometrics), which is an important and specific caveat: even MSP-Conversation's own continuous annotations show only moderate cross-annotator consistency, especially for dominance. — [search synthesis, MSP-Conversation corpus papers](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22536)

### Inferences
- The explicit methodological difference from MSP-Podcast is: MSP-Podcast labels *discrete, pre-segmented speaking turns* (one VAD/categorical rating per turn, treating each turn as an independent classification instance), whereas MSP-Conversation labels a *continuous trace over real, flowing multi-turn conversations* (ratings at a fixed high frame-rate that can be re-segmented post hoc at any granularity). This is directly relevant to Boli's framing: a system that wants to track *change over the course of a call* (not just classify isolated utterances) needs the MSP-Conversation-style continuous-trace paradigm as its validation reference, not the MSP-Podcast per-turn paradigm, because only the former has ground truth for *within-conversation dynamics* rather than just between-utterance classification.
- The moderate Cronbach's alpha values (0.41–0.54) for the continuous traces are an important caution: even the best available time-continuous VAD ground truth has real human-rater disagreement, especially for dominance — a passive-observability system should not expect any acoustic model trained or validated against such traces to exceed this ceiling of human agreement, and should treat "dominance" as the least reliable of the three VAD dimensions based on this specific evidence.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the exact definition/boundaries of what counts as a single "conversation" in MSP-Conversation (e.g., are these natural call-center-like dyadic conversations, podcast-style, or something else) beyond "naturalistic" framing in the paper title; full text was not accessible due to the egress block.
- No direct comparison source found quantifying how much *better* (in e.g. downstream task performance or correlation with ground truth) a continuous-trace-trained model does on a tracking-change-over-a-call task versus a per-utterance-classification model — the *rationale* for time-continuous traces mattering is well-sourced (quoted above), but a quantified empirical demonstration of the benefit was not found in this pass.

## The 2025 systematic review (PLOS ONE / PMC12289014): verified findings on anger, stress, cognitive load vs. fear/anxiety

### Takeaway
Confirmed and verified via multiple independent, convergent search queries: the review (Schewski, Magimai Doss, Beldi, Keller; PLOS ONE, published 24 July 2025, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0328833, PMC12289014) screened 38 articles / 39 studies across four databases (PubMed, PsycInfo, Web of Science, Scopus) and found reasonably consistent, direction-specific acoustic patterns for anger and stress (and a partially consistent but noisier pattern for cognitive load), while explicitly concluding that **no consistent acoustic pattern was identified for fear or anxiety** — this is the review's own stated conclusion, not an inference, and it was corroborated identically across two separately-phrased search queries.

### Cited Findings
- Review identity and scope: "Measuring negative emotions and stress through acoustic correlates in speech: A systematic review," by Lilien Schewski, Mathew Magimai Doss, Guido Beldi, and Sandra Keller, published in PLOS One, 24 July 2025; systematic search across PubMed, PsycInfo, Web of Science, and Scopus; 38 articles / 39 studies included. — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0328833); [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/); [PubMed 40705747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40705747/)
- Anger: "associated with elevated fundamental frequency (F0), increased speech volume, and faster speech rate" — a three-feature, direction-specific, consistent profile. — [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)
- Stress: "associated with increased F0 and intensity, and reduced speech duration" — again a specific, direction-consistent multi-feature profile, distinct from anger's profile (stress shows reduced duration rather than faster rate, though the two are related). — [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)
- Cognitive load: "linked to increased F0 and intensity, although the results for F0 were overall less clear than those for negative emotions and stress" — the review explicitly and directly grades cognitive load's F0 evidence as *less clear* than anger/stress's, i.e., a three-tier gradient (anger/stress clearest, cognitive load partially clear) rather than a flat "consistent vs. inconsistent" binary. — [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)
- Fear and anxiety: "No consistent acoustic patterns were identified for fear or anxiety." This exact conclusion was returned by two independently-phrased WebSearch queries ("PMC12289014 systematic review acoustic correlates emotion" and "systematic review acoustic correlates fear anxiety inconsistent speech 2025"), increasing confidence this is the review's actual stated finding rather than a search-snippet artifact. — [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/); [PubMed 40705747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40705747/)
- Overall review-level conclusion (paraphrased from snippets, both queries converging on this framing): "Prosodic features were the most investigated and showed the greatest accuracy in detecting negative emotions, stress, and cognitive load" among the feature classes studied (prosodic, spectral, voice-quality), and more broadly, "research remains inconsistent in identifying reliable acoustic markers, providing limited guidance for researchers and practitioners in the field" — i.e., even for anger/stress (the "best" constructs), the review's overarching framing is one of a field still short of full consensus, just with anger/stress/cognitive-load being *relatively* more consistent than fear/anxiety, not absolutely settled. — [PMC12289014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289014/)

### Inferences
- This review gives Boli's "passive observability, not emotion-reactive agent" framing direct, citable support: it is scientifically defensible to flag anger-like and stress-like acoustic *patterns* (elevated F0, increased intensity/volume, speech-rate/duration changes) with moderate confidence, but it would be scientifically indefensible to build any fear/anxiety-specific acoustic classifier or to label an observation as "the user sounds afraid/anxious" based on acoustic features alone, per this review's own explicit negative finding. Any Boli feature that currently infers or labels "anxiety" or "fear" from acoustic signal alone is not supported by this review and should be flagged as such in the main report.
- The anger/stress vs. cognitive-load vs. fear/anxiety three-tier gradient (clear > partially clear > absent) found here is a cleaner, more usable reliability ranking for the main report than a flat "well-established/inconsistent" binary, and should be reproduced in Boli's construct-reliability table essentially as-is, since it comes from the most directly relevant single source in this entire research pass.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the review's full-text discussion of *why* fear/anxiety specifically lacks consistency (e.g., is it elicitation-method heterogeneity, read-speech vs. spontaneous-speech differences, state-anxiety vs. trait-anxiety conflation, or something else) due to the egress block on journals.plos.org and pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov; the review almost certainly discusses methodological heterogeneity as a candidate explanation (this is a very common conclusion in such reviews), but this pass could not confirm the specific attributed reasons with a citable snippet, so this should be marked as a gap rather than asserted.
- Could not retrieve a specific list of which of the 39 studies measured which features, or any quantified meta-analytic effect sizes (vs. qualitative "elevated"/"increased" directional language) — the review may or may not report quantitative pooled effect sizes; the snippets available only surfaced qualitative/directional conclusions.
