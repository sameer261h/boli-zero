## Voice Activity Detection (VAD): what it computes, and connections to hesitation/speech-rate/interaction difficulty

### Takeaway
VAD is a narrow, well-defined subsystem (binary speech/non-speech per frame) that sits upstream of ASR, endpointing, diarization, and overlap detection. The general speech-science literature does connect VAD-adjacent features (pause count/length, speech rate, hesitation ratio) to disfluency and cognitive/clinical state, and at least one escalation-detection paper explicitly uses VAD output as an input feature for detecting conversational escalation — but this is "VAD as a feature-extraction step inside a built-for-purpose classifier," not "VAD output alone as an incidental signal." On the Gnani side, there is no documented evidence (in anything reachable in this pass) that Prisma exposes VAD-adjacent output via its REST or streaming APIs beyond the `audio_duration_ms`/timing fields already identified in the prior research pass; Gnani's own technology marketing describes a "denoise-first streaming pipeline," which implies VAD-like preprocessing exists internally, but this is not documented as a user-facing feature.

### Cited Findings
- VAD is defined as "the task of deciding, for each short slice of audio, whether it contains speech or not," and is distinct from endpointing: "VAD makes a per-frame acoustic call (speech or not), while endpointing decides the speaker is done using VAD's output plus timing; a long pause is 'not speech' to VAD and 'not finished yet' to a good endpointer." — [Soniox: What is voice activity detection (VAD)](https://soniox.com/wiki/voice-activity-detection)
- General speech-feature literature (clinical/dementia-detection context) lists VAD-derived/pause-adjacent features explicitly tied to hesitation: "speech rate, pause rate, number of pauses, total length of pauses, average length of pauses, hesitation ratio, and rhythm (rate of syllable production during speech)," and notes that "filled pauses (sounds like 'er', 'um' etc.) also indicate hesitations and can take up a significant amount of speech time." — [PMC: Pause Extraction for Alzheimer's Detection from Spontaneous Speech](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10046767/); [ScienceDirect: Voice Activity Detection overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/computer-science/voice-activity-detection)
- A dedicated escalation-detection paper ("Detecting Escalation Level from Speech with Transfer Learning and Acoustic-Lexical Information Fusion") reports: "A system based on acoustic-lexical features to detect escalation from speech uses Voice Activity Detection (VAD) and label smoothing to enhance performance," achieving "81.5% unweighted average recall (UAR), significantly outperforming the baseline (72.2% UAR)." — [arXiv:2104.06004 via CatalyzeX](https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/arxiv:2104.06004)
- On the endpointing/UX side (not Gnani-specific, but describing the general architecture relevant to any production VAD): "Every endpointing system navigates a fundamental tradeoff... Setting the silence threshold too short creates the frustrating experience of being interrupted mid-sentence... while setting it too long makes every interaction feel like a lagged phone call," and "barge-in errors can have a significant impact to the perceived usability of the system and might cause it to be abandoned." — [Decagon: What is Endpointing in Voice AI?](https://decagon.ai/glossary/what-is-endpointing); [Hamming.ai: Voice Agent Interruption Handling](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-interruption-handling-runbook)
- Gnani's own technology marketing page states the platform uses a "denoise-first streaming pipeline" with "chunk-wise decoding" as part of "ASR + SLM + LLM + TTS + Agentic AI Orchestration," and that models "adapt in real time to various accents, dialects, and speaking speeds." This implies internal preprocessing (consistent with VAD/denoising existing in the pipeline) but is marketing copy, not a technical spec, and makes no mention of VAD being exposed to API consumers. — [Gnani: How Our Technology Works](https://www.gnani.ai/technology)
- A WebSearch for "Gnani Prisma VAD voice activity detection documentation" surfaced only a generic glossary-style page title, "VAD (Voice Activity Detection): AI Glossary – Definitions & Concepts Explained," hosted at gnani.ai but not reachable for content verification (egress blocked) — [gnani.ai/glossary/vad-voice-activity-detection](https://www.gnani.ai/glossary/vad-voice-activity-detection) (title only, content unverified)

### Inferences
- Given that Gnani markets a "denoise-first streaming pipeline," some VAD-like speech/non-speech segmentation almost certainly exists inside Prisma's processing chain (this is standard architecture for any streaming ASR system, not a Gnani-specific claim) — but this is architectural plausibility, not documentation, and there is no evidence it is exposed via the REST or WebSocket API fields identified in the prior research pass.
- The streaming endpoint's documented `audio_duration_ms`, `segment_id`, and `segment_index` fields (established in the prior pass) are the most plausible "VAD-adjacent" exposed markers: segment boundaries in a streaming ASR system are typically determined by VAD/endpointing logic, so segment duration and inter-segment gaps could in principle be used by a downstream consumer to approximate pause structure — but this is inference about what the fields likely represent, not something Gnani documents as a hesitation or interaction-state signal.
- The escalation-detection paper (arXiv:2104.06004) is the clearest literature example of VAD being used as one ingredient in a purpose-built escalation/conflict classifier, which answers the "is there literature connecting VAD to interaction difficulty" question affirmatively — but note it uses VAD as a feature-engineering step inside a trained classifier, not as a freestanding signal read off an API response the way Prisma's `audio_duration_ms` field could be.

### Gaps
- No documentation (Gnani's own or third-party) was found describing whether Prisma's internal pipeline literally uses a VAD module, what its parameters are, or whether any VAD-level output (e.g., per-segment speech/silence boundaries) is available at any API tier (including enterprise/research tiers) beyond what was already found in the prior pass.
- The gnani.ai VAD glossary page's actual content could not be verified due to egress blocking; its title suggests only an educational/definitional glossary entry, not a product feature description, but this could not be confirmed.
- No direct literature was found using "VAD segment duration" or "VAD boundary timing" by itself (absent a trained classifier) as a hesitation proxy — the dementia/disfluency literature uses dedicated pause-extraction algorithms, which overlap conceptually with VAD but are typically presented as a distinct, more fine-grained processing step than a generic streaming-ASR VAD module.

## Diarization: does Gnani offer it, what metadata might it expose, and literature on turn-taking/overlap and interaction state

### Takeaway
Gnani does not appear (in anything reachable this pass) to document a standalone diarization feature on Prisma's STT endpoints specifically; general diarization/speaker-separation capability is implied for Gnani's broader "speech analytics" and multi-speaker-identification product lines, but the search results describing diarization mechanics in detail came from third-party industry glossaries, not from Gnani directly — this is an important correction to flag, since the search tool's initial synthesis incorrectly implied the content was "from Gnani's glossary." Separately, the turn-taking/overlap literature robustly connects speaker overlap to conflict and escalation, which is the single strongest "unexpected signal" finding in this whole audit.

### Cited Findings
- **Source-attribution correction:** A WebSearch for "gnani.ai glossary speaker diarization" returned a synthesized answer describing diarization as "who spoke when," critical for "quality monitoring, compliance review, and agent coaching" — but the actual underlying links were to third-party glossaries (Hamming.ai, FutureAGI, 101voice, MeetStream, DIYAI, AudioToTextAI, AnveVoice), not to any gnani.ai page. No gnani.ai-branded diarization documentation was found in this pass. — [Hamming.ai: Speaker Diarization glossary](https://hamming.ai/glossary/speaker-diarization); [FutureAGI: What Is Speaker Diarization?](https://futureagi.com/glossary/speaker-diarization/); [MeetStream: What is speaker diarization?](https://meetstream.ai/glossary/speaker-diarization)
- One of those third-party sources notes an important general caveat relevant to interpreting any diarization-derived metadata: "Diarization accuracy is measured separately from WER. A system can have excellent WER but poor diarization, correctly transcribing words while misattributing them to the wrong speaker." — [Hamming.ai: Speaker Diarization glossary](https://hamming.ai/glossary/speaker-diarization)
- Gnani does document (via its own marketing blog) a broader "speech analytics" product that includes multi-party/turn-level signal: "Gnani's analytics provides 100% call coverage - transcribed, scored, and surfaced for compliance, performance, and coaching," evaluating "tone, script adherence, and customer reactions"; one search summary (not independently verified against the source page directly) also mentioned "silence/overtalk rate" and "conversation duration" as part of general speech-analytics metrics, though this specific phrase's direct attribution to a Gnani page vs. the broader industry context could not be fully disambiguated in the snippet. — [Gnani: Omnichannel Speech Analytics blog](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/omnichannel-speech-analytics-a-competitive-edge-for-call-center/)
- Gnani also markets "Multi-speaker Identification" as differentiating/labeling "individual speakers in multi-participant conversations, useful for meetings, call centers, and transcription services" — i.e., diarization-like capability exists somewhere in Gnani's product suite, associated with voice biometrics rather than confirmed as a Prisma STT feature specifically. — [Gnani: Voice Biometrics in AI Agents](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/voice-biometrics-in-ai-agents)
- On overlap and conflict: a 2013 Interspeech paper, "Let Me Finish: Automatic Conflict Detection Using Speaker Overlap," is squarely on-topic (title alone, full text unreachable due to egress block to isca-archive.org) and search-summary synthesis states "overlapping speech is strongly correlated with high conflict levels." — [ISCA Archive: Grezes et al. 2013 (title/abstract only, blocked)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2013/grezes13_interspeech.pdf)
- Turn-taking literature distinguishes overlap types by speaker intent: "if the user intends to take the turn from the agent, this behavior is defined as an interruption; if the user has no intention of taking the turn, this behavior is considered backchannel or a listener response" — meaning raw overlap-detection output (without intent modeling) is an ambiguous signal that could reflect either agitation/interruption or benign backchanneling. — [arXiv:2507.07518, Triadic Multi-party Voice Activity Projection](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07518) (title/summary only; arxiv.org fetch blocked)
- Overlapped Speech Detection (OSD) is described in the technical literature as a standard preprocessing step that sits alongside VAD ahead of diarization: "Voice Activity Detection (VAD) and Overlapped Speech Detection (OSD) are key pre-processing tasks for speaker diarization," and "without an OSD system, the performance of a speaker diarization system degrades proportionally to the existing overlaps in a dataset." — [arXiv:2505.23207, Towards Robust Overlapping Speech Detection](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23207) (title/summary only; arxiv.org fetch blocked)
- Call-center-specific conflict literature: a "microethnographic analysis of call center data focused on prosodic features of interaction suggests that where conflict occurs, it is mirrored in the prosodic features of the interaction," based on research combining "conversation analytic approaches with quantitative methods" on UK call-center recordings. — [per WebSearch synthesis; underlying primary source not independently identified/verified by URL in this pass]

### Inferences
- The clearest, best-evidenced "adjacent subsystem → interaction-state signal" pathway in this entire audit is overlap/turn-taking detection → conflict/escalation. This is a purpose-built research area (not an accidental repurposing), but it is architecturally close to what any diarization pipeline (including a hypothetical Prisma diarization feature) would need to compute as a byproduct of separating speakers (turn boundaries, overlap regions, speaker-count). If Gnani ever exposed turn-level diarization metadata (turn count, overlap duration, interruption counts) via an API, that would be a directly literature-supported basis for interaction-state inference — but no evidence was found that Prisma's REST/WS endpoints expose any such thing today.
- The ambiguity between interruption and backchannel in raw overlap signal is an important caveat: a naive "overlap rate" metric, if it were ever exposed, would need intent-aware modeling (as in the Interspeech 2013 and VAP literature) to avoid false-positive "conflict" readings on ordinary backchannel-heavy conversations.

### Gaps
- No Gnani-branded documentation of a diarization feature attached to the Prisma STT product specifically (REST v3 or streaming v3) was found; Gnani's diarization-adjacent capability appears to live in separate "speech analytics" and "voice biometrics / multi-speaker identification" product lines, and whether/how those products relate architecturally to the Prisma ASR engine (shared backend vs. separate stack) is undocumented and could not be determined in this pass.
- The ISCA Archive paper on conflict detection via overlap, and two arXiv papers on OSD/VAP, could not be fetched directly (egress blocked), so findings rely on search-engine snippet synthesis rather than verified abstracts — treat the specific recall/precision numbers and detailed claims from these papers as unverified until a source with working egress can confirm them.

## Speaker/channel modeling: internal speaker-embedding/channel features in production ASR, Gnani evidence, and usefulness for longitudinal speaker-relative comparison

### Takeaway
This is the strongest documented finding in the whole audit: Gnani has a separate, clearly-documented product (Armour365, voice biometrics) that explicitly builds persistent per-speaker voiceprints/embeddings from "300+ proprietary audio features," stores them in an encrypted database, and reuses them across calls for authentication — which is structurally exactly the kind of "speaker-relative longitudinal" infrastructure the research question asks about. There is no evidence this is integrated with or exposed via the Prisma STT API, but it demonstrates Gnani has the underlying technical capability and existing speaker-embedding infrastructure in-house. Separately, the general literature does support personalized/speaker-embedding-conditioned baselines improving stress-detection accuracy, directly validating the "speaker-relative comparison" concept, though not tied to Gnani specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Gnani's Armour365 voice biometrics product: "works on 300 plus proprietary audio features," and during enrollment "the voice biometric solution extracts unique vocal characteristics and creates a digital voiceprint that gets securely stored in an encrypted database;" minimum enrollment audio is "7 seconds." — [IoT For All: Gnani.ai Launches armour365](https://www.iotforall.com/news/gnani.ai-launches-armour365--voice-biometrics-software-based-on-patented-technologies)
- Armour365 supports "both active and passive voice verification in less than 2 seconds with an Equal Error Rate (EER) of sub 2%," and includes "anti-spoof layer," "replay attack detection," and analysis of "environmental acoustic signatures that indicate whether audio is being captured directly from a human speaker or played through a device" (i.e., channel/device characteristics are modeled as part of the biometric pipeline). — [Gnani: Voice Biometrics - How Armour Detects Spoofs and Deepfakes](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/voice-biometrics-how-armour-detects-spoofs-and-deepfakes)
- Gnani markets voice biometrics as reducing authentication friction: "customer authentication times have been reduced from an average of 2-3 minutes to less than 15 seconds" when integrated with agentic AI systems. — [Gnani: Voice Biometrics in AI Agents](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/voice-biometrics-in-ai-agents)
- A GitHub repo documents a dedicated Armour365 voice-biometric API as a distinct product/SDK from the ASR API-service repo, confirming it is a separately engineered component. — [GitHub: gnani-ai/voicebiometric-api-armour365](https://github.com/gnani-ai/voicebiometric-api-armour365)
- On the general literature side, a dedicated paper, "Speaker Embeddings as Individuality Proxy for Voice Stress Detection," reports (per search synthesis; direct fetch blocked) that researchers "concatenated pre-trained audio representations with well-established speaker embeddings such as Resemblyzer and ECAPA on cross-dataset voice stress data, concluding that personalized audio representation significantly improves voice stress detection," and that "a personalized speech profile for each individual end-user... serves as an integral baseline for subsequent comparative stress analysis." — [arXiv:2306.05915 (title/summary only; fetch blocked, also mirrored at isca-archive, also blocked)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05915)
- General note on diarization-adjacent speaker-embedding stability relevant to any system attempting longitudinal matching: "For long-form conversations like call center calls, speaker embedding models settle into accurate, stable labels well before the conversation ends" — i.e., within-call embedding stability is an established property, which is a necessary (though not sufficient) precondition for cross-call/longitudinal speaker-embedding comparison to be technically viable. — [per WebSearch synthesis; specific source URL not independently confirmed]
- Gnani's ASR legacy API-service GitHub README (directly fetched and verified in this pass) documents only request-side parameters (token, language code, audio format/encoding) and error codes; it contains **no documentation of response fields** such as confidence, VAD, diarization, timestamps, or signal quality — reinforcing the prior pass's finding that Gnani's documented APIs are consistently thin on response metadata across both the legacy gRPC/REST service and the newer v3 REST/streaming endpoints. — [GitHub: gnani-ai/API-service](https://github.com/gnani-ai/API-service) (directly fetched)

### Inferences
- It is architecturally very likely (standard practice, not Gnani-specific) that production ASR systems compute internal speaker/channel-adaptation features (e.g., i-vectors, x-vectors, or channel/codec profiling for 8kHz telephony normalization) as part of acoustic-model adaptation, since Gnani explicitly markets "telephony-grade audio (8kHz, PSTN)" handling and "accent-aware training" — both of which typically require some per-call or per-speaker acoustic conditioning internally. This is plausible/typical-architecture speculation, not something Gnani documents explicitly for the ASR (Prisma) engine.
- Because Gnani already operates a persistent, encrypted per-speaker voiceprint store (Armour365) for an unrelated purpose (fraud/authentication), the organizational and technical precedent for "speaker-relative longitudinal comparison" infrastructure clearly exists within the company — meaning that if Gnani (or a user of Gnani's various products) wanted to build a speaker-relative "this call vs. this same speaker's past calls" comparison, the voiceprint infrastructure to do so already exists in Armour365, even though it is not integrated with or exposed by Prisma's STT API today. This is an inference bridging two separate, unconnected pieces of documented fact, not a documented integration.
- The stress-detection literature (arXiv:2306.05915) directly validates the research question's premise: speaker-embedding-conditioned/personalized baselines do measurably improve detection of individual-level deviations (stress) compared to speaker-agnostic models — supporting the general idea that "comparing a speaker's current state to their own documented baseline" is a legitimate enhancement over population-level baselines, independent of whether any Gnani product exposes this today.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found of any point at which Armour365's speaker-embedding/voiceprint infrastructure is connected to or shared with Prisma's ASR engine; these appear to be positioned as separate product lines (authentication/fraud vs. transcription) and nothing found in this pass confirms or denies a shared backend.
- The core stress-detection paper (arXiv:2306.05915) could not be fetched directly due to egress blocking of arxiv.org and isca-archive.org mirrors; its specific quantitative results (effect sizes, dataset details) are therefore unverified beyond search-snippet paraphrase.

## Noise/signal-quality estimation: internal SNR/clipping estimation in ASR, Gnani documentation, and literature on repurposing it

### Takeaway
SNR/signal-quality estimation is a well-established internal ASR technique used to predict/explain word-error-rate and to gate whether a recording is usable, and Gnani explicitly markets a "denoise-first" pipeline with "AI-powered noise suppression," confirming internal noise processing exists — but there is no documented "signal quality score" or "audio quality" field exposed via any Gnani API found in this pass (consistent with the prior pass's finding that the REST v3 response has no such field). Separately, no literature was found that repurposes an SNR/signal-quality metric as an incidental signal of a speaker's behavioral/interaction state (e.g., effort, distress) — this is the clearest "gap" finding in the whole audit; signal-quality estimation in the literature is used entirely for ASR-engineering purposes (WER prediction, reliability gating), not behavioral inference.

### Cited Findings
- SNR estimation is explicitly used to predict ASR reliability: "SNR gives a number value for the ratio of background noise to speech... If the SNR is higher than the selected threshold value, the audio is regarded as high quality and the ASR output is likely to be reliable. If the SNR is lower than the threshold, then the output ASR transcript... might not be reliable." — [SoapboxLabs: Audio Quality Feature docs](https://docs.soapboxlabs.com/guides-&-tutorials/audio-quality-feature/)
- A refinement relevant to VAD/noise-estimation overlap: "Estimating signal power over VAD-identified speech regions and noise power over non-speech regions yields an SNR measure that better reflects recognition difficulty" — i.e., SNR estimation in practice is typically computed jointly with VAD output, reinforcing that these "adjacent subsystems" are architecturally intertwined, not independent modules. — [per WebSearch synthesis of MITRE/related audio-quality literature; original MITRE paper: "Using Audio Quality to Predict Word Error Rate in an Automatic..."](https://www.mitre.org/sites/default/files/pdf/06_1154.pdf)
- A re-recording-based quality-assessment methodology exists in the ASR evaluation literature: representative audio files are "re-recorded using a quality microphone in a quiet environment and the resulting files transcribed by the ASR system" to isolate the effect of recording quality from content — confirming that "request a re-recording" workflows are a documented engineering practice in principle, though this describes an evaluation methodology, not necessarily a live production feature. — [per WebSearch synthesis; original source not independently confirmed by distinct URL]
- Gnani's technology marketing confirms internal noise handling: "Gnani's denoise-first streaming pipeline uses chunk-wise decoding," and "Gnani.ai's AI-powered noise suppression enhances ASR and call quality — even in the noisiest settings," explicitly targeting "telephony-grade audio (8kHz, PSTN)" and "noisy field environments." — [Gnani: How Our Technology Works](https://www.gnani.ai/technology)
- No search in this pass (covering both "Gnani signal quality score" style queries and direct inspection of the legacy API-service GitHub README) surfaced any documented "audio quality," "SNR," or "signal quality" field in any Gnani API response, consistent with the prior research pass's finding for the v3 REST endpoint.

### Inferences
- Gnani's pipeline almost certainly computes some internal noise/SNR estimate as part of its denoising step (this is standard architecture for any "denoise-first" pipeline — a denoiser typically needs at least an implicit noise-level estimate to decide how aggressively to filter), but this is architectural inference, not documentation; nothing suggests this internal estimate is logged, scored, or exposed to API consumers at any tier found in this pass.
- The near-total absence of literature on "repurposing SNR/signal-quality for behavioral inference" (as opposed to "SNR for WER prediction") is itself informative: unlike VAD (which has a thin but real connecting literature to escalation detection, see above) and diarization/overlap (which has a substantial connecting literature to conflict), noise/signal-quality estimation appears to be the most purely "engineering-only" of the four adjacent subsystems, with no identified secondary research literature treating it as a behavioral or interaction-state proxy. One could speculate (pure speculation, not supported by any source found) that very poor signal quality might indirectly correlate with caller environment stress (e.g., calling from a chaotic or distressing location), but no study was found testing or supporting this.

### Gaps
- No Gnani documentation of a user-facing "signal quality" / "audio quality" score was found on any endpoint (legacy gRPC/REST, v3 REST, or v3 streaming); this is a confirmed absence within the limits of what is publicly documented and reachable from this environment, not a confirmed absence from Gnani's internal engineering.
- No literature was found connecting signal-quality/SNR estimation to behavioral/interaction-state inference (speaker effort, distress, environment stress) in any vendor's system, not just Gnani's. This should be reported as a clear, modest "no" finding per the task's own framing: this is the one adjacent subsystem among the four where the "unexpected incidental signal" hypothesis finds essentially no support in the literature surveyed.
- The MITRE WER-prediction paper and the re-recording evaluation methodology could not be independently verified beyond one directly-linked PDF (MITRE) and one unverified secondary description; treat the re-recording methodology claim as lower-confidence.

## Broadly: cases where a VAD/diarization/channel/noise-estimation metric was repurposed as an incidental interaction-state signal (any vendor)

### Takeaway
Across all four adjacent subsystems, exactly one clear, literature-documented case of "engineering-purpose metric repurposed as behavioral/interaction-state signal" was found: VAD-informed and overlap-informed features being used inside purpose-built escalation/conflict classifiers (not Gnani-specific; general speech-processing research). Diarization/turn-taking has the most developed connecting literature (conflict ↔ overlap). Speaker-embedding/channel modeling has a direct literature analog (speaker-embedding-conditioned stress baselines) but this is closer to "designed for the purpose" than "incidentally repurposed." Noise/signal-quality estimation has essentially no connecting literature to behavioral inference — this is a genuine, reportable gap, not a failure of the search.

### Cited Findings
- VAD as a feature-engineering input to a purpose-built escalation classifier: "A system based on acoustic-lexical features to detect escalation from speech uses Voice Activity Detection (VAD) and label smoothing to enhance performance" (81.5% UAR vs. 72.2% baseline). — [arXiv:2104.06004 via CatalyzeX summary](https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/arxiv:2104.06004)
- Overlap/turn-taking as a signal of conflict: "overlapping speech is strongly correlated with high conflict levels" (per search synthesis of the 2013 Interspeech "Let Me Finish" paper), and separately, call-center-specific conversation-analytic work finding conflict "mirrored in the prosodic features of the interaction." — [ISCA: Grezes et al. 2013 (title only, blocked)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2013/grezes13_interspeech.pdf)
- Speaker-embedding-conditioned personalization improving stress detection: "personalized audio representation significantly improves voice stress detection" (per search synthesis of arXiv:2306.05915) — notable as the closest literature analog to "speaker-relative longitudinal comparison," though this work designs the personalization deliberately rather than discovering it as an accidental byproduct of an unrelated system (e.g., a voice-biometrics authentication engine).
- No literature was found treating raw ASR confidence instability, VAD segment timing, or SNR estimates, by themselves and without a purpose-built classifier layered on top, as a validated interaction-state signal — consistent with the prior research pass's finding that "ASR behavior as uncertainty signal" is a thin, underdeveloped research area, and extending that same thinness to the VAD-timing and SNR cases specifically.

### Inferences
- The overall pattern across all four subsystems is: the closer a subsystem is to modeling *people interacting with each other* (diarization/turn-taking/overlap), the richer the connecting literature to interaction-state inference (conflict, escalation); the closer a subsystem is to modeling *the physical recording channel* (noise/SNR/signal quality), the thinner that literature becomes, with essentially nothing found. VAD and speaker-embedding/channel modeling sit in between: both have some connecting literature, but typically as an input feature inside a purpose-built classifier rather than as a freestanding incidental signal.
- None of the four subsystems have cases where Gnani specifically is documented as having repurposed an engineering signal for behavioral inference; all affirmative literature findings here are generic (any vendor, mostly academic/Interspeech-style work), and all Gnani-specific findings are either (a) separate, undocumented-as-connected product lines (Armour365 vs. Prisma) or (b) architectural inference from marketing copy, not confirmed API features.

### Gaps
- This section's findings depend heavily on search-engine snippet synthesis of papers behind blocked domains (arxiv.org, isca-archive.org); a researcher with working egress to those domains could meaningfully strengthen or weaken these findings by reading the full papers, particularly on the quantitative strength of the overlap↔conflict and VAD↔escalation relationships.
- No vendor-neutral industry-wide survey was found that systematically catalogs "secondary uses of ASR-adjacent engineering signals" as a named research area (unlike, say, speech emotion recognition, which is an established field with surveys); this appears to be a genuinely under-surveyed cross-cutting topic rather than one where this search simply failed to find an existing survey — though that absence-of-absence claim cannot be proven and should be treated as the search's own limit, not a definitive statement that no such survey exists.
