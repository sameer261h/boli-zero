You will help identify which of 8 related Indian language varieties a speaker is using. Below, for each variety, are the 5 grammatical markers that best told it apart from the other 7 in an earlier analysis of speech-recogniser transcripts. Each marker has a code. "More common here" lists forms at least twice as frequent in that variety as in the others; "rarer here" lists forms at most half as frequent. Some markers are clues by absence: the variety uses that kind of form less than the others do.

Hindi
  HIN-1 Copulas (forms of 'to be' that link a subject to a description (Hindi है, हैं)). Used more here than in the others. More common here: हूं, हैं.
  HIN-2 Participles / converbs (verb forms used like adjectives or to join actions (Hindi बना हुआ, करके)). Used more here than in the others. More common here: हुई, हुआ, हुए.
  HIN-3 Non-copular auxiliaries (helper verbs after a main verb that mark ongoing or continuing action (Hindi रहा, रही, रहे)). Used more here than in the others. More common here: रही, रहा.
  HIN-4 Locative / deictic adverbs (words for where something is: here, there, in front, behind, inside, outside). Used more here than in the others. More common here: चारों, सामने, यहां, पीछे, लगे, अंदर, बाहर.
  HIN-5 Gender agreement (verb and adjective endings that change with the gender of the subject (Hindi बैठा vs बैठी)). Used more here than in the others. More common here: लिखा, बनी, रही, बैठा, रहा, रखा, बना.
